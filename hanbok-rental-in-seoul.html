Hanbok Rental in Seoul: How It Works and Where to Go

By Dragon Kim · Updated October 7, 2026 · 7 min read

Quick answer

Renting a hanbok in Seoul costs about 15,000 to 30,000 won for roughly two to four hours, and wearing one gets you free entry to four royal palaces and Jongmyo Shrine. Most visitors rent near Gyeongbokgung Station, choose a top-and-bottom outfit, and return it the same day.

What should I know before I rent a hanbok?

Prices, hours and rules checked October 7, 2026

Decide on your palace first, because opening hours, closing days and rental return times all depend on it. Hanbok rental is a short, same-day service: you choose an outfit, wear it for a few hours and return it. Gyeongbokgung Palace, the most popular choice, charges adults 3,000 won normally and closes on Tuesdays, according to VisitKorea.

  • Worth knowing: Gyeongbokgung is open about 09:00 to 18:00 from March to May and September to October, with last admission at 17:00. Hours change by season, so check before you go.
  • A common mistake: Picking a one-piece hanbok-style dress. It may be refused for free admission (see the rules below).
  • Local tip: Ask for a locker or bag storage when you rent, and confirm what time you must return the outfit.

Does wearing hanbok really get me into palaces for free?

Yes: visitors wearing hanbok get free admission to Seoul's main royal palaces, including Gyeongbokgung, Changdeokgung, Changgyeonggung and Deoksugung. Visit Seoul lists five palaces including Gyeonghuigung and says the waiver applies even on holidays and special events. Korean press coverage of the heritage authority's criteria adds Jongmyo Shrine and several royal tombs, and says the policy began in 2013 (Asiae).

The saving is modest for one palace at 3,000 won, but it adds up if you visit several. Many visitors plan a single hanbok day that links two palaces and the neighborhood between them. For ideas on where to base yourself, see our Seoul neighborhoods guide.

  • Free: adults who wear a qualifying hanbok.
  • Already free: visitors under 19 and over 65 enter Gyeongbokgung free anyway, per VisitKorea.
  • Not covered: separate paid programs such as special night openings or guided tours may have their own tickets, so read the palace notice.

What counts as hanbok at the palace gate?

An outfit must have a hanbok top and a hanbok bottom, such as a jeogori with a skirt or with pants; modern and traditional styles both qualify. This is the core criterion described by officials in the Asiae report on the heritage authority's guidelines.

  • Accepted: a jeogori with an overlapping collar plus a skirt or pants, in traditional or modern designs. Gender restrictions were lifted in July 2019, so men can wear skirts and women can wear pants.
  • Not accepted: one-piece hanbok-style dresses, a jeogori worn with jeans, a hanbok skirt with an ordinary T-shirt, or outer robes worn alone.
  • Fusion styles: The Korea Times reported in September 2025 that roughly 80% of renters choose fusion hanbok and that officials had no plans to regulate heavily modified styles. Rules can change, so ask the shop to confirm the outfit qualifies.

Where do I rent a hanbok in Seoul, and what does it cost?

Expect about 15,000 to 30,000 won for a two- to four-hour rental, with the densest cluster of shops near Gyeongbokgung Station. The Korea Times reported more than 30 shops near exits 2 and 3 with multilingual staff, and Visit Seoul also names Myeong-dong, Dongdaemun and Gangnam as rental areas.

Visit Seoul reports that hair accessories and a purse are usually included in the base price, while an optional fur vest costs a few thousand won extra (about 3,000 to 5,000 won). Extras such as hair styling vary by shop, so ask before you pay. Standard rentals usually run about four hours, so read the rental terms before you pay.

How does a hanbok rental work step by step?

Pick a shop, choose an outfit, change, visit the palace and return the hanbok before closing time.

  1. Choose a time. Arrive in the morning if you can; palaces close in the late afternoon and shops need time to process returns.
  2. Pick an outfit. Select a top and bottom, and ask whether the set qualifies for free palace entry.
  3. Change and get styled. Staff help with the ties and accessories. Store your own clothes and bag in the shop.
  4. Visit the palace. Show your outfit at the ticket gate. Staff may check that it meets the rules. If an outfit is refused, ask the shop to swap it.
  5. Return on time. Take everything back by the agreed time and ask the shop about its damage or stain policy before you leave.

How do I wear hanbok respectfully and comfortably?

Wear comfortable shoes, watch hems on stairs and wet ground, and treat palace grounds as heritage sites rather than photo sets. Hanbok skirts are long and volumes vary, so give yourself room when walking. Our Korean etiquette guide covers wider manners.

  • Weather: Autumn mornings can be cool, so ask about a coat or shawl that fits over the outfit.
  • Crowds: Almost 1.72 million hanbok-wearing visitors entered Gyeongbokgung in a year, according to the Korea Times, so expect queues at popular photo spots on weekends.
  • Short on time: VisitKorea lists short hanbok experiences, such as a 20-minute program at the Seoul Insa-dong PR Center for about 3,000 won, though these do not qualify for palace entry.

FAQ

Is hanbok rental in Seoul worth it?

Yes if you plan to visit a palace: a hanbok is a costume you wear for photos, and palace admission is free for hanbok wearers, which offsets part of the rental fee.

Can I wear hanbok to a palace without renting?

Yes. Any outfit with a proper top and bottom that meets the palace rules is accepted, including a hanbok you own or buy, but a rental is the easiest route for a short trip.

Do I need to book a hanbok rental in advance?

Walk-in rentals are common near Gyeongbokgung Station, but weekends and autumn afternoons are busy, so booking a time slot online can save waiting. Confirm the shop's return time when you book.

Can men wear skirt hanbok and women wear pants hanbok?

Yes. Gender restrictions on free palace admission were lifted in July 2019, so any combination of a jeogori top and skirt or pants qualifies.

Which palaces are free if I wear hanbok?

Gyeongbokgung, Changdeokgung, Changgyeonggung and Deoksugung, plus Jongmyo Shrine. Visit Seoul also lists Gyeonghuigung, so check the palace's own notice before you go.
