Bukchon Hanok Village: Visiting Hours, Rules and Route

By Dragon Kim · Updated October 15, 2026 · 7 min read

Quick answer

Visit Bukchon Hanok Village between 10:00 and 17:00 every day. The core residential lanes are a "red zone": sightseeing there from 17:00 to 10:00 can bring a 100,000 won fine. Entry is free. Start at Anguk Station on Line 3, walk uphill quietly, and keep groups to ten people or fewer.

What should I know before I arrive?

Rules and directions checked October 15, 2026

Bukchon is a place where people live, so plan to arrive in the daytime and expect to be quiet. The village sits on a hillside between Gyeongbokgung Palace and Changdeokgung Palace, and the city has limited tourist visits to its busiest lanes since November 2024 because of overcrowding.

  • Worth knowing: The tourist window is 10:00 to 17:00, and Visit Seoul says the village is open every day of the year.
  • A common mistake: Arriving for a sunset walk. After 17:00 the red zone is closed to sightseeing, so you cannot linger there for photos.
  • Local tip: Wear comfortable shoes. The lanes climb steeply, and the best rooftop views come at the top of the slope.

What are the visiting hours and the red zone rule?

Tourists may visit the red zone from 10:00 to 17:00; sightseeing there between 17:00 and 10:00 can lead to a 100,000 won fine. The Seoul Metropolitan Government calls the area a Special Management Area, and its Seoul Metropolitan Government notice describes the restriction on tourist visits.

The Korea Times reported that the rule moved from a trial in November 2024 to fines from March 1, 2025. It places the red zone behind the Jeongdok Library, between the two palaces, and says the Jongno District Office is responsible for the fines. Because rules can change, confirm the latest wording on the linked official pages before you go.

The red zone is the residential core around the lanes of Bukchon-ro 11-gil, not the whole district. Shops, cafes and the main streets nearby are outside it, which is why an evening walk elsewhere in Bukchon is still possible.

How should I behave in a residential village?

Keep your voice down, stay on the public lane and never photograph inside a home. Visit Seoul asks visitors to practise "silent tourism" and lists specific requests:

  • Keep noise low, especially around Gahoe-dong 31, and avoid loud voices and horseplay.
  • Keep groups to 10 people or fewer, and do not use microphones, megaphones or loudspeakers.
  • Do not photograph or film house interiors, even when a door or gate is open.
  • Do not litter. Carry your rubbish with you.

Practical example: if you rent a hanbok for photos, shoot from the open lane at the viewpoint and move on quickly rather than stopping in front of someone's gate. Our hanbok rental guide covers where to rent nearby.

How do I get to Bukchon Hanok Village?

Take Seoul Subway Line 3 to Anguk Station and walk about 500 metres uphill. VisitKorea suggests leaving by Exit 2 or 3, while Visit Seoul lists Exit 3 as roughly 500 metres from Gyedong-gil in the village.

  1. Ride Line 3 to Anguk Station.
  2. Leave by Exit 2 or 3 and head toward Changdeokgung Palace's stone wall.
  3. Walk uphill into Wonseo-dong and Gahoe-dong.
  4. Stop at the information centre if you want a map. The Bukchon Village Information Center is inside the Jeongdok Public Library, reached from Exit 1, and is open 9:00 to 18:00 with free admission.

Combine the visit with Gyeongbokgung Palace, which is a short walk away: see our Gyeongbokgung Palace guide for tickets and hours.

What is a good walking route?

Follow the Changdeokgung wall uphill, pass the Wonseo-dong workshops, and finish at the Gahoe-dong 31 viewpoint. VisitKorea describes this sequence from Anguk Station, and it is a short loop you can fit into a morning or afternoon.

  1. Changdeokgung wall. Follow the old stone wall for a short uphill walk.
  2. Wonseo-dong Workshop Street. Traditional artisan studios line this stretch.
  3. Gahoe-dong. Hanok houses are densely clustered here, with an information and rest area at the entrance.
  4. Gahoe-dong 31 viewpoint. Turn around in the alley for rooftops with Namsan Tower beyond them, and keep your voice low.

Aim to arrive before 15:00 so you finish well ahead of the 17:00 cut-off, and walk back down a different lane to avoid crowding a single street.

Where can I go if I arrive after 17:00?

Choose a hanok area outside the red zone, such as the Ikseon-dong alleys near Jongno 3-ga. Third-party guides describe Ikseon-dong as a place for evening hanok atmosphere with no visiting-hour limit, but check opening hours of individual cafes yourself.

FAQ

Can tourists visit Bukchon Hanok Village at night?

Not in the red zone. Tourism visits are limited to 10:00 to 17:00, and entering for sightseeing between 17:00 and 10:00 can lead to a 100,000 won fine. Residents, customers of local businesses and people simply passing through are exempt.

Is there an entrance fee for Bukchon Hanok Village?

No. Visit Seoul lists the village as open to visitors every day with no admission fee, but remember that it is a residential neighbourhood, not a theme park.

How do I get to Bukchon Hanok Village by subway?

Take Seoul Subway Line 3 to Anguk Station. VisitKorea suggests starting from Exit 2 or 3, and Visit Seoul lists Exit 3 for Gyedong-gil, about 500 metres from the village. The Bukchon Village Information Center is reached from Exit 1.

Are there other hanok areas open in the evening?

Yes. Areas outside the red zone, such as the Ikseon-dong hanok alleys near Jongno 3-ga, have no visiting-hour limit, so they suit an evening hanok walk.

Can I take photos of the houses?

You can photograph streets and rooftops from the public alley, but Visit Seoul asks visitors not to photograph or film house interiors, even when doors are open, and to avoid loud voices and filming near Gahoe-dong 31.
