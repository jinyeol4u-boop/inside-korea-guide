Themed Cafes in Seoul: What to Know Before You Go

By Dragon Kim · Updated November 8, 2026 · 6 min read

Quick answer

Expect to pay for an experience, not just a coffee: animal cafes commonly sell timed admission with a drink included, such as a 3-hour cat cafe ticket. Since December 14, 2023, wild animals may not be exhibited outside registered zoos, with a grace period to December 13, 2027, so check each cafe's current rules first.

What should I know before I arrive?

Rules and listings checked November 8, 2026

Themed cafes change faster than almost any other kind of venue, so confirm that a cafe is still open and still offering its theme before you travel. Animal cafes in particular are shaped by new laws, and character or pop-up cafes may run only for a limited period.

  • Worth knowing: Many themed cafes charge admission or ask for a minimum order per person. An ordinary Seoul cafe expects you to buy a drink; a themed one often builds the drink into a ticket.
  • A common mistake: Turning up without checking opening days. One cat cafe listing in Hongdae reports that it is closed on the first and third Monday of each month.
  • Local tip: Visit on a weekday if you can. Animal cafes and pop-ups are quieter, which is kinder to the animals and gives you more time at a table.

What kinds of themed cafes are there in Seoul?

The main groups are animal cafes, game and hobby cafes, character or pop-up cafes, and traditional-style tea houses. Each group has its own etiquette, so it helps to know which one you are walking into.

  • Animal cafes: Cat cafes and dog cafes are the best known. These keep companion animals, which Korean law treats differently from wild animals (see the next section).
  • Game and hobby cafes: Board game and similar cafes usually charge for your time at the table, so staying longer costs more. Ask the staff how time is counted.
  • Character and pop-up cafes: These are often tied to a brand, show or artist and may last only weeks. Neighbourhoods such as Seongsu are known for them; our Seongsu-dong guide explains the area.
  • Traditional-style cafes: Hanok tea houses sell the theme through the building itself. See our hanok cafes guide.

Practical example: a first-time visitor staying near Hongik University Station can compare a cat cafe, a board game cafe and a hanok-style tea room within a short walk, then choose one based on opening hours and how much time the day allows.

Are animal cafes allowed, and what are the rules?

Cat and dog cafes are allowed, but cafes showing wild animals such as raccoons or meerkats are being phased out. According to the Korea Herald, an amendment to the Wildlife Protection and Management Act took effect on December 14, 2023 and made it illegal to exhibit wild animals in facilities that are not registered as zoos or aquariums.

  • Grace period: The same report says existing cafes have a four-year grace period running to December 13, 2027, during which they are technically allowed to operate without penalties. Another report, from the Korea JoongAng Daily, gave a slightly different description of the grace period when the decree was announced in December 2023, so treat any single figure as approximate.
  • Touching and feeding: The Herald reports that touching, feeding or climbing on the animals is banned immediately, with fines of 1.5 million won per violation, up to 5 million won.
  • Which animals: Companion animals such as dogs, cats, parrots and rabbits are excluded from the ban, as are livestock.

Dogs and cats can now be allowed in some ordinary restaurants and bakeries, but only if the owner opts in. The Asia Business Daily reports that an amendment to the Enforcement Rules of the Food Sanitation Act took effect on March 1, 2026. Only vaccinated dogs and cats are eligible, operators must register as pet-friendly and meet hygiene and safety standards, and entrances must carry a sign saying whether pets are allowed. Restaurants that do not opt in continue as before.

How do prices and tickets work?

How do I visit a themed cafe without problems?

Check the cafe's current page, arrive within its admission window, and follow the posted animal or seating rules.

  1. Confirm it is open. Look for the cafe's own page or map listing for current hours, closed days and last admission time.
  2. Read the rules before paying. Note age limits, photo rules and what the ticket includes.
  3. Arrive early on busy days. Timed tickets and small rooms can fill up, especially at weekends.
  4. Follow the staff. Wash or sanitise your hands if asked, keep your voice low and never chase or pick up an animal.
  5. Keep your bill clear. Ask what is included before ordering extras such as snacks for the animals.

FAQ

Are raccoon and meerkat cafes still open in Seoul?

Some may be, but not for long. Korea's amended wildlife law made it illegal on December 14, 2023 to exhibit wild animals in facilities not registered as zoos or aquariums, with a grace period reported to run to December 13, 2027. Touching, feeding or climbing on the animals is already banned.

Can I bring my dog into a Seoul cafe?

Only where the cafe allows it. Since March 1, 2026, restaurants and bakeries can opt in as pet-friendly if they meet hygiene and safety rules, and only vaccinated dogs and cats are eligible. A sign at the entrance should say whether pets are allowed.

How much does a cat cafe cost in Seoul?

Prices vary by cafe and change often. A reseller listing for Roof Cat Me in Hongdae describes a 3-hour admission with one free drink per person for visitors aged 12 and over, but you should confirm the current price with the cafe or the ticket seller.

Do themed cafes expect you to order a drink?

Usually yes. Many animal cafes sell an admission that includes a drink, and ordinary cafes expect at least one order per person, so check the counter or ticket page before you sit down.
