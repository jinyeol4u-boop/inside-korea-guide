Korean Convenience Stores: A Tourist's Practical Guide

By Dragon Kim · Updated November 20, 2026 · 7 min read

Quick answer

Korea's four main chains had 53,266 convenience stores at the end of 2025, so one is almost always nearby. Use them for cheap meals, banana milk, instant noodles, parcels and ATMs. Currency kiosks, tax refunds and translation exist only at selected branches, so confirm on the spot.

What should I know before I go in?

Sources checked November 20, 2026

Treat a convenience store as a food stop first and a service counter second. Basic items are everywhere, but extras such as currency kiosks or tax refunds are branch-specific, and published prices are not fixed.

  • Worth knowing: The ministry says Korea has about one convenience store per 900 people, far denser than Japan (about one per 2,300) or the United States (about one per 2,500) (Ministry of Culture, Sports and Tourism, April 2026).
  • A common mistake: Assuming every branch has the same services. Tourist kiosks and translation tools are concentrated in places such as Myeong-dong, Hongdae and Incheon Airport.
  • Local tip: Keep a payment card and a little cash. Our cash or card guide explains what works where.

Why are Korean convenience stores worth visiting?

They are dense, open around the clock in many places, and increasingly built for visitors. The four main chains (CU, GS25, 7-Eleven and emart24) had 53,266 stores at the end of 2025, and Korea ranks first in the world for convenience stores per capita, according to the Ministry of Culture, Sports and Tourism.

The same page says stores also handle parcel pickup, utility bill payments, over-the-counter medicine and meals, and that store ATMs help fill the gap as bank branches close. A Korea.net report from December 2024 describes themed branches such as CU's Music Library in Hongdae and a GS25 in Jongno that makes pizza and robot-poured coffee in about eight minutes.

Foreign shoppers matter to these stores. JoongAng Daily (May 2025) reported in May 2025 that foreigners account for more than half of sales in Myeong-dong and Hongdae, and that nearly 80% of revenue at CU's Hongdae ramen library comes from foreign shoppers.

Practical example: if your hotel is in Hongdae, a branch with an instant-noodle section can cover a late snack, a drink and a card top-up question in one stop.

What should visitors buy?

Start with banana-flavored milk, a triangle gimbap or sausage, and an instant-noodle cup. Binggrae banana-flavored milk was the top product among foreign buyers at CU, GS25 and 7-Eleven in the first half of 2023 (The Korea Times), and the ministry still singles it out for tourist-heavy branches.

A June 2026 Asia Business Daily (June 2026) feature lists banana milk, strawberry milk, protein milk, Picnic drinks, Kkokkalcorn snacks and sausages as current foreign favourites, and mentions combinations such as banana milk with pouch coffee over ice. A GS25 owner in Songpa-gu said Buldak Bokkeum Myun used to lead foreign sales but newer items have overtaken it.

Which services can tourists use?

Common services are ATMs, parcels and card payment; kiosks for exchange or tax refunds are limited to selected branches. Always confirm the service at the specific store.

  • Currency exchange: JoongAng Daily (May 2025) reported in May 2025 that GS25 introduced a kiosk covering 15 currencies, described as running 24 hours a day. It is not a feature of every store.
  • Tax refund: The ministry mentions tax refund kiosks at emart24's K-food Lab in Myeong-dong. GS25 had announced plans to add refunds. See our tax refund guide for the general rules.
  • Translation: CU piloted an AI interpretation service in 38 languages at five company-run stores, including Myeong-dong, Hongdae and Incheon Airport, as of May 2025.
  • Airport browsing: The ministry notes a Snack Library at Incheon Airport run by BGF Retail, and CU opened a Snack and Ramen Library at Terminal 2.
  • ATMs and parcels: Store ATMs and parcel services are noted by the ministry. Foreign-card acceptance and fees vary by machine, so read the screen before you confirm.

For transit cards, read our T-money guide and confirm at the register whether the branch sells or tops up cards.

How do I make a quick convenience store meal?

Pick a ready-to-eat item, pay at the counter, then ask staff or look for a microwave or hot-water area. Facilities differ by branch.

  1. Choose. Take a lunchbox, gimbap or cup noodles from the shelves.
  2. Pay. Card and mobile payment are widely used; see our cash or card guide.
  3. Heat or cook. Ask the cashier to microwave it, or use the in-store hot water or microwave if the branch has one.
  4. Eat and tidy. Use a table if the branch provides one and sort your rubbish into the bins provided.

For a bigger sit-down meal, compare it with Korean street food.

FAQ

How many convenience stores does Korea have?

The four main chains (CU, GS25, 7-Eleven and emart24) had 53,266 stores at the end of 2025, about one per 900 people, according to the Ministry of Culture, Sports and Tourism.

Can tourists exchange money at a Korean convenience store?

Some GS25 stores have a currency exchange kiosk that handles 15 currencies, reported as running 24 hours a day. It is not in every store, so check the chain's app or the shop before relying on it.

Can I get a tax refund at a convenience store?

Only at selected stores. The ministry mentions tax refund kiosks at the emart24 K-food Lab near Myeong-dong Station; most branches do not offer this, so confirm at the counter.

What do foreign visitors buy most at convenience stores?

Binggrae banana-flavored milk has been the best seller among foreign buyers at CU, GS25 and 7-Eleven, and a June 2026 report also lists strawberry milk, protein milk, sausages and instant noodles as popular.
