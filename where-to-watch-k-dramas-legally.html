Where to Watch K-Dramas Legally Outside Korea

By Dragon Kim · Updated November 24, 2026 · 7 min read

Quick answer

There is no single legal K-drama app for the whole world. KOCOWA+ focuses on the Americas, Europe and Oceania, Viki runs a free tier plus paid passes, and Netflix libraries differ by country. Search a title in each app, and check the plan prices in your own store.

What should I know before I choose an app?

Details checked November 24, 2026

Rights to Korean dramas are sold country by country, so the same drama can sit on different services in different places. Prices and libraries also change, so treat the figures below as a snapshot.

  • Worth knowing: Several broadcasters, including KBS, MBC and SBS, license shows to more than one platform, and the licence can end. Always search the exact title before paying for a year.
  • A common mistake: Paying for a full year first. Start with a monthly plan, confirm your favourite show is there, then switch.
  • Local tip: Some dramas are listed under English titles that differ from the Korean one. Search both, as noted in our Munmu guide.

What does KOCOWA+ offer?

KOCOWA+ is a Korean-broadcaster-backed streaming service whose App Store page says it carries KBS, MBC and SBS programming with English, Portuguese and Spanish subtitles across the Americas. The KOCOWA+ App Store page is run by Wavve Americas, Inc. and lists these US prices:

  • Beyond the Americas: The KOCOWA+ corporate site reported in April 2024 that KOCOWA+ would launch in Europe and Oceania. Drama Wanderlust describes selected countries in the Americas, Europe and Oceania, and says it is not available throughout Asia or Africa.
  • Recent shows: The streaming guide Reelgood lists KOCOWA+ as the US home for Munmu, as noted in our Munmu guide.

What does Viki offer?

Viki has a free ad-supported tier and paid Viki Pass plans, but what you can watch depends on your country. The Viki App Store page (Singapore) describes a free tier plus Viki Pass for ad-free HD viewing and says title availability may vary by region.

  • Standard and Plus: TechRadar (March 10, 2026) describes Standard as ad-free on one device at 720p, and Plus as up to 1080p on up to four devices with downloads.
  • Prices: TechRadar quoted $7.99 (Standard) and $11.99 (Plus) per month for the US. The Singapore store lists different amounts in Singapore dollars, so check your own country's store.
  • Not verified: DaebakDaily reports that KOCOWA ended its content arrangement with Viki on December 3, 2025. We could not confirm that on an official page.

What about Netflix, Disney+, Prime Video and others?

Netflix, Disney+, Prime Video and HBO Max all carry some Korean titles, but none carries everything, and the lists differ by country. We could not read these services' official catalogue pages for this guide, so the details below come from two 2026 guides and should be confirmed in each app.

  • Netflix and Prime Video: Drama Wanderlust and DaebakDaily both cover them as mainstream options. Search the title in the app; the catalogue shown is your country's.
  • HBO Max: Drama Wanderlust reports a TVING hub in 17 Asia-Pacific markets from January 2026.
  • Viu: DaebakDaily says Viu operates in roughly 16 to 18 markets across Asia, the Middle East, Africa, Australia and New Zealand.
  • OnDemandKorea: Drama Wanderlust says it serves North and South America.

Are there free legal options?

Yes: Viki's free tier, Tubi in the countries it serves, and free channels run by Korean broadcasters. DaebakDaily lists the KBS World, SBS World and MBC Drama YouTube channels as sources of free episodes with English subtitles, and Pluto TV and The Roku Channel as having K-drama channels in supported regions. Free libraries are smaller and rotate, so confirm on the official channel page.

  • Safe habit: Use only apps from your official app store and sites you recognise. Avoid pages that ask you to install a player.
  • VPNs: Region locks are part of licensing, and TechRadar notes that even paying viewers may not be able to watch from abroad depending on the region they registered.

How do I choose in five steps?

Pick the show first, then the service that licenses it where you live.

  1. Find the broadcaster: Note whether the drama is on KBS, MBC, SBS or a cable channel; KOCOWA+ focuses on the first three.
  2. Search each app: Try Netflix, Viki and KOCOWA+ with the English and Korean titles.
  3. Compare plans: Look at monthly versus annual prices in your own store.
  4. Start monthly: Cancel when you finish the series; one-month plans cost less than most annual commitments.
  5. Plan the trip too: Many dramas film at places you can visit; see our Seoul K-drama locations guide.

FAQ

Where can I watch K-dramas legally in the United States?

Common legal options include Netflix, Viki, KOCOWA+ and Prime Video, plus free ad-supported services such as Tubi. KOCOWA+ lists KBS, MBC and SBS programming on its App Store page. Libraries change, so search the title in each app.

Is Viki free?

Viki has a free ad-supported tier and paid Viki Pass plans. A March 2026 TechRadar guide describes Standard as ad-free on one device at 720p and Plus as up to 1080p on four devices with downloads. Prices differ by country.

Does KOCOWA+ work outside the Americas?

KOCOWA+ announced launches in Europe and Oceania in 2024, and its App Store page describes the Americas. A 2026 guide says it is not available across Asia or Africa. Check the app store for your country before you subscribe.

Can I use a VPN to watch Korean broadcasts?

Streaming services restrict content by region in their terms, and one 2026 guide notes that even paying viewers may be blocked abroad depending on the region they registered. We recommend choosing a service that officially offers the title where you live.

Why is a drama missing from my country's Netflix?

Licensing is sold country by country, so the same title can be on Netflix in one market and on Viki or KOCOWA+ in another. Search the title in several apps, and check the broadcaster's official page for current licensing news.
