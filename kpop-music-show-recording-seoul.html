How to Watch a K-Pop Music Show Recording in Seoul

By Dragon Kim · Updated November 26, 2026 · 7 min read

Quick answer

15 is the minimum age for SBS Inkigayo, and you apply rather than buy: use an identity-verified broadcaster account or an artist’s fan club notice, then bring your original passport. Winning is not guaranteed, so set up accounts weeks early and keep your show day flexible.

What should I know before I arrive?

Details checked November 26, 2026

A place in a music show audience is applied for, not simply bought, and applying does not guarantee entry. A Visit Seoul answer says seats are very limited and that foreigners need a passport or alien registration card. That answer dates from April 2016, so use it for the principle and follow the current notice for details.

  • Worth knowing: "Recording" can mean two different things: a pre-recording of one artist's stage, often arranged through that artist's fan club, or the main broadcast with several acts.
  • A common mistake: Booking flights around one show and assuming a ticket will follow. Line-ups, times and audience calls are announced close to each broadcast.
  • Local tip: Keep the rest of the day flexible. Check-in can be hours before the cameras roll.

How do I apply for SBS Inkigayo?

Use SBS's audience application with an identity-verified account, pick a broadcast date and wait for an app notification. The SBS Inkigayo application page is in Korean.

  • Account: Identity verification is required. SBS says foreigners must use an account that has a registered date of birth. If sign-up stops at a verification step you cannot complete, do not use someone else's details.
  • What you enter: The page collects your name, birth date and the last four digits of your phone number.
  • Places: A winning entry admits one person.
  • Result: Winners are told by app push notification, can check the application history, and receive a mobile ticket in the app. Turn notifications on.
  • Venue: KFanRoute, an unaffiliated guide checked November 17, 2026, gives the SBS Deungchon-dong Open Hall at 442 Yangcheon-ro, Gangseo-gu, near Gayang Station (Line 9) Exit 1. Confirm the venue on your ticket.

How do Music Bank applications work?

Applications are reported to go through a lottery on the KBS website, using a verified KBS account. We could not read a current KBS application page, so the points here come from Korealogue (updated September 9, 2026), an independent guide.

  • Account: The sign-up is in Korean, and non-Korean applicants are asked for passport or alien registration details. Try it weeks before your trip.
  • Timing: Applications open the week before the broadcast, with results reported the following Monday by text message. Sources differ on the closing day, so read that week's notice on KBS.
  • Companion: A winner is reported to receive two places, with the companion named when applying and both showing ID.
  • Venue: KBS New Wing Open Hall, 13 Yeouigongwon-ro, near National Assembly Station (Line 9).

How do fan club and agency notices work?

Artists' agencies post their own audience calls, usually for fan club members, with a short application window and their own conditions. Three notices for Music Core show how much they vary.

  • Where to look: The artist's official fan platform, fan cafe, website and social accounts. Ignore reposts you cannot trace to an official account.
  • Conditions: Some notices ask for a membership card, specific albums, streaming records or an official light stick. Requirements change from call to call, so do not buy anything on the strength of an old notice.
  • Speed: Windows can be very short and close days before you expect. Convert times to your own time zone.

What ID, age and entry rules apply?

Bring your original passport, with the same name you applied under, and expect no filming inside. SBS lists a passport and alien registration card among accepted IDs and says staff check on the day.

  • Age: SBS does not admit anyone under 15 to Inkigayo, even with a parent. Other calls set their own limits.
  • No transfers: SBS bans handing over or reselling tickets. Do not pay a stranger for a "guaranteed" place.
  • Cameras: SBS bans photos and video.
  • Late arrival: SBS does not allow entry after the broadcast starts. Check-in times on fan notices are much earlier than air time.
  • Banners and food: The Music Core notices above banned recording, food and drink, and VIBY's also limited cheering items to the official light stick.
  • Bags: INI's notice said no luggage storage was available, so leave suitcases at your accommodation or a station locker.

As a matter of courtesy, stay where staff place you, do not crowd or follow artists' vehicles, and do not approach performers.

How do I plan an application from abroad?

Set up accounts early, watch notices the week before, and apply only through official channels. This is our suggested order, not an official procedure.

  1. Pick two shows: Match them to your travel dates and the artists expected that week.
  2. Create accounts: Try the SBS, KBS or Mnet Plus sign-up several weeks ahead, so a verification problem shows up early.
  3. Follow the artist: Join the official fan platform if a notice requires membership, and check the sign-up deadline.
  4. Apply: Type your name exactly as it appears in your passport.
  5. Check results: Watch app alerts, texts and the notice page.
  6. Attend: Pack your passport and anything the notice lists, charge your phone and arrive before check-in. Our Naver Map guide helps with the route.

Practical example: you are in Seoul from a Wednesday to a Monday. Before leaving home, test the SBS and KBS accounts. The week before arrival, check the KBS notice for that Friday and the SBS page for that Sunday, and look for your artist's own call. Plan a palace or neighborhood visit that you can drop if you win.

What if I do not win a place?

Watch the live broadcast, try a paid package with clear terms, or plan a different K-pop day.

  • Packages: Some travel companies sell music show admission with a tour. Check what is guaranteed, the refund policy and the ID rules before paying; we do not endorse any seller.
  • Free K-pop day: Our K-pop fan guide to Seoul covers K-Star Road, COEX and Hongdae busking.
  • Year-end shows: December award shows and specials follow different ticket rules; see our award show guide.
  • Help: The Korea Travel Hotline, +82-2-1330, can help with general travel questions in English. It does not hand out show tickets.

FAQ

Can foreigners attend a K-pop music show recording?

Yes, in some cases. SBS's Inkigayo application page covers foreigners with accounts that have a registered birth date and completed identity verification, and accepts a passport as ID. Other shows depend on a broadcaster account or an artist's fan club notice, and a place is never guaranteed.

Is it free to watch a K-pop music show in Korea?

Third-party guides describe broadcaster audience passes as free, and we found no fee on SBS's application page. Fan club routes may require a paid membership or album purchases, and travel companies sell paid packages. Read the exact terms before spending money.

How old do you have to be to attend?

SBS says people under 15 are not admitted to Inkigayo, even with a parent. Age limits in fan club notices for Music Core ranged from 15 to 19 and over, so check the limit on the notice you are applying under.

Can I film or take photos inside the studio?

No. SBS bans photos and video for Inkigayo, and the Music Core fan club notices we read ban photography and recording too. Put your phone away when staff ask and follow their instructions.
