Best Hanok Cafes in Seoul: Ikseon-dong to Seochon

By Dragon Kim · Updated October 9, 2026 · 6 min read

Quick answer

Start in Ikseon-dong, Seoul's best-known hanok cafe alley. Seoul Coffee Ikseon is listed open daily 11:00 to 22:00, about 150 m from Jongno 3-ga Station Exit 4, and its red bean butter rice flour bread is 5,000 won. For quieter tea, walk Seochon or Bukchon.

What should I know before I arrive?

Details checked October 9, 2026

A hanok cafe is a cafe in a traditional Korean house, and the best ones cluster in a few walkable Jongno-gu neighbourhoods. The hanok is a low, tile-roofed house built around courtyards; many in central Seoul have been converted into cafes, tea rooms and small shops.

  • Worth knowing: The alleys are narrow. Visit Seoul notes that Ikseon-dong's lanes are tight, so expect to queue or share space at busy times.
  • A common mistake: Relying on old opening hours. Cafes change hours and closing days, and some listings are several years old, so confirm on the cafe's own page or map listing before you go.
  • Local tip: Arrive earlier in the day. Narrow alleys feel much calmer before the afternoon crowds arrive.

Where are the best hanok cafe areas in Seoul?

Ikseon-dong is the densest cluster; Seochon and Bukchon are quieter and more residential. All three are in the old centre near Gyeongbokgung and Changdeokgung palaces.

  • Ikseon-dong: About 801 m of hanok alleys linking Insadong and Nakwon Music Market, now filled with fusion cafes, restaurants and shops, per Visit Seoul. The nearest station is Jongno 3-ga, Exit 4, roughly 120 m from the village marker.
  • Seochon: VisitKorea describes it as an artistic, literary area in the foothills of Inwangsan, with small galleries, independent bookshops and long-running local bakeries.
  • Bukchon: VisitKorea describes a historically prestigious residential area that is still lived in, with high walls, large gates and quiet streets. Visit as a guest of the neighbourhood and keep your voice down.

Practical example: a traveller with one free afternoon can take Subway Line 1, 3 or 5 to Jongno 3-ga, walk the Ikseon-dong alleys for a dessert stop, then continue toward Insadong for tea.

What should I order at a hanok cafe?

Try one Korean drink or dessert you would not find at a chain cafe, then add coffee if you like. The official pages above point to a few good starting points.

  • Traditional teas: Tteruan Teahouse is described as serving more than 10 types of tea, including herbal, fermented, wild leaf and floral teas. Osulloc Tea House Bukchon serves traditional teas and tea-based desserts.
  • Rice flour and red bean treats: Seoul Coffee Ikseon lists red bean butter rice flour bread at 5,000 won and sweet rice cake tiramisu. Tteruan lists red bean soup and red bean shaved ice.
  • A cold Korean drink: Seoul Coffee Ikseon also lists pumpkin sikhye, a sweet traditional rice-based drink, alongside Vienna coffee and milk tea.

Practical example: two friends sharing one dessert and ordering one tea and one coffee at Seoul Coffee Ikseon can try both traditional and modern flavours. Check the current menu, because items and prices change. If you are unsure what a dish is, ask staff or look it up on the Visit Seoul listing first, and look for a seat that does not block the narrow walkways of a converted house.

How do I plan a hanok cafe afternoon?

Pick one neighbourhood, go on a weekday if you can, and leave about two hours for a walk plus one cafe stop.

  1. Choose your area. Ikseon-dong for several cafes within a few minutes' walk; Seochon or Bukchon for quieter tea and sightseeing.
  2. Get there. For Ikseon-dong, use Jongno 3-ga Station Exit 4 (Lines 1, 3, 5). For the Jaedong Alley area, VisitKorea names Anguk Station Exits 2 and 3.
  3. Check hours. Look up the cafe's current hours and closing day. Several of the cafes above close one day a week.
  4. Walk a loop. VisitKorea's Seochon walk is about 2.1 km (around 2 hours) and its Bukchon walk about 3.2 km (around 2.5 hours), with cafes and museums along the way.
  5. Order light and linger. Order a drink and a dessert to share, and give up the table when others are waiting.

Pair your visit with hanbok rental or a first-timer neighbourhood walk for a full day in the old centre.

FAQ

What is a hanok cafe?

A cafe run inside a traditional Korean house (hanok) or in a building styled like one. Many serve Korean teas and rice-flour desserts alongside coffee, and some are in narrow alleys such as Ikseon-dong.

Which area has the most hanok cafes in Seoul?

Ikseon-dong in Jongno-gu. Visit Seoul describes it as a historic hanok alley area converted into cafes, restaurants and shops, about 150 m from Jongno 3-ga Station Exit 4.

Are hanok cafes quiet?

Not always. Ikseon-dong's alleys are narrow and popular, so visit early on a weekday. Seochon and Bukchon are described by VisitKorea as quieter, more residential areas.

Do hanok cafes only serve traditional tea?

No. The cafes in this guide list coffee, milk tea and desserts as well as Korean drinks such as pumpkin sikhye. Check each cafe's current menu before you go.
