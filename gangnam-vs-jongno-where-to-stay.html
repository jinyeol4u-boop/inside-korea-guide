Gangnam vs Jongno: Where Should You Base Yourself?

By Dragon Kim · Updated October 28, 2026 · 7 min read

Quick answer

Choose Jongno if palaces, hanok lanes and walkable history matter most; choose Gangnam for K-beauty shopping, COEX and a modern, quieter-at-night base. Visit Gangnam lists an airport limousine bus of about 70 minutes, while Jongno works via the AREX train to Seoul Station and Line 1. First-timers sightseeing the old city usually fit Jongno better.

What should I know before I arrive?

Information checked October 28, 2026

Gangnam and Jongno are two different Seoul bases on opposite sides of the Han River, so decide by what you want to see each day. Gangnam means “south of the river” and is known for COEX, K-beauty and luxury shopping, while Jongno holds the old royal quarter with palaces, hanok lanes and markets.

  • Worth knowing: Visit Gangnam lists Gangnam Station on Line 2, Sinnonhyeon Station on Line 9 and Apgujeong Rodeo Station on the Bundang Line, so your hotel’s exact station matters more than the district name.
  • A common mistake: Booking in Jongno for the palaces and then planning a Monday or Tuesday visit without checking closing days. Our palace comparison lists Gyeongbokgung as closed Tuesdays and the other main palaces as closed Mondays.
  • Local tip: Check the walk from your hotel to the station with luggage. Neither district is one single walkable block.

Is Gangnam or Jongno better for a first-time stay?

Jongno is better for history and sightseeing on foot; Gangnam is better for shopping, beauty stores and a modern feel. Neither is wrong, but each rewards a different kind of trip.

Gangnam pros

  • Shopping and K-beauty: Visit Gangnam describes Garosu-gil in Sinsa-dong as K-beauty territory with flagship and experience stores, and Cheongdam as a luxury shopping area.
  • A compact COEX day: VisitKorea’s Gangnam Style course lists a roughly 5 km route through COEX, the COEX Aquarium, Bongeunsa Temple (about 1,200 years old), Dosan Park and Garosu-gil.
  • A free rainy-day stop: Visit Seoul’s Starfield Library page says the Starfield Library inside COEX Mall is free and open daily 10:30 to 22:00.

Gangnam cons

  • Far from the palaces: Gangnam is south of the river, so a Gyeongbokgung morning needs a subway ride (our judgment; check the route in your map app).
  • Business and shopping feel: Visit Gangnam calls COEX the center of the district’s convention and exhibition industry, so expect a polished commercial setting rather than old lanes.

Jongno pros

  • History within walking range: VisitKorea’s Gyeongbokgung surroundings page lists Bukchon Hanok Village about 485 m from Anguk Station Exit 2 and Tongin Market near Gyeongbokgung Station Exits 2 and 3.
  • Ikseon-dong nearby: Our Ikseon-dong guide places the free hanok alleys at Jongno 3-ga Station, Exit 4, on Lines 1, 3 and 5.
  • Palace fees are low: Our palace comparison lists Gyeongbokgung and Changdeokgung at 3,000 won each, and VisitKorea’s Gyeongbokgung surroundings page says wearing hanbok gives free palace entry.

Jongno cons

  • Early closing times: The same VisitKorea page lists Bukchon’s alley as open Monday to Saturday 10:00 to 17:00 and closed on Sundays (the page was last updated in April 2024, so confirm before you go).
  • Less shopping-mall variety: The official pages for the area emphasize heritage and markets rather than malls (our judgment).

Practical example: a three-night trip built around Gyeongbokgung, Bukchon and Ikseon-dong fits Jongno, because each is a short walk or one subway stop from the next. A trip built around COEX, Garosu-gil and K-beauty stores fits Gangnam for the same reason.

How do I get from Incheon Airport to Gangnam or Jongno?

Take an airport limousine bus to Gangnam and the AREX train to Seoul Station for Jongno. Door-to-door times depend on traffic and your exact hotel.

  • To Gangnam by bus: Visit Gangnam lists an Airport Limousine Bus taking about 70 minutes. iVisitKorea’s Gangnam airport guide (an undated secondary source) gives about 17,000 won and stops such as Sinsa, Sinnonhyeon and Gangnam. Route numbers differ between sources, so confirm on the Incheon Airport website.
  • To Gangnam by train: The same secondary guide describes AREX all-stop to Hongik Univ. Station, then Line 2, at about 1 hour 30 minutes in total. Use an all-stop train because the express skips Hongik Univ.
  • To Jongno by train: Our Incheon Airport to Seoul guide lists the AREX all-stop train at about 59 minutes to Seoul Station for roughly 4,150 to 4,750 won. From Seoul Station, Line 1 continues toward the Jongno stations (check your map app for the exact transfer).

Late arrivals follow different rules, since public transit from the airport thins out around 23:30; see the airport guide above for the night options.

How do I choose between Gangnam and Jongno in four steps?

List your must-see places, check their closing days, test the airport route, then book near one station.

  1. List your top five sights. If three or more are palaces, Bukchon or Ikseon-dong, choose Jongno. If they are COEX, Garosu-gil or beauty stores, choose Gangnam.
  2. Check closing days. Use the palace comparison for Jongno sights and each venue’s own page for Gangnam.
  3. Test the airport route. A direct bus suits big suitcases; the train suits light packers heading for Seoul Station.
  4. Pick a hotel near one station exit. Confirm the exit on a map, then book a room within a short walk of it.

FAQ

Is Gangnam or Jongno better for first-time visitors?

Jongno suits most first-timers because the palaces, Bukchon, Insadong and Ikseon-dong are close together. Gangnam fits shoppers, K-beauty fans and travelers who prefer a modern district.

How far apart are Gangnam and Jongno?

They sit on opposite sides of the Han River. Visit Gangnam gives about 30 minutes from Seoul Station to Gangnam including a Line 4 transfer, so allow a similar subway ride between the two areas and check your map app for the exact route.

Is there a direct airport bus to Gangnam?

Yes. Visit Gangnam lists an Airport Limousine Bus taking about 70 minutes, and a secondary guide gives about 17,000 won. Route numbers differ between sources, so confirm the current number and stops on the airport website.

Can I visit both areas on one trip without changing hotels?

Yes. Seoul's subway links the two districts, so you can base in one and take day trips to the other. Staying put saves repacking time, which matters on a short visit.
