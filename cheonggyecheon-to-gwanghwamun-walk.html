Cheonggyecheon to Gwanghwamun: An Easy Downtown Walk

By Dragon Kim · Updated October 19, 2026 · 7 min read

Quick answer

Start at Cheonggye Plaza, reached from Gwanghwamun Station Exit 5 or City Hall Station Exit 4, and walk down the stream. The restored section is 5.84 km with 22 bridges. A guided walk from the plaza to Dongdaemun Design Plaza takes about two hours, and the paths close in heavy rain.

What should I know before I arrive?

Sources checked October 19, 2026

Plan for the weather first, because the stream paths can close in heavy rain. Cheonggyecheon runs through the middle of Seoul between Jongno-gu and Jung-gu, and the walk starts right beside one of the city's main avenues.

  • Worth knowing: Published lengths differ. The Seoul Facilities Corporation page gives 10.84 km for the stream in one place and 8.12 km in another, and says the restored section is 5.84 km. Treat the 5.84 km section as the walkable core and check the operator's page for the figure you need.
  • A common mistake: Assuming the evening light show runs all year. The operator lists the digital canvas at 19:00 to 21:00 from March 1 to November 30, with a later 20:00 to 21:30 window from May to September, so confirm before you plan a night walk.
  • Local tip: Use the Gwanghwamun end and walk downstream, so the Spring sculpture and Cheonggye Falls come first.

What is the Cheonggyecheon walk?

It is a restored city stream in central Seoul, reopened in 2005, with a footpath running along the water. According to the Seoul Facilities Corporation, construction began in July 2003 and finished on October 1, 2005. The restoration runs from the Dong-A Ilbo building in Gwanghwamun to Shindap Railroad Bridge in Seongdong-gu, and the stream flows west to join the Han River.

The operator also lists 12 fountains, 22 bridges and 10 cultural features along the route, including the Banchado of King Jeongjo. Seoul Danurim gives the stream's address as 37 Mugyo-ro, Jongno-gu, Seoul.

How do I get to the start of the stream?

Take Line 5 to Gwanghwamun Station Exit 5, or Lines 1 and 2 to City Hall Station Exit 4, then walk along Sejong-daero to Cheonggye Plaza. Seoul Danurim lists both exits as the nearest to the stream.

  1. Choose your station. Gwanghwamun (Line 5) and City Hall (Lines 1 and 2) are the two stations named by Seoul Danurim.
  2. Walk along Sejong-daero. From Gwanghwamun Exit 5, The Seoul Guide says to continue straight and the plaza is on your left. From City Hall Exit 4, continue toward Gwanghwamun and the plaza is on your right.
  3. Find the sculpture. The plaza sits at the head of the stream and is marked by the red and blue Spring sculpture by Coosje van Bruggen and Claes Oldenburg.
  4. Go down to the water. Take the steps or the sloped entrance to the stream path and walk downstream.

What can I see along the way?

Expect fountains, light displays, 22 bridges and a few historic features, all within a short stroll of the downtown start. The Seoul Facilities Corporation page lists the main attractions and when they run.

How long does it take and is it accessible?

Allow about two hours to walk from Cheonggye Plaza to Dongdaemun Design Plaza (DDP), longer if you stop often. Seoul Danurim gives that time for a guided walk. It also says the entrance is gently sloped with no steps, so wheelchair users can enter and leave easily.

  • Surface: The route is generally flat and gently inclined, but some sections are uneven.
  • Guidance: Tactile paving and Korean braille guide maps are available.
  • Elevators: The ones at Samilgyo Bridge and Hwanghaggyo Bridge are fairly small, so take care.
  • Restrooms: Accessible restrooms are listed at the Korea Tourism Organization Seoul Center and at Hwanghaggyo Bridge.
  • Not available: The page's captions indicate no stroller rental, family restrooms or accessible parking.

Practical example: a first-time visitor can ride Line 5 to Gwanghwamun, walk the first section to Cheonggye Falls and the early bridges, then leave at any bridge with steps or an elevator rather than committing to the full route to DDP.

Is the stream path ever closed?

Yes. Seoul restricts access to stream paths during heavy rain, and Cheonggyecheon was among those closed in July 2026. Korea JoongAng Daily reported that access to paths along 29 streams, including Cheonggyecheon, was restricted on Tuesday, July 21, 2026, after intense rain that reached 53.5 mm in an hour. The report added that another 30 to 100 mm was possible through Wednesday morning.

Check the forecast on the day, avoid the water's edge when rain is expected, and have a plan to walk along the street above if the stream path is closed. The sources checked do not give a standing closure schedule, so follow posted notices.

FAQ

Is Cheonggyecheon free to walk?

Yes. The sources checked list no admission fee for the stream paths, but the Seoul Danurim page leaves its fee field blank, so confirm on the operator's page.

How do I get to Cheonggye Plaza by subway?

Take Line 5 to Gwanghwamun Station Exit 5, or Lines 1 and 2 to City Hall Station Exit 4, then walk along Sejong-daero to the plaza at the head of the stream.

Is the Cheonggyecheon walk wheelchair accessible?

The entrance is gently sloped with no steps and tactile paving is provided, though some sections are uneven and the Samilgyo and Hwanghaggyo elevators are small.

Can I walk Cheonggyecheon when it rains heavily?

Not always. Seoul restricted access to paths along 29 streams, including Cheonggyecheon, during heavy rain in July 2026, so check the weather and follow posted notices.
