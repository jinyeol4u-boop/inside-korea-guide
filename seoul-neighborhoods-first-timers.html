First Time in Seoul: The Neighborhoods Worth Your Time

By Dragon Kim · Updated October 1, 2026 · 7 min read

Quick answer

Pick three or four of Seoul's seven main visitor areas rather than all of them. Bukchon and Insa-dong share Anguk Station on Line 3, Myeongdong suits shopping on Line 4, Hongdae and Seongsu suit cafes and youth culture, Itaewon is the most international, and Gangnam is modern and upscale.

What should I know before I arrive?

Details checked October 1, 2026

Seoul is a collection of districts linked by subway, so choose areas by theme and by station rather than trying to see everything. Each area below has its own personality, and several have rules or timing that affect when you should go.

  • Worth knowing: Bukchon Hanok Village is a residential neighborhood. Visit Seoul: Bukchon Hanok Village lists visiting hours of 10:00 to 17:00 with restricted entry outside them, and asks visitors to keep noise down and not photograph house interiors.
  • Timing tip: Insa-dong is liveliest on Sundays, when Visit Seoul: Insa-dong says the street is traffic-free and filled with performers and buskers.
  • Weekend note: The Visit Seoul: Hongdae Free Market is listed for Saturdays from March to November, 13:00 to 18:00; check the page for the current schedule.
  • Local tip: Load a T-money card first so you can tap through subway gates between areas without buying single tickets; see our airport transfer guide and essential apps guide for the basics.

Which Seoul neighborhood suits my trip?

Match the area to what you want from the day: heritage, shopping, nightlife, international food or design-led cafes. Station names and exit numbers below come from Visit Seoul pages and can change, so confirm on the day.

  • Bukchon Hanok Village (heritage): A residential area of traditional houses between Gyeongbokgung and Changdeokgung palaces. The nearest access listed is Anguk Station (Line 3), Exit 3, about 516 metres away.
  • Insa-dong (crafts and tea): Tea houses, galleries, antique shops, stationery and pottery. Anguk Station Exit 5 is listed about 418 metres away, and Jongno 3-ga Station is the other main access.
  • Myeongdong (shopping): Described by Visit Seoul: Myeongdong as Seoul's shopping mecca, with street stalls beside retail outlets and a strong reputation for Korean cosmetics. Nearest station: Myeongdong, Line 4, Exits 5 to 10.
  • Hongdae (youth and nightlife): The area around Hongik University is known for creative young artists, busking and clubs. The Free Market near Sangsu Station (Line 6, Exit 2) has run since 2002 and sells original artwork rather than second-hand goods.
  • Itaewon (international): Visit Seoul calls it the city's most cosmopolitan neighborhood. Visit Seoul: Itaewon lists more than 100 antique shops, the Seoul Central Mosque and dining from many countries; the station is Itaewon, Line 6, Exit 3.
  • Seongsu-dong (cafes and design): A former industrial district nicknamed the "Brooklyn of Seoul". Visit Seoul: Seongsu-dong highlights cafes in converted factories, a handmade-shoe street and the large Seoul Forest park.
  • Gangnam (modern and upscale): South of the Han River, with tree-lined Garosu-gil (Sinsa Station Exit 8 or Apgujeong Station Exit 4, Line 3) per Visit Seoul: Garosu-gil. The Visit Seoul: Gangnam City Tour route also lists Gangnam Station, COEX, Bongeunsa Temple and K-Star Road.

Practical example: a first-time visitor who likes history and photography might choose Bukchon and Insa-dong for one day, Myeongdong for an evening, and add Seongsu only if there is a spare half day.

How do I fit them into a day?

Group areas that share a station or subway line, and keep one theme per day. A sample outline for a first visit:

  1. Heritage day. Go to Anguk Station. Visit Bukchon within its 10:00 to 17:00 visiting window, then walk to Insa-dong for tea and crafts.
  2. Shopping evening. Take Line 4 to Myeongdong for cosmetics and street food once shops are open.
  3. Youth culture day. Visit Hongdae, adding the Saturday Free Market if your trip falls between March and November, then continue to Seongsu for cafes if you have time.
  4. International or modern day. Choose Itaewon for dining variety, or Gangnam for Garosu-gil and COEX; both are far from the heritage core, so allow travel time.

Seoul's subway and buses run on one T-money system, so you can change between areas without separate tickets. Check the route in a map app before leaving, as covered in our apps guide section.

FAQ

How many Seoul neighborhoods can I see in one day?

Two nearby ones comfortably. Bukchon Hanok Village and Insa-dong both use Anguk Station on Line 3, so they pair easily; crossing the city to a third area usually costs an hour or more of travel.

Is Bukchon Hanok Village open at night?

Visit Seoul lists visiting hours of 10:00 to 17:00, with restricted entry outside those hours because people live there. Check the official page for current rules before you go.

Which Seoul neighborhood is best for shopping?

Myeongdong for cosmetics and street food, Garosu-gil in Gangnam for boutiques and cafes, and Insa-dong for traditional crafts and tea houses.

Which area has the most international dining?

Itaewon. Visit Seoul describes it as the city's most cosmopolitan neighborhood, with cuisines from Indian and Thai to Greek, German and Mexican, plus halal restaurants near the Seoul Central Mosque.

When is Insa-dong at its liveliest?

On Sundays, when Visit Seoul says the main street is traffic-free and buskers and street performers fill it.
