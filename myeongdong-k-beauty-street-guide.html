Myeongdong K-Beauty Street: How to Shop Cosmetics

By Dragon Kim · Updated November 26, 2026 · 7 min read

Quick answer

Spend 15,000 won or more on one receipt at a tax refund shop to qualify for a refund on Myeongdong cosmetics. Take Line 4 to Myeongdong Station, Exits 5 to 10, bring your physical passport, compare prices between shops, and keep liquids over 100 ml for checked baggage.

What should I know before I arrive?

Details checked November 26, 2026

Myeongdong is open daily, but there is no single opening time, so check the shops you care about. Visit Seoul (page edited November 20, 2026) describes many Korean cosmetics stores with competitive prices and says hours vary by store.

  • Worth knowing: This guide covers cosmetics only. For fashion, department stores and bag storage, see our Myeongdong shopping guide.
  • A common mistake: Buying in the first shop you enter. The same type of product can be sold by a chain, a brand shop and a discount store within a short walk.
  • Local tip: Write down what you actually need first: for example one cleanser, one sunscreen and a few small gifts. A list is easier to stick to than a wall of sheet masks.

What kinds of cosmetics stores are on the street?

Expect health and beauty chains, brand or multi-brand cosmetics shops, discount outlets, department store counters and pharmacies. The mix changes often, so treat any single address as something to confirm on the day.

How do testers, samples and labels work?

Testers are for trying, not buying: Korean law bans the sale of cosmetics made for consumers to try in advance. That is how the English text of the Cosmetics Act reads; the Korean statute is the legal version.

  • Free samples: We found no rule that entitles you to any. Treat them as a shop's own choice, and decline politely if a gift comes with pressure to spend more.
  • Tester hygiene: Use a clean applicator or cotton swab where one is provided, avoid putting a shared tester straight on lips or eyes, and patch-test on your hand or inner arm.
  • Labels: The Act lists the use-by date or period after opening, and the price, among labelling items. Some items are handled differently on small packages.
  • Sensitive skin: If a product stings or causes a rash, stop using it and speak to a pharmacist or doctor. Our Korean skincare routine guide explains a simple order of use and how the Ministry of Food and Drug Safety category of "functional cosmetics" works.

How can I check prices before paying?

Look for a displayed price, ask for the total before you tap your card, and read the receipt at the counter. Jung-gu district made price display compulsory for shops in a Myeongdong zone of about 0.42 square kilometres from October 2023, according to Edaily (in Korean).

  • Limits of that rule: The 2023 report says street vendors were not covered. We did not find a current official page restating the zone, so do not rely on it alone.
  • Compare like with like: Check volume in millilitres, the number of items in a set and whether a "1+1" offer needs a minimum purchase.
  • Discount claims: A percentage off means little without the actual price. Compare the won figure with another shop.
  • Returns: Ask before paying. Opened cosmetics and discounted sets may have different terms.

Practical example: you find a two-pack cleanser set in one shop. Photograph the price and volume, walk to a second shop, and compare the price per 100 ml. If the difference is small, buy where the refund is taken off at the till and the return terms are clear.

How do tax refunds and duty free differ?

A tax refund gives back part of the VAT on goods bought in ordinary shops; duty-free shops sell tax-free goods to departing travellers under their own pickup rules. Most street cosmetics shops fall in the first group, and only participating ones offer it.

  • Minimum: 15,000 won on one receipt at one store, per our tax refund guide (checked October 6, 2026), which cites VisitKorea.
  • Immediate refund limits: Under 1,000,000 won per purchase and 5,000,000 won per stay. The government announced these ceilings in November 2023 to start on January 1, 2024, as reported by Korea JoongAng Daily.
  • Passport: Carry the physical document and show it before payment.
  • Export: Goods generally need to leave Korea within three months. Keep them unused with the receipt if you claim at the airport.
  • Duty free: Read our city versus airport duty-free guide before ordering, and check your home country's import allowance.

How do I fly home with cosmetics?

On international flights, hand-luggage liquids must be in containers of 100 ml or less inside one 1 L transparent zip bag. Incheon Airport's restricted items page also says duty-free liquids of any volume are allowed only in a duty-free liquid item bag that stays sealed until you board the flight to your final destination.

  • Full-size bottles: Put them in checked baggage, with caps taped and bottles in a sealed bag.
  • Sprays: The airport page limits non-flammable sprays in checked bags to 500 ml per item and 2 L in total. Read the can and ask your airline about flammable aerosols.
  • Connections: Transit airports can apply their own screening, so check each one before opening any sealed bag.
  • Refund goods: If customs needs to see them, do not check them in until that step is done.

How do I plan a two-hour beauty run?

Set a list and budget, compare two or three shops, then buy once so refunds and bags are simple. Two hours is our planning suggestion, not a measured walking time.

  1. Prepare: Pack your passport, list your skin type and the products you need, and set a won budget.
  2. Arrive: Take Line 4 to Myeongdong Station and use an exit between 5 and 10. Our Naver Map guide helps you find each address.
  3. Browse first: Visit a chain, a brand shop and a discount outlet, noting prices without buying.
  4. Test: Patch-test textures and check shades in daylight near the entrance.
  5. Buy and refund: Group items from one store on one receipt, show your passport and check the total.
  6. Pack: At your room, sort bottles into hand-bag sizes and checked-bag sizes.

Need help in English? The Myeongdong Tourist Information Center at 66 Eulji-ro is listed by Visit Seoul as open 09:00 to 18:00, closed Sundays and holidays (page last edited June 2023). The Korea Travel Hotline is +82-2-1330. For dinner afterwards, see the Myeongdong street food guide.

FAQ

Which subway exit is best for Myeongdong cosmetics shops?

Visit Seoul lists Line 4, Myeongdong Station, Exits 5 to 10 for the Myeongdong shopping area. Choose the exit closest to the first shop on your list, because every store has its own address and hours.

Can I buy cosmetic testers or samples in Myeongdong?

You should not be sold them. The English text of Korea's Cosmetics Act bans selling, or displaying for sale, cosmetics made or imported for consumers to try in advance. Whether a shop gives free samples is up to the shop.

Do I get a tax refund on cosmetics in Myeongdong?

At participating tax refund shops, yes. Our tax refund guide, which cites VisitKorea, gives a 15,000 won minimum per receipt. Bring your physical passport and ask before paying, because not every shop takes part.

Can I carry Korean cosmetics in my hand luggage?

Liquids and gels in containers of 100 ml or less go in one 1 L transparent zip bag on international flights, according to Incheon Airport. Larger bottles belong in checked baggage unless they are sealed duty-free purchases.
