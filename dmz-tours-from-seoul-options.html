DMZ Tours from Seoul: Which Option Is Right for You?

By Dragon Kim · Updated October 10, 2026 · 7 min read

Quick answer

About 3 hours on site is typical for the standard Paju DMZ trip: the Third Tunnel, an observatory and Imjingak. Bring your passport and avoid Mondays. The JSA at Panmunjom is reportedly not open to the general public, so confirm before booking.

What should I know before I arrive?

Details checked October 10, 2026

The DMZ is an active security zone, so you can only enter through a guided tour with valid ID, and tours can be cancelled at short notice. The Paju DMZ visitor guide says visitors may go only to designated areas, that there are no separate tour guides, and that tours may be cancelled for DMZ security threats, bad weather or safety issues.

  • Worth knowing: Bring a passport or Alien Registration Card. VisitKorea says all visitors must bring identification, and the Paju guide says photos or screenshots of ID are not accepted.
  • A common mistake: Planning a Monday visit. VisitKorea lists the Third Tunnel as closed on Mondays, weekday public holidays and the Seollal and Chuseok holidays.
  • Local tip: Stay on the marked roads. The Paju guide prohibits leaving designated roads because of possible minefields, and bans alcohol, weapons, hazardous materials and fishing equipment.

What DMZ tour options are there from Seoul?

There are three main options: a standard Paju tour with the Third Tunnel and an observatory, a government-run Paju ticket you buy at Imjingak, and the JSA at Panmunjom, which is currently the hard one to get. The table compares them using the official pages and one private guide site.

What does the standard Paju tour include?

It centres on the Third Tunnel, a North Korean infiltration tunnel discovered in 1978, about 52 km from Seoul, with the tour starting at Imjingak Pavilion. VisitKorea gives the address as 210-358 Je3ttanggul-ro, Gunnae-myeon, Paju-si, and an average visit of about 3 hours as part of a DMZ Peace Tour.

  • Hours: 09:10 to 14:40, closed Mondays, weekday public holidays, Seollal and Chuseok, per VisitKorea.
  • Entry fees: Monorail 12,200 won for adults and 9,500 won for teenagers and children; on foot 9,200 won for adults and 7,000 won for teenagers and children.
  • Who may not enter the tunnel: The Paju guide says wheelchair users and people with mobility difficulties may not enter the Third Tunnel, and elderly visitors or those with conditions such as blood pressure or respiratory problems may be barred.
  • Facilities: A restroom, a convenience store and a Tongilchon local produce shop are listed at the site.
  • Booking note: VisitKorea says visitors must reserve in advance, while the Paju guide describes buying tickets in person at Imjingak. The two pages differ, so call the numbers below or use an operator that confirms your seat.

Practical example: a traveller who wants the shortest path to the tunnel can pick the monorail for 3,000 won more than the walking fee, according to the figures above, and save energy for the observatory view.

Can I visit the JSA at Panmunjom?

As of the sources I could read, general civilian JSA tours have not resumed, so treat any JSA offer as unconfirmed until the operator shows proof. The Korea JoongAng Daily reported that the UN Command stopped the programme after a US soldier crossed into North Korea during a tour in July 2023.

  • Limited reopening: NK News reported a special tour on May 16, 2025 for 17 trainees in a unification policy programme, described as a limited reopening.
  • Current status: A commercial travel blog (updated June 17, 2026) says JSA tours are not open to the general public and cites no primary source, so this is not officially verified.
  • If it opens: The private guide site says JSA tours need passport details submitted about 2 weeks ahead and a strict dress code with closed shoes.

Should I join an operator tour or go on my own?

Join an operator tour for convenience; go on your own only if you are comfortable reaching Imjingak and buying a ticket in person. Imjingak is the common starting point for both routes.

  1. Pick a day. Avoid Mondays and weekday public holidays; check the Seollal and Chuseok dates.
  2. Decide how you will travel. An operator tour handles pickup; on your own, plan your route to Imjingak with a map app (see our essential apps guide) and allow time for the ticket queue.
  3. Secure the ticket. The Paju guide says tickets are bought and picked up in person at Imjingak, and are issued once all group members arrive.
  4. Have ID ready. The original passport or card, not a photo.
  5. Keep a backup plan. Tours may be cancelled for security or weather, so ask the operator about refunds.

What should I bring, wear and book?

Bring ID, wear comfortable shoes and layers, and book early if you use an operator. The private guide site says no strict dress code applies to the standard route, but JSA rules are strict.

  • Bring: Passport or ARC, a light daypack, and some cash or a card for entry fees (see cash or card in Korea).
  • Do not bring: Alcohol, weapons or fishing equipment, which the Paju guide prohibits.
  • Questions: VisitKorea lists the 1330 Travel Hotline at +82-2-1330 in Korean, English, Japanese and Chinese, and the Paju guide lists the Imjingak ticket office at 031-954-0303 and 031-954-0640.

Practical example: a family with a child under 7 should check the operator's age limit before paying; the private guide site mentions age 7 and over for the standard tour.

FAQ

Do I need a passport for a DMZ tour?

Yes. VisitKorea says all visitors must bring identification such as a passport or Alien Registration Card, and the Paju DMZ guide says photos or screenshots of ID and expired passports are not accepted.

Can I visit the DMZ without a tour?

Not the restricted sites. The Paju DMZ guide says there are no separate tour guides and that visitors must join a guided tour before entering. You can visit Imjingak, the pavilion where tours start, but the tunnel and observatory sit inside the controlled area.

Is the JSA at Panmunjom open to tourists?

Not to the general public, as far as published reports show. Civilian tours stopped in 2023 and a May 2025 reopening was a special tour for 17 government trainees. Confirm with a licensed operator before you pay.

Which day is best for a DMZ tour?

Any day except Monday. VisitKorea lists the Third Tunnel as closed on Mondays, weekday public holidays, and the Seollal and Chuseok holidays, and the Paju guide adds New Year's Day.

How long does a DMZ tour from Seoul take?

VisitKorea gives an average of about 3 hours at the sites themselves. A private guide site says full-day operator tours run 5 to 8 hours including transfers, so ask your operator for the exact schedule.
