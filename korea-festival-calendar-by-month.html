Korea Festival Calendar: The Best Festivals by Month

By Dragon Kim · Updated October 9, 2026 · 8 min read

Quick answer

Korea has a major festival in almost every month. Winter brings ice fishing and lights, spring brings Jinhae cherry blossoms and May lanterns, summer brings the Boryeong Mud Festival, and autumn is the busiest season. Dates shift yearly, so use the 2026 dates below as a guide and confirm with organizers.

Ice fishing, snow and light festivals

What should I know before I plan around a festival?

Details checked October 9, 2026

Treat every date below as the latest published date, not a promise for next year. Organizers move festivals by days or weeks, and lunar-calendar events shift more. This guide is compiled from official listings and is not a record of attending these events.

  • Worth knowing: VisitKorea states that its festival dates and schedules are subject to change, and lists the 1330 Korea Travel Helpline at +82-2-1330 for checking details (VisitKorea winter festivals).
  • A common mistake: Booking flights around one date. The Seoul Lantern Festival ran December 12, 2025 to January 18, 2026 in the latest winter list, while an older VisitKorea page describes it as a November event (VisitKorea: Seoul Lantern Festival).
  • Local tip: Festival crowds fill transit and beds. Book rooms early, and see our best time to visit Korea guide for weather by month.

Which festivals are worth it from December to February?

Choose the Hwacheon Sancheoneo Ice Festival for a winter activity and the Seoul or Busan light festivals for city evenings. The latest VisitKorea winter list gives these 2025 to 2026 dates (VisitKorea winter festivals):

  • Hwacheon Sancheoneo Ice Festival, Gangwon: January 10 to February 1, 2026, with ice fishing, bare-handed mountain trout catches and ice sledding. VisitKorea says it draws over one million visitors a year.
  • Seoul Lantern Festival, Cheonggyecheon: December 12, 2025 to January 18, 2026, with light installations along the Cheonggyecheon and Uicheon streams.
  • Haeundae Light Festival, Busan: November 29, 2025 to January 18, 2026, opening with a lighting ceremony and fireworks from 18:00.
  • Taebaeksan Mountain Snow Festival, Taebaek: January 31 to February 8, 2026, known for snow sculptures.
  • New Year sunrise festivals: Jeongdongjin (Gangneung), Homigot (Pohang) and Hyangiram (Yeosu) each ran December 31, 2025 to January 1, 2026.

Practical example: a traveller based in Seoul in early January could see the lantern lights on a weeknight and add a Hwacheon day trip, then check the Hwacheon organizer for the exact year's dates.

What are the best spring festivals in Korea?

Jinhae Gunhangje is the cherry blossom headliner in late March and early April, and the Lotus Lantern Festival lights up May. See also our hanbok rental guide if you want a traditional look for blossom photos.

  • Jinhae Gunhangje Festival, Changwon: listed as March 27 to April 5, 2026. VisitKorea says it draws more than two million visitors a year with about 360,000 cherry trees, and that admission is free though fees vary by program (VisitKorea: Jinhae Gunhangje). Visitors can also enter the Korea Naval Academy and Naval Base Command, which are normally closed to the public.
  • Lotus Lantern Festival (Yeon Deung Hoe): tied to Buddha's Birthday, which fell on May 24, 2026. Seoul ran May 8 to 25 around Jogyesa Temple, with a parade of more than 100,000 lanterns about 3 km from Heunginjimun Gate to Jogyesa on Saturday, May 16. Busan ran May 1 to 17, Jeonju April 25 to May 24 and Gyeongju May 14 to 31 (VisitKorea: 2026 Yeon Deung Hoe guide).

The same page says the lantern parades are free, usually start around sunset, and that Seoul requires advance registration to join the parade. Some roads close, so take public transport.

Is there a big summer festival in Korea?

Yes: the Boryeong Mud Festival at Daecheon Beach, listed for July 24 to August 9 in 2026. VisitKorea places it at the Boryeong Mud Expo Plaza in Chungcheongnam-do, overlooking Daecheon Beach (VisitKorea: Boryeong Mud Festival).

  • Zones: a General Zone with a Mud Run, a Family Zone and a Water Park Zone.
  • By day and night: a Mud Mob Scene with DJ performances and mud water cannons by day, and a drone show over the sea in the evening.
  • Cost: VisitKorea marks it as paid but gives no price, and points to +82-41-930-0891 and the festival site for fees.

Which festivals happen in autumn?

Autumn has the most choice, from the Andong Maskdance Festival to Suwon's fortress procession and Jinju's river lanterns. VisitKorea's 2026 autumn list (last updated August 2026) gives these dates (VisitKorea: 2026 autumn festivals):

  • Seoul International Fireworks Festival: September 5, over the Han River.
  • Andong Maskdance Festival: September 24 to October 4, with Korean and international mask dance performances and a parade.
  • Suwon Hwaseong Festival: October 4 to 11, with a royal procession reenactment at the UNESCO-listed fortress.
  • Jinju Namgang Yudeung Festival: October 3 to 18, with more than 70,000 lanterns along the Namgang River.
  • Busan International Film Festival: October 6 to 15 in the table, though the page's own FAQ lists October 6 to 11, so confirm the closing date.
  • Food events: Sunchang Jangryu (October 15 to 18), Gangneung Coffee (October 21 to 25), Gimcheon Gimbap and Gwangju Kimchi (both October 23 to 25) and Gumi Ramyun (November 6 to 8).

FAQ

What is the biggest festival month in Korea?

There is no single peak, but October is dense: the latest VisitKorea autumn list shows the Andong Maskdance Festival, Suwon Hwaseong Festival, Jinju lantern festival and Busan International Film Festival all running in October 2026.

When is the Lotus Lantern Festival in Seoul?

It is tied to Buddha's Birthday. In 2026 the Seoul festival ran May 8 to 25, with the main parade on Saturday, May 16. Seoul requires advance registration to join the parade, so check the official page when 2027 dates appear.

Are Korean festivals free to attend?

Many parades and street events are free, but some programs charge separately. The Lotus Lantern Festival parades are free, while VisitKorea marks the Boryeong Mud Festival as a paid event and lists no price, so confirm fees with the organizer.

Do festival dates change every year?

Yes. Lunar-calendar events like Buddha's Birthday move, and organizers shift others by weeks. The Seoul Lantern Festival, for example, ran December 12, 2025 to January 18, 2026, while an older VisitKorea page describes it as a November event.

Where can I check current festival dates?

Use the VisitKorea festival pages linked below, then the organizer's own website. VisitKorea also lists the 1330 Korea Travel Helpline at +82-2-1330 for checking details before you go.
