Taejongdae Busan: Danubi Train Status, Access and Walk

By Dragon Kim · Updated October 31, 2026 · 7 min read

Quick answer

Taejongdae Resort Park is free to enter and its ring road runs about 4.3 km. The Danubi tourist train has been halted since July 2026, with temporary free shuttles instead, so plan to walk. Buses 8, 30, 88 or 186 from Nampo take about 30 minutes; confirm shuttle status on the day.

What should I know before I arrive?

Access, hours and service status checked October 31, 2026

Taejongdae is a free coastal park of pine forest and sea cliffs on Yeongdo Island, but its Danubi tourist train is not running, so expect to walk or use temporary shuttles. VisitKorea lists Taejongdae among Korea's must-visit spots and describes its dense pine forest and towering cliffs. Busan Navi gives the address as Yeongdo-gu, Busan, with free park admission.

  • Worth knowing: The Danubi train was halted after a July 3, 2026 accident, per Busan Ilbo (in Korean). Nocut News reported on October 27, 2026 that the halt has passed three months and no replacement has been decided.
  • A common mistake: Planning around the train. Older guides still describe fares and timetables that do not apply now.
  • Local tip: Search 태종대 in Naver Map or Kakao Map for live bus arrivals. See our Naver Map in English guide to set it up.

Because service can change without notice, treat every shuttle detail below as a starting point and confirm it on the day.

Is the Danubi train running, and what replaces it?

No: the Danubi train is halted, and temporary free shuttle buses carry visitors around the park. The Busan Tourism Organization stopped service after tie-rod problems caused accidents in April and July 2026, per Busan Ilbo. It also reported three of the organization's own shuttles running at 10 to 20 minute intervals, which it said cannot meet demand.

How do I get to Taejongdae?

Take a city bus from Nampo or Busan Station; the ride is roughly 30 to 40 minutes. There is no subway station at the park, so the usual route is Busan Subway Line 1 to Nampo, then a bus. The routes below come from visitor guides rather than the city, so verify them in a map app.

  1. From Nampo-dong: buses 8, 17, 30, 88 or 186, listed at about 30 minutes.
  2. From Busan Station: buses 17, 88 or 101, listed at about 40 minutes.
  3. From Seomyeon: bus 88 directly, about an hour, or transfer at Nampo.
  4. Pay with a card: a T-money card works on city buses. Our Busan Metro guide covers the subway leg.

Weekends and holidays are crowded, according to Busan Navi's description of the park, so arrive early if you rely on shuttles.

What are the hours and is there an entry fee?

Entry is free and the park is open from early morning to midnight, but individual facilities keep shorter hours. One July 2026 guide lists park hours of 04:00 to 24:00 from March to October and 05:00 to 24:00 from November to February. Treat those as approximate and confirm with the operator.

  • Park and ring road: free, with a ring road of about 4.3 km.
  • Yeongdo Lighthouse: generally 09:00 to 18:00 per the same guide; Busan Navi says it was built in December 1906.
  • Shuttles, cafes and stalls: may run on separate, shorter schedules.
  • Parking: a 2024 guide listed 1,000 won per hour for a small vehicle, which may have changed.

In November sunset is early, so plan to finish the lighthouse area by mid-afternoon. See Korea in November for weather and clothing.

What is worth seeing in the park?

The observatory, Yeongdo Lighthouse and Sinseon Rock are the main stops, and all are on the ring road. Visitor guides describe the following:

  • Taejongdae Observatory: a free viewpoint over the rocky coast and open sea; on clear days Tsushima Island is said to be visible.
  • Yeongdo Lighthouse: a short walk from the observatory, reached by a long stairway, with a viewing deck and seafood stalls on the rocks below.
  • Sinseon Rock: cliff formations below the main road.
  • Taejongsa Temple: a wooded temple known for hydrangeas in early summer.

The Ocean Flying zipline closed on June 6, 2026 over an unpaid usage fee, with a target reopening around September per Busan Ilbo. Whether it has reopened was not confirmed when checked.

How should I plan the visit?

Allow half a day: 1.5 to 2 hours with shuttles and the observatory only, or 3 to 4 hours to walk the full loop. Those estimates come from the July 2026 visitor guide, and the loop is described as moderate to difficult because of slopes and stairs.

  • Short visit: bus to the park, shuttle to the observatory, view, shuttle back.
  • Full loop: walk the 4.3 km ring road with water, comfortable shoes and a rain layer.
  • Add Yeongdo: Huinnyeoul Culture Village on the west coast is a refugee settlement revamped in 2011; guides list buses 7, 70, 71 or 508 from Busan Station.

Practical example: two travelers staying in Nampo could take bus 30 to Taejongdae in the morning, ride the free shuttle if it is running, walk down to the lighthouse, and return by bus. Pair it with Gamcheon Culture Village the next day, or see Busan in 3 Days and First Time in Busan for where to base yourself.

FAQ

Is Taejongdae Park free to enter?

Yes. Park admission is free according to Busan Navi, and the lighthouse is also listed as free by a recent visitor guide. Costs come from optional extras such as food, parking or rides when they operate.

Is the Taejongdae Danubi train running?

No. The Busan Tourism Organization halted the Danubi train after accidents in April and July 2026, and a news report dated October 27, 2026 says the halt has lasted more than three months. Temporary free shuttle buses run inside the park instead.

How long does it take to see Taejongdae?

A July 2026 guide estimates about 1.5 to 2 hours for the shuttle and observatory only, 2.5 to 3 hours with the lighthouse, and 3 to 4 hours for the full loop on foot. The ring road is about 4.3 km.

How do I get to Taejongdae from Nampo or Busan Station?

From Nampo-dong, buses 8, 17, 30, 88 and 186 are listed at about 30 minutes. From Busan Station, buses 17, 88 and 101 are listed at about 40 minutes. Check live routes in Naver Map before you go.

What else is on Yeongdo Island?

Huinnyeoul Culture Village is a hillside neighborhood on the west coast, a former Korean War refugee settlement revamped in 2011. Visitors usually combine it with Taejongdae by bus or taxi.
