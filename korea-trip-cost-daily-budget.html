How Much Does a Trip to Korea Cost? A Daily Budget

By Dragon Kim · Updated October 29, 2026 · 7 min read

Quick answer

Plan on about 60,000 to 80,000 won a day on a lean budget with a hostel dorm bed, and about 105,000 to 175,000 won mid-range with a private room, excluding flights and shopping. Meals and transit are cheap; lodging and paid attractions decide the total. These are illustrative estimates, so confirm current prices.

What should I know before I budget?

Prices checked October 29, 2026

Korea has no single official "tourist daily cost", so every total here is our own arithmetic on published prices. Food and transit figures come from the Korea Consumer Agency price data and city transit pages; lodging ranges are unofficial because official pages list no rates.

  • Worth knowing: Figures are per person, exclude flights, shopping and tours, and assume you stay in Seoul.
  • A common mistake: Budgeting from restaurant "averages" and forgetting that lodging usually costs more than food and transport combined.
  • Local tip: Treat the totals as a starting range, then replace each line with a live price from the linked official pages.

How much do meals cost in Korea?

A typical sit-down Korean meal costs about 8,000 to 12,500 won in Seoul, and simple snacks such as gimbap cost under 4,000 won. Korea Consumer Agency data reported by SBS Biz lists Seoul's March 2026 averages, published April 16, 2026.

Practical example: gimbap (3,800), a kimchi stew set (8,654) and kalguksu (10,038) total 22,492 won for three meals, before coffee.

How much does local transport cost?

A subway ride costs about 1,550 won with a transport card, and a Climate Card day pass is 5,000 won. Our Seoul subway guide covers card fares of 1,550 won and single tickets of 1,650 won, plus a refundable deposit.

  • From the airport: The AREX all-stop train costs about 4,150 to 4,750 won; a taxi costs more. The Incheon Airport taxi guide lists a standard taxi base fare of 4,800 won plus distance and night surcharges.
  • Around the city: Four rides at 1,550 won is 6,200 won, so a pass pays off only on busy days. See Seoul transit passes compared and the Seoul Climate Card page.
  • Between cities: KTX fares vary by route and date; check Seoul to Busan KTX times and book on the official site.

A lean day is about 60,000 to 80,000 won and a mid-range day about 105,000 to 175,000 won per person. These are our sums from the prices above, not an official figure.

How do I build my own budget?

Price your bed first, add a food and transport allowance, then list the paid sights you actually want.

  1. Fix your nights. Check live rates on Visit Seoul stay pages and multiply by your nights.
  2. Add food. Use 22,000 to 48,000 won a day depending on how often you eat sit-down meals.
  3. Add transport. Count airport transfer, daily rides and any KTX leg.
  4. List paid sights. Keep free options in mind and pick two or three paid ones.
  5. Add a buffer. Reserve about 10 to 15 percent for shopping and surprises, and review cash or card in Korea for payment planning.

FAQ

How much money do I need per day in Korea?

About 60,000 to 80,000 won a day on a lean plan with a dorm bed, and roughly 105,000 to 175,000 won mid-range with a private room, before flights and shopping.

Is Korea expensive for tourists?

Meals and transit are cheap: the Seoul average for a kimchi stew set was 8,654 won in March 2026 and a subway ride is about 1,550 won. Lodging and paid attractions drive the total.

Is a transit pass cheaper than a T-money card?

Only if you ride about four or more times a day. A Climate Card day pass costs 5,000 won plus a 3,000 won card, against roughly 1,550 won per ride with T-money.

Do I need cash in Korea?

Cards cover most spending, but carry small cash for market stalls and some small shops. See our cash or card guide before you go.
