Jeonju Hanok Village Day Trip from Seoul by KTX

By Dragon Kim · Updated November 12, 2026 · 7 min read

Quick answer

Jeonju is about 1.5 to 1.75 hours from Seoul by KTX, with economy fares around 34,400 won from Yongsan. From Jeonju Station, bus 79 or a taxi of about 6,000 to 7,000 won reaches Hanok Village. Walking the village is free; Gyeonggijeon costs 3,000 won for non-resident adults.

What should I know before I go to Jeonju?

Fees, hours and routes checked November 12, 2026

Jeonju Hanok Village is a walkable cluster of traditional houses, heritage sites and food stalls in the middle of Jeonju city, and it is easy to see in one long day from Seoul. The official Hanok Village guide lists a tourist information center at 99 Girin-daero, Wansan-gu, open 24 hours a day, every day (phone 063-282-1330).

  • Worth knowing: The village offers hanok stays, hanbok experiences, craft workshops and daily performances, according to the official site. Our Jeonju and Gyeongju hanok stays guide covers staying overnight.
  • A common mistake: Booking a slow train. Mugunghwa and ITX-Saemaeul services take roughly three hours or more each way, so choose a KTX if you only have one day.
  • Local tip: The last Wednesday of each month is Culture Day, when Gyeonggijeon is free (official site). In November 2026 that is November 25.

How do I get from Seoul to Jeonju?

The KTX from Yongsan Station is the fastest option, taking roughly 1.5 to 1.75 hours and costing about 34,400 won in economy class. Those figures come from third-party schedules (Klook and Asia Odyssey Travel), not Korail itself, so confirm times and fares on the Korail English site before you book.

  • KTX: Departures run from Yongsan, and some from Seoul Station, Yeongdeungpo and Suseo. All trains arrive at Jeonju Station. Seoul Station is listed at about 34,600 won, first class at about 48,200 won from Yongsan.
  • Express bus: Departures from the Honam side of the Seoul Express Bus Terminal take about 2 hours 40 minutes to 3 hours 30 minutes, with fares of roughly 14,300 won (economy) to 27,100 won (premium). Allow up to 4 hours on Friday evenings, Sunday afternoons and holidays.
  • Slower trains: ITX-Saemaeul is about 3 hours for roughly 26,200 won, and Mugunghwa about 3.5 hours for roughly 17,600 won.

If this is your first long-distance train, read our guide to booking KTX tickets as a foreigner first. Book a return seat at the same time, because late-afternoon trains toward Seoul fill up on weekends.

How do I get from Jeonju Station to Hanok Village?

Take a city bus from the station and get off in front of Jeondong Catholic Church, or take a taxi for roughly 6,000 to 7,000 won. The official map and access page lists these options:

  1. Bus from Jeonju Station: Board at the station platform and take bus 112, 79, 508, 536, 542, 545, 546, 101, 119 or 999. The ride takes about 25 minutes.
  2. Get off at Jeondong Catholic Church, which sits inside the village.
  3. Taxi from the station: About 6,000 to 7,000 won.
  4. From the express bus terminal: Buses 79, 999 or 1994 take about 15 minutes, and a taxi is about 5,000 to 6,000 won.

The page does not give bus operating hours or walking distances, so check a navigation app such as the one in our Naver Map guide for live bus times.

What should I see in Hanok Village?

Start with Jeondong Catholic Cathedral and Gyeonggijeon, then wander the lanes toward Pungnammun Gate and Nambu Market. The official place guide gives these hours and fees:

The official special travel spaces page adds smaller stops: the Gyo Dong Museum of Art (10:00 to 18:00, free), the Sudajag cultural space (3,000 won, closed Mondays) and Cafe Haeng-Won, which lists pansori and gayageum performances every Saturday at 4 pm. Hours can change, so confirm anything you plan the day around.

Pungnammun Gate sits between the village and Nambu Market, according to the official site. If you want to dress up for photos, see our hanbok rental guide for how rentals generally work; the Jeonju site lists a "Hanbok Experience" menu but no prices, so ask at the tourist information center.

What should I eat in Jeonju?

Bibimbap is the dish to try, and Nambu Market, under three minutes' walk from the village, is the easiest place to eat well. The Jeonju tourism page on Nambu Market lists these shops, with hours that may change:

  • Pungnammun Bibimbap: Bibimbap and bean sprout soup, 04:00 to 18:00 (sometimes until 19:00), closed the third Sunday.
  • Dongrae Bunsik: Red bean porridge and hand-torn noodle soup, 07:00 to 19:00, closed Tuesdays.
  • Hankook Dakjip: Fried chicken, 08:00 to 21:00.
  • Eunhye Hyugesil: Nut-based ssanghwa tea for about 1,000 to 2,000 won, 06:00 to 16:00.

The official special spaces page lists the Nambu Night Market on Friday and Saturday nights, 6:30 to 11:00 pm. A day trip usually ends before then, so consider staying one night if the night market matters to you. For more on Korean street snacks, see our street food guide.

What does a sample day look like?

Take a morning KTX, spend about six hours in the village and head back on an early-evening train. Times below are a suggestion built on the figures above; confirm train times on Korail.

  1. Morning: Board a KTX at Yongsan and arrive at Jeonju Station about 1.5 to 1.75 hours later.
  2. Before noon: Take bus 79 or a taxi to Jeondong Catholic Church and walk the cathedral and nearby lanes.
  3. Lunch: Walk to Nambu Market for bibimbap before 18:00, mindful of each shop's closing day.
  4. Afternoon: Visit Gyeonggijeon before it closes at 18:00, then stop at the Gyo Dong Museum of Art or a tea house.
  5. Evening: Return to Jeonju Station by bus or taxi for the KTX back to Seoul. Compare with our Nami Island day trip and Suwon Hwaseong day trip for shorter outings.

FAQ

How long does the KTX take from Seoul to Jeonju?

Roughly 1 hour 30 minutes to 1 hour 45 minutes from Yongsan on the fastest KTX trains, according to third-party schedules. Slower Mugunghwa and ITX-Saemaeul trains take about 3 to 3.5 hours, so check the train type before you book.

How do I get from Jeonju Station to Hanok Village?

Take a city bus such as 79, 112, 508 or 999 from the station and get off in front of Jeondong Catholic Church, about 25 minutes, or take a taxi for roughly 6,000 to 7,000 won, per the official Jeonju Hanok Village map page.

Is Jeonju Hanok Village free to enter?

Yes, walking the village is free. Individual sites charge separately: Gyeonggijeon is 3,000 won for a non-resident adult, while Jeondong Catholic Cathedral and the Jeonjuhyanggyo Confucian School are free.

Can I do Jeonju as a day trip from Seoul?

Yes. With a KTX out in the morning and back in the evening you have about six to seven hours in the village, enough for the main sights and a bibimbap lunch. Stay overnight if you want the Nambu Night Market on a Friday or Saturday.

What should I eat in Jeonju?

Bibimbap is the classic. Near the village, Nambu Market has bibimbap, bean sprout soup, red bean porridge, hand-torn noodles and fried chicken, with hours that vary by shop.
