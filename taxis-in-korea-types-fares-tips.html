Taking Taxis in Korea: Types, Fares and Tips

By Dragon Kim · Updated November 11, 2026 · 7 min read

Quick answer

A regular Seoul taxi starts at 4,800 won for the first 1.6 km in the daytime and rises to 5,800 or 6,700 won late at night. Deluxe taxis start at 7,000 won. Hail one with a red "빈차" sign, or book through an app, and pay by card, T-money or cash.

What should I know before I take a taxi in Korea?

Details checked November 11, 2026

Korean taxis are metered, and the meter is what you pay, plus tolls and any app fee. A Korea Herald guide from October 27, 2026 says Seoul taxis come in regular, deluxe and premium services with different pricing, and that an app-booked ride is not automatically a premium one.

  • Worth knowing: Fares in this guide are for Seoul. Other cities set their own rates, so treat the numbers as a Seoul reference and confirm locally.
  • A common mistake: Adding a surcharge on top of a late-night base fare. The Korea Herald says the late-night base fares already include the surcharge, so no extra 20% or 40% should be added.
  • Local tip: Have your destination ready in Korean, for example on a map app. Our Naver Map guide shows how to copy a Korean address, and tipping is not expected (see our tipping guide).

What types of taxi are there?

For most visitors the choice is a regular taxi for everyday trips, a deluxe taxi for comfort, or a large van for groups and luggage. Incheon Airport lists standard, first class, oversized (9-seater deluxe or jumbo), international and smart taxis and pre-booked taxis, and says international and smart taxis offer foreign-language support.

How much do Seoul taxi fares cost?

A regular taxi starts at 4,800 won in the daytime, and the base fare steps up late at night. The Korea Herald gives these Seoul regular-taxi rates:

  • Daytime (04:00 to 22:00): 4,800 won for the first 1.6 km, then 100 won per 131 m, plus 100 won per 30 seconds when speed is below 15.72 km/h.
  • 22:00 to 23:00 and 02:00 to 04:00: 5,800 won base, with 120 won increments.
  • 23:00 to 02:00: 6,700 won base, with 140 won increments.
  • Out-of-area: Regular Seoul taxis can add a 20% surcharge for eligible trips beyond the service boundary, and combined surcharges can reach 60%.
  • Deluxe: 7,000 won for the first 3 km, with a 20% surcharge from 22:00 to 04:00.

Apps can add fees. The same article says the K.ride app, aimed at international visitors, adds a 10% service fee on its domestic taxi categories. Our Kakao T guide covers ride types and the pay-to-driver option. Fares change, so confirm the current rate with the operator or Seoul city before relying on them.

How do I hail, pay and ride, step by step?

Find a taxi with a red vacant sign, show your destination, ride on the meter and keep the receipt. The creatrip guide and the Korea Herald describe this flow:

  1. Check the sign. A red "빈차" (bincha) light means vacant, and a green "예약" (yeyak) light means reserved, according to the Korea Herald. Use licensed taxis with normal taxi markings.
  2. Or book in an app. Kakao T, Uber, k.ride, TADA and i.M are named in the creatrip guide; apps show an estimated fare before you confirm.
  3. Show your destination. Give a Korean name or address, for example from a map app, and ask the driver to use the meter.
  4. Pay. Credit cards work in most taxis, but card readers sometimes fail, so the guide suggests carrying a T-money card, WOWPASS or some cash. Our T-money guide and cash or card guide explain each option.
  5. Take the receipt. Seoul added English labels to key receipt fields in December 2025, including the final fare, boarding and arrival times and whether surcharges applied, per the Korea Herald.

Are airport taxis different?

Airport taxis use the same meters, with no boundary surcharge between Incheon Airport and Seoul, but tolls and night rates still apply. Incheon Airport says no boundary surcharge applies to trips between the airport and Seoul, Incheon, Goyang, Gwangmyeong, Gimpo or Bucheon, and that night surcharges of about 20% to 40% apply between 22:00 and 04:00. The Korea Herald adds that road tolls are charged separately.

  • Typical cost: The creatrip guide estimates about 60,000 to 95,000 won to central Seoul for a standard taxi and 100,000 to 135,000 won for a large one, plus 6,600 to 7,900 won in tolls. These are third-party estimates, not official fares.
  • Where to board: Use designated stands. The airport page lists numbered stands for each terminal and warns about illegal vans posing as taxis, and says to confirm the stand on site.
  • Compare options: See Incheon Airport to Seoul and late-night arrival options.

What if something goes wrong?

Keep your receipt and plate number, then report it to the 120 Dasan Call Center or through the QR sticker inside the taxi. The Korea Herald advises keeping the receipt, payment record and app booking details, and notes that missing receipts or photos can make violations hard to prove. The creatrip guide lists the multilingual 1330 tourist hotline, open 24 hours, as another route, with the vehicle number, locations, time and receipt to hand.

FAQ

How much is a taxi in Seoul?

A regular Seoul taxi starts at 4,800 won for the first 1.6 km in the daytime, then adds 100 won per 131 m, according to the Korea Herald on October 27, 2026. Late-night base fares are 5,800 or 6,700 won, and a deluxe taxi starts at 7,000 won.

Do I need to tip a taxi driver in Korea?

No. Tipping is not expected in Korea, and the meter plus any tolls is what you pay. See our tipping guide for the wider picture.

Can I pay for a taxi with a foreign card?

Most taxis accept credit cards, though a creatrip guide updated May 2026 warns that card reads occasionally fail. Carry a T-money card, WOWPASS or some cash as a backup, and check Kakao T's pay-to-driver option where it is offered.

Is there an extra charge for taxis from Incheon Airport?

Airport pages list no boundary surcharge on trips between Incheon Airport and Seoul, but tolls are charged separately and late-night surcharges apply. A third-party guide from May 2026 estimates about 60,000 to 95,000 won to central Seoul, plus 6,600 to 7,900 won in tolls.

What should I do if I think I was overcharged?

Keep the receipt and note the licence plate, then report it to the 120 Dasan Call Center or scan the QR sticker inside the taxi. The 1330 tourist hotline is a multilingual option, per a May 2026 guide.
