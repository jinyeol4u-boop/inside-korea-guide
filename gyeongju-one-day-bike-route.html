Gyeongju in One Day: A Bike Route Through Silla

By Dragon Kim · Updated October 8, 2026 · 7 min read

Quick answer

For about 2 hours by KTX from Seoul, Gyeongju's main sights sit within roughly a 30-minute walk of each other, so a bike links them easily. Rent by the hour near Daereungwon, ride to Hwangnidan-gil, Cheomseongdae and Wolji Pond, and keep Bulguksa for another day.

What should I know before I ride?

Details checked October 8, 2026

Start with a bike from the Daereungwon area, keep your loop in downtown Gyeongju, and treat Bulguksa and Seokguram as a separate bus trip. The Korea Bicycle Happy Sharing site says Gyeongju has six cycling routes totaling 108 km and that the city tourism site (guide.gyeongju.go.kr) describes each one (Korea Bicycle Happy Sharing).

  • Worth knowing: Bike shops near Daereungwon rent by the hour, and prices vary by shop, per VisitKorea. No fixed rate was found on an official page, so ask the shop before you hand over money.
  • A common mistake: Riding into the National Museum's outdoor area. VisitKorea says bikes are not allowed there, so lock up first.
  • Local tip: The downtown tourist office offers free bicycle maps, according to Korea Bicycle Happy Sharing.

How do I get to Gyeongju and pick up a bike?

Take the KTX to Singyeongju Station in about 2 hours from Seoul, then a local bus or taxi into the city, and rent a bike near Daereungwon. Korail trains stop at Singyeongju, which one guide describes as outside the city centre; local buses 60 or 61 take about 30 minutes to downtown and a taxi is roughly 12,000 to 15,000 won (secondary guide, so confirm locally). Book trains on Korail or see our KTX booking guide.

Rental points listed by Korea Bicycle Happy Sharing include the Daereungwon parking lot, Gyeongju Station, the Gyeongju Express Bus Terminal and Bomun Tourist Complex. Most one-day plans start at Daereungwon because the main sights are minutes away.

What is a good one-day bike route?

Ride a loop from Daereungwon through Hwangnidan-gil to Cheomseongdae, then finish at Donggung Palace and Wolji Pond when the lights come on. Downtown sights are all within about 30 minutes' walk from end to end, and Cheomseongdae to Wolji Pond is about 13 minutes on foot (per the secondary guide Korea To Do), so cycling shortens each leg further.

  1. Daereungwon Tomb Complex (about 1 to 2 hours). Rows of grass-covered Silla royal tomb mounds in a park; Cheonmachong is the tomb you can enter.
  2. Hwangnidan-gil. A street about 1.5 km long lined with hanok buildings and, by 2023, around 400 shops, cafes and restaurants, next to Daereungwon. Good for lunch.
  3. Cheomseongdae. The stone observatory tower from the Silla era, a short ride away. Guides list it as free to see, open until about 22:00.
  4. Woljeonggyo Bridge and Gyochon Hanok Village. Cross toward the river side for a quieter stretch before the final stop.
  5. Donggung Palace and Wolji Pond. The former Silla royal pleasure garden, popular for its night illumination; guides list hours of about 09:00 to 22:00.

Optional extra: VisitKorea’s own bike route runs from Daereungwon to the Five Royal Tombs, Hwangnyongsa Temple History and Culture Hall and the Gyeongju National Museum, for a longer, quieter day. Practical example: start at 09:00, ride the five stops above with a long lunch on Hwangnidan-gil, and reach the pond at dusk, then return the bike before the shop closes.

How much do the sights and the bike cost?

Expect roughly 3,000 won per adult for the paid sights, plus an hourly bike fee set by each shop. Fees have changed over time: VisitKorea (page updated March 2025) lists Daereungwon at 3,000 won for adults, 2,000 for teens and 1,000 for children, while the Korea Herald has reported plans to make the grounds free and charge separately for Cheonmachong. Confirm at the gate.

What should I plan around on the day?

Plan for lunch on Hwangnidan-gil, keep a Bulguksa trip for another day, and watch closing times so you can see Wolji Pond lit at night.

  • Bulguksa and Seokguram: Both are mountain sites reached by bus or taxi, with hours around 09:00 to 17:00 or 18:30 depending on the source. A day trip usually does them by lunchtime, so a bike day is better kept downtown.
  • Season: Spring and autumn are most comfortable for cycling, according to travel guides.
  • Navigation: Use KakaoMap or Naver Map; see our essential apps guide.
  • Next step: Compare a coastal base in the Busan area guide, since Busan is only about 30 minutes by KTX.

FAQ

Where can I rent a bike in Gyeongju?

The Korea Bicycle Happy Sharing site lists rentals at the Daereungwon parking lot, Gyeongju Station, Gyeongju Express Bus Terminal and Bomun Tourist Complex. VisitKorea says bikes can be rented by the hour near Daereungwon, with prices varying by shop.

Is Gyeongju good for cycling?

Yes. The city has six cycling routes totaling about 108 km, and the main downtown sights lie within roughly 30 minutes' walk of each other, so they are easy by bike.

Can I take a bike into the Gyeongju National Museum grounds?

No. VisitKorea notes that bikes are not allowed in the museum's outdoor area, so park the bike before you go in.

Can I see Bulguksa and Seokguram on the same bike day?

It is not practical. These two sites sit in the mountains outside downtown and are reached by bus or taxi, so most one-day plans visit them separately or save them for a second day.

How do I get to Gyeongju from Seoul?

By KTX from Seoul Station to Singyeongju Station in about 2 hours, then a local bus or taxi into the city, or by express bus to the Gyeongju Express Bus Terminal in about 3.5 to 4 hours.
