Sleeping in a Jjimjilbang: A Budget Option Explained

By Dragon Kim · Updated November 9, 2026 · 7 min read

Quick answer

Sleeping in a jjimjilbang costs roughly 12,000 to 20,000 won, and Riverside Spa Land in Seoul lists 14,000 won at night and 24-hour opening. You get a towel and uniform, then sleep on a thin mat in a shared heated hall. Not every spa stays open overnight, so confirm first.

What should I know before I sleep in a jjimjilbang?

Prices, hours and rules checked November 9, 2026

Treat it as a cheap, basic place to rest, not a hotel, and confirm that the venue lets you stay overnight before you pay. Lonely Planet describes many jjimjilbang as open 24 hours and used as budget accommodation, with overnight stays costing a few thousand won more than standard entry.

  • Worth knowing: The bath area is gender-separated and nude. You change into a provided uniform for the shared floor where you sleep.
  • A common mistake: Turning up late at a spa that closes at night. Official listings show some close as early as 21:00 or 22:00.
  • Local tip: Bring very little. Towels and a uniform are normally provided, and valuables are best left at your hotel or in the locker.

For the full bathing routine and etiquette, read our jjimjilbang guide.

Which jjimjilbang let you stay overnight?

Only venues that stay open through the night work, and the official listings show that this is a real difference between spas. Riverside Spa Land in Seoul is listed as open 24 hours with no rest days on VisitKorea tips, and Visit Seoul also lists it as a round-the-clock venue. By contrast, Spaland Centum City in Busan is listed on VisitKorea as open 09:00 to 22:00 with last entry at 21:00, and Supsok Hanbang Land in Seoul is listed on VisitKorea as open 08:00 to 21:00.

How much does a night cost?

Plan on roughly 12,000 to 20,000 won, with a small surcharge at night at many venues. Lonely Planet gives a general entry range of 7,000 to 20,000 won and says evening entry is usually a bit higher than daytime. Prices at specific venues differ between sources, as the table shows.

What is a night in a jjimjilbang actually like?

You sleep on a thin mat on a heated floor in a shared hall, with a hard block pillow, after bathing and changing into the supplied uniform. Lonely Planet describes a wide room with thin plastic mattresses and squishy plastic block pillows at simpler venues, and says you receive a towel and cotton pajamas on arrival. Venues differ, so expect a basic setup rather than a bed.

  1. Arrive in the evening. Bathing and eating before the hall fills up is easier. one visitor guide suggests arriving around 8 or 9 PM, and that weeknights are quieter than Fridays and Saturdays.
  2. Pay and store your shoes. Leave outdoor shoes in the entrance locker, then collect your locker key or wristband, towel and uniform.
  3. Bathe in the gender-separated area. Shower before any pool and keep phones out of the bath area.
  4. Change and find a spot. Put on the uniform and look for the sleeping hall. Pick a place away from walkways and the snack bar, because people pass through at all hours.
  5. Take one mat and one pillow. Taking extra is considered rude when it is crowded, according to one visitor guide.
  6. Check out on time. Return the key or wristband, pay any extras and leave before the venue's time limit to avoid overtime charges.

How do I sleep well and stay safe?

Keep valuables in your locker, bring earplugs and an eye mask, and expect lights and noise to continue through the night. The hall is communal, so privacy is limited.

  • Valuables: Your wristband or key also records charges, so keep it on you while asleep. Do not leave a passport or large amounts of cash in the hall.
  • Noise and light: Snoring, people walking past and early-morning activity are common. Earplugs and an eye mask help.
  • Sleeping areas: one visitor guide describes the sleeping areas as mixed, with separate baths. Layouts differ, so ask staff if you want a quieter or women-only area.
  • Health: Hot rooms can be intense. Drink water and do not fall asleep in the hottest rooms.
  • Alternatives: If you want a real bed, see our guide to budget guesthouses and hostels in Seoul.

FAQ

Can you sleep overnight in a jjimjilbang in Korea?

At many venues, yes. Lonely Planet notes that many jjimjilbang stay open 24 hours and work as budget accommodation, but some close at night, so confirm the hours and overnight policy before you go.

How much does it cost to sleep in a jjimjilbang?

Expect roughly 12,000 to 20,000 won. VisitKorea tips lists Riverside Spa Land in Seoul at 12,000 won by day and 14,000 won at night, while another VisitKorea page lists 15,000 and 17,000 won, so confirm at the counter.

Is a jjimjilbang safe to sleep in?

Guides describe a communal setting with lockers, but you should keep valuables in the locker or at your hotel and wear the locker key on your wrist or ankle. A jjimjilbang is a shared space, so it is not a substitute for a private room.

Is a jjimjilbang better than a hostel?

It can be cheaper and includes a bath, but you sleep on a thin mat in a shared hall with snoring and light. A hostel gives you a real bed and a quieter night, so choose by how well you sleep in noise.
