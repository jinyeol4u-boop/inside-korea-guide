How to Book KTX Tickets as a Foreigner

By Dragon Kim · Updated October 4, 2026 · 6 min read

Quick answer

KTX tickets now go on sale up to two months before departure. Book on Korail's English website or app with your passport details and a Visa, Mastercard or UnionPay card, pay before the deadline shown, and keep the QR e-ticket. Seoul to Busan takes roughly 2 hours 15 minutes and costs about 55,000 to 60,000 won in standard class.

What should I know before I book?

Booking rules checked October 4, 2026

KTX is Korea's high-speed rail service, and visitors can reserve seats online without a Korean phone number or bank account. According to the VisitKorea train page, KTX runs at up to 330 km/h and connects Seoul with Busan, Pohang, Mokpo, Yeosu, Gangneung and Andong, with trains leaving from Seoul, Yongsan and Cheongnyangni stations.

  • Worth knowing: Booking options listed by VisitKorea include the Korail website, the KORAIL+ app, ticket kiosks, ticket offices, NAVER Train Booking and KakaoT. The app is also sold under the name Korail Talk in some guides, so search the app store for Korail.
  • A common mistake: Waiting until the day of travel. Popular Friday evening, Sunday afternoon and holiday trains can sell out, so reserve as soon as your dates are fixed.
  • Local tip: Have your passport details ready, because the English booking pages ask for them when you create an account. Settle your arrival route first with the Incheon Airport to Seoul guide.

When can I book KTX tickets?

Since October 1, 2026, Korail has sold railway tickets up to two months before departure instead of one month. The Korea Times and SBS News both report the change, and the Korea Times says it is meant to help foreign visitors plan itineraries. The old rule was one month in advance.

  • Sales opening time: Older VisitKorea wording gives 07:00 as the usual opening time and says tickets can be bought until about 20 minutes before departure, or later in apps. I could not confirm the opening time under the new two-month rule, so check the booking screen.
  • Peak holidays: For Chuseok, Seollal and other long weekends, plan to book on the first day your date becomes available.
  • Language support: The Korea Times reports that the foreigner booking site supports seven languages today, with French, German and Mongolian planned by the end of the year.

How do I book a KTX ticket step by step?

Create an account on the Korail English page or app, search your route, choose a train and seat class, pay by card and save the e-ticket. Screens change, so use the steps as a guide and follow the Korail ticket reservation page for the current layout.

  1. Open the booking site or app. Use the Korail global site or the Korail app in English. Korail recommends Chrome, Samsung Internet or Safari and a stable mobile network on its notice page.
  2. Sign up with your passport details. The English pages ask foreign visitors for passport details, according to guides for visitors; follow the prompts on screen.
  3. Search your trip. Enter departure and arrival stations, the date and a time, then compare departures.
  4. Choose class and seat. Pick standard or first class and your preferred seat, then confirm the fare on screen.
  5. Pay before the deadline. A reservation is held only for a limited time, and unpaid bookings are released. Use the time limit shown after you reserve.
  6. Save the e-ticket. Keep the QR code or booking number on your phone and take a screenshot as a backup.
  7. Arrive early. Find your platform, check the car and seat number and board before departure.

What if my foreign card is declined?

Korail's English booking page accepts overseas Visa, Mastercard and UnionPay, and wallet payments such as WeChat Pay and Alipay are under development. The Korea Times and SBS News report these payment details.

  • Try another channel: If the website declines a card, try the app, or another card, as guides for foreign visitors suggest. Tell your bank you will pay a Korean merchant online.
  • Check card verification: Some overseas cards fail at the extra cardholder-verification step. Contact your issuer if this happens.
  • Use a station as a fallback: VisitKorea lists kiosks and ticket offices as booking channels, plus an English customer line at +82-1599-7777, open 08:00 to 20:00 daily.
  • Money basics: For cards and cash elsewhere in Korea, see the cash or card guide.

How much does KTX cost, and is a Korail Pass worth it?

A one-way standard-class Seoul to Busan ticket costs roughly 55,000 to 60,000 won, and a Korail Pass only pays off if you take several long rides within a few days. Published fares differ by source and by day, so confirm the exact price on the booking screen.

FAQ

When can I book KTX tickets?

From October 1, 2026, Korail sells KTX tickets up to two months before departure, instead of one month. Check the booking screen for the exact opening time of your travel date.

Can I book KTX tickets with a foreign credit card?

Yes. Korail's English booking page accepts overseas Visa, Mastercard and UnionPay cards, according to the Korea Times. If a payment fails, try the app or a different card, and ask your card issuer about online verification.

Do I need to print my KTX ticket?

No. Guides for visitors report that the Korail app and website issue a QR e-ticket that you can show on your phone. Take a screenshot as a backup and check the Korail page for printing or station collection.

Is the Korail Pass worth it for visitors?

It can be if you plan several long-distance rides in a short time. VisitKorea lists 2-, 3-, 4- and 5-day passes usable within ten days, with up to two seat reservations a day. Compare the total with single fares first.

What happens if I miss my KTX train?

Rules depend on the ticket and the delay, so read the refund and change terms shown at purchase and on Korail's official page. Do not assume you can use a later train on the same ticket.
