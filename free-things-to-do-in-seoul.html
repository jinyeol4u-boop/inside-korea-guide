25 Free Things to Do in Seoul

By Dragon Kim · Updated October 20, 2026 · 8 min read

Quick answer

Seoul offers at least 25 free sights, from Bukchon and Cheonggyecheon to the Han River parks and museum permanent collections. Palaces usually charge, though Gyeongbokgung is free in hanbok. Budget only for transit and food, and confirm hours and free-day rules on the linked official pages before you go.

What should I know before I arrive?

Sources checked October 20, 2026

"Free" in Seoul often means a free core with paid extras, so check each place before you build a day around it. The list below draws on the independent Seoul Tourism Guide page, last updated September 25, 2026, plus an Seoul Tourism Organization newsletter and official tourism pages. That guide itself notes that some entries charge for extras such as palace tickets, bike rental and special exhibitions.

  • Worth knowing: Culture Day falls on the last Wednesday of each month, when palaces and national museums have traditionally been free. Korean press reported in May 2026 that royal tombs became free every Wednesday and that palaces would follow in phases, so confirm the current rule on the royal palaces site.
  • A common mistake: Arriving on a closing day. Many Seoul museums close on Mondays and some palaces close on a weekday, so check the weekly closure.
  • Local tip: Load a T-money card first, because the free sights are spread across the city and subway fares add up. See our Seoul subway guide.

Which palaces and temples can I visit for free?

Six heritage sites are free or have a free route: Bukchon, two temples, a palace ceremony, Gyeongbokgung in hanbok and traditional music at Namsangol. Hanbok entry only works with a full outfit; see our hanbok rental guide.

  1. Gyeongbokgung Palace in hanbok. Admission is 3,000 won, but visitors in a complete hanbok enter free. Details are in our Gyeongbokgung guide.
  2. Deoksugung guard ceremony. The Seoul Tourism guide lists the ceremony at about 11:00, 14:00 and 15:30, not on Mondays. Watching it is free; confirm times on the royal palaces site.
  3. Bukchon Hanok Village. Free to walk, but tourist visits are limited to 10:00 to 17:00 in the red zone. Read our Bukchon rules first, and see the official Visit Seoul page.
  4. Jogyesa Temple. The head temple of Korean Seon Buddhism is listed as free year-round.
  5. Bongeunsa Temple. Free entry, across from COEX in Gangnam, with a large Maitreya Buddha statue.
  6. Namsangol Hanok Village music. Traditional gugak performances are listed as free; check the visitor center for the schedule.

What are the best free parks, walks and views?

Seven outdoor options give you hours of free sightseeing: Cheonggyecheon, the Han River parks, the Banpo fountain, Seoullo 7017, Namsan, Seoul Forest and Bukhansan.

  1. Cheonggyecheon Stream. A restored 5.84 km downtown stream walk from Cheonggye Plaza at Gwanghwamun Exit 5. See our Cheonggyecheon walk and the Seoul Facilities Corporation page.
  2. Han River Parks. Eleven riverside parks including Yeouido, Banpo and Ttukseom are listed as free; bike rental is about 3,000 won an hour.
  3. Banpo Bridge Rainbow Fountain. A free water, light and music show from April to October, about 20 minutes each. The Seoul Tourism Organization newsletter gives 19:30 to 21:00 in spring and autumn, with a later last show in July and August.
  4. Seoullo 7017 Skygarden. An elevated garden walkway on a former overpass near Seoul Station, listed as free.
  5. Namsan trails and the N Seoul Tower base. Hiking and skyline views are free; the observation deck is ticketed. Our N Seoul Tower guide covers the free forest trail.
  6. Seoul Forest. A large park with wetlands and a deer enclosure. It pairs well with our Seongsu-dong guide.
  7. Bukhansan National Park. Granite peaks including Baegundae (836 m) with free trail access, a subway-and-bus ride from central Seoul.

Which free neighborhoods and street scenes should I add?

Six neighborhood stops are free to wander: Ihwa Mural Village, Ikseon-dong, Hongdae, the Starfield Library, DDP and Gwangjang Market. You will pay only if you shop or eat.

  1. Ihwa Mural Village. A street-art hillside by Naksan, reached from Hyehwa Station (Line 4) Exit 2. It is a residential area, so keep your voice down.
  2. Ikseon-dong Hanok Alley. Narrow lanes of hanok houses turned into cafes and shops. Browsing is free.
  3. Hongdae street performances. Listed as free on Friday and Saturday evenings near the Hongdae playground area.
  4. Starfield Library at COEX. A free public library with towering shelves in the mall; the guide gives roughly 10:30 to 22:00.
  5. Dongdaemun Design Plaza. The exterior of the Zaha Hadid-designed building is free to see; interior exhibitions vary.
  6. Gwangjang Market. Free to stroll; you pay only for food. See our Gwangjang Market guide.

The Seoul Tourism guide names about 30 free options in total, including Namdaemun Market and Changdeokgung on Culture Day, if you need more for a longer stay.

How do I plan a free day in Seoul?

Group sights by area, start early at the palaces and finish with a river or night walk. A simple central route looks like this.

  1. Morning: palace and Bukchon. Enter Gyeongbokgung in hanbok, then walk to Bukchon before the 17:00 cut-off.
  2. Midday: museum and market. Visit the National Museum of Korea or MMCA Seoul, then lunch at Gwangjang Market.
  3. Afternoon: stream walk. Follow Cheonggyecheon from Cheonggye Plaza.
  4. Evening: river or view. Choose Banpo Bridge (April to October) or the Namsan trail.

FAQ

Is it really free to see Seoul's palaces?

Not always. Gyeongbokgung charges 3,000 won but is free if you wear a full hanbok, and several palaces are free on Culture Day, the last Wednesday of each month. Korean press reports a phased move to free Wednesdays in 2026, so check the royal palaces site.

Are Seoul's big museums free?

The permanent collections of the National Museum of Korea and several city museums, including the Seoul Museum of History and the War Memorial of Korea, are listed as free. Special exhibitions usually charge, so confirm on each museum's site.

What can I do in Seoul for free at night?

Walk Cheonggyecheon or the Han River parks, watch the Banpo Bridge Rainbow Fountain from April to October, or catch Hongdae street performances on Friday and Saturday evenings. Check show times first.

Do I still need money for a free day in Seoul?

Yes, a little. Entry may be free, but subway fares, food, bike rental (about 3,000 won an hour on the Han River) and special exhibitions are not. Load a T-money card before you start.
