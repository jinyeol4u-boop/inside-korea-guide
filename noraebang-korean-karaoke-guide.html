Noraebang: How Korean Karaoke Works for Visitors

By Dragon Kim · Updated November 10, 2026 · 7 min read

Quick answer

Noraebang means "song room": you rent a private, soundproofed room and sing with on-screen lyrics. Third-party guides list about 20,000 to 30,000 won per hour for a regular room and about 500 won per song in a coin noraebang. Choose songs with the remote, and pay at the counter or kiosk.

What should I know before I go to a noraebang?

Details checked November 10, 2026

A noraebang is a private singing room that your group rents, so nobody outside your group hears you. The Korean Ministry of Foreign Affairs describes the word as 노래 ("song") plus 방 ("room"), and says each group gets a soundproofed room that you can use to party with friends or release stress.

  • Worth knowing: Rooms vary in size and decor. The ministry page lists a sofa, a large TV screen, disco and neon lights and a stereo as typical features, and says some rooms can record your singing.
  • A common mistake: Assuming noraebang always means a bar. Many are plain singing rooms where you buy snacks and soft drinks, and a growing newer type, the coin noraebang, charges per song.
  • Local tip: It is a social activity, often at the end of an evening out. See our drinking etiquette guide and etiquette dos and don'ts for the wider context.

The format dates back to 1991, when a Busan arcade owner reportedly adapted a Japanese karaoke machine so users could type in a song number themselves, according to the Korea JoongAng Daily.

What types of noraebang are there?

The two types visitors meet most often are the regular, time-based room and the small pay-per-song coin room. The Ministry of Foreign Affairs page notes that coin noraebang rooms are smaller than traditional ones and cost less because you pay per song. A third-party guide also describes alcohol-serving venues and more expensive entertainment-style venues, which most visitors can skip.

How much does a noraebang cost?

Expect about 20,000 to 30,000 won per hour for a regular room and about 500 won per song in a coin room, but prices vary by venue and time of day. These figures come from a 2024 third-party guide, not an official price list, so treat them as approximate and read the price board at the door.

  • Regular room: charged by the hour per room, so a bigger room for a larger group costs more, per the same guide.
  • Coin room: the guide gives an example of about 2,000 won for 5 songs, with an hourly option at some places.
  • Time of day: the guide suggests late morning or early afternoon to avoid peak-period rates, and says some venues add 5 to 30 minutes of free "service" time (not at coin rooms).
  • Payment: pay upfront. A June 2026 guide says most coin rooms have self-service kiosks that take coins, bills or cards.

How do I sing, step by step?

Pay, find your room, pick songs on the remote and press start; everything else is optional. The June 2026 guide and the 2024 guide agree on the basic flow:

  1. Pay first. At a staffed venue, tell the counter how many people and how long. At a coin room, use the kiosk and choose a number of songs or a time.
  2. Take a microphone cover. Most venues provide free covers at the entrance, and the guide recommends using one for hygiene. You may need to remove your shoes in some rooms.
  3. Find your song. Type the song number on the keypad, or press the title or singer button to search on screen. English keyboards are supported.
  4. Change the language list. The guide says a country button lists pop, J-pop and Chinese songs, and the back of the songbook shows international charts.
  5. Adjust and play. Use the pitch or tempo buttons to suit your voice, and the cancel button if you pick the wrong song. A button labeled Yeyak (reserve) queues a song and Sijak starts it, according to the 2024 guide.

The score at the end is a measure of accuracy, not talent, and the 2024 guide says you can turn it off.

What are the etiquette and house rules?

Share the microphone, queue a mix of songs and check age limits before you go. The Ministry of Foreign Affairs page gives these etiquette points:

  • Do not hog the mic. Pass it on after your song.
  • Do not queue endless songs. Give others a turn.
  • Mix your list. If you are the only Korean speaker, include songs others know.
  • Age limits: the 2024 guide says people under 19 are not allowed in after 10 pm, even with an adult, and alcohol-serving venues may check ID or a passport. Venue rules can differ, so ask at the counter.

Where can I try one in Seoul?

Noraebang are found in every neighborhood, and one VisitKorea-listed venue in Hongdae is open 24 hours. The VisitKorea page lists Luxury Su Noraebang Hongdae at 67 Eoulmadang-ro, Mapo-gu, open 24 hours, with several room types across a two-story building. It does not list prices, so call or ask at the door. For nearby sights, see our K-pop fan guide, which covers Hongdae busking, and Seoul at night.

FAQ

How much does a noraebang cost?

Prices vary by venue and are not set by an official source. A third-party guide published in 2024 lists about 20,000 to 30,000 won per hour for a regular room and about 500 won per song in a coin noraebang. Check the price board at the entrance before you pay.

Can I use a noraebang if I do not speak Korean?

Yes. The remote lets you search by title or singer on screen, and English keyboards are supported, so you can find pop songs by typing the English title. Many coin rooms also use a self-service kiosk, so you can pay without talking to staff.

Do I have to drink alcohol at a noraebang?

No. A regular noraebang is mainly a singing room, and a third-party guide says that type does not sell alcohol, though you can buy other drinks and snacks. Separate alcohol-serving venues exist; they restrict entry to adults aged 19 and over and may check ID.

Is a coin noraebang better for solo travelers?

Usually yes. Coin noraebang rooms are smaller and you pay per song, which suits one or two people. Larger groups tend to find a regular room with a time-based rate better value.

Can children go to a noraebang?

Yes, but with limits. One third-party guide says people under 19 are not allowed in after 10 pm, even with an adult, so families are better off going earlier in the day. Confirm the venue's own rules at the counter.
