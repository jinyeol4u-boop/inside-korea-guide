One Week in Korea Without Rushing: Seoul and Jeju

By Dragon Kim · Updated November 8, 2026 · 7 min read

Quick answer

Spend three nights in Seoul, fly from Gimpo to Jeju on the morning of day 4, and keep one region of Jeju per day. Gimpo to Jeju is called the world’s busiest air route, so book early. Plan around closures: Gyeongbokgung on Tuesdays, Changdeokgung on Mondays, and Seongsan Ilchulbong on the first Monday of the month.

What should I know before I plan this week?

Details checked November 8, 2026

Treat the flight as the only fixed point and build each half of the week around closing days. VisitKorea: Domestic Flights says the Gimpo to Jeju section “has been selected as the busiest route in the entire world” and notes that flight routes can change without prior notice. This is a plan built from official listings and our other guides, not a record of a trip.

  • Worth knowing: The VisitKorea pages we read do not list a Gimpo to Jeju flight time or identification rules for domestic flights. Check your airline for both before you book.
  • A common mistake: Packing Jeju into one day per sight. Jeju is large, so pick one region per day (see our East vs West Jeju guide).
  • Local tip: Jeju has no rail line; buses, shuttles and taxis do the work, and local buses can be sparse (see Jeju without a car).

How should I split seven days between Seoul and Jeju?

Use three nights in Seoul, three nights on Jeju and a departure day, with the flight on the morning of day 4. This keeps travel to a single morning and leaves a buffer on the last day.

What should I do in Seoul on days 1 to 3?

Do the palaces on days 1 and 2, then leave day 3 open so the week starts slowly. The full route, with station exits, is in our Seoul in 3 Days guide.

  1. Day 1: Gyeongbokgung, Bukchon, Insa-dong, Gwangjang Market. Visit Seoul: Gyeongbokgung lists the palace as closed on Tuesdays, with adult entry for foreigners at 3,000 won. Visit Seoul: Bukchon lists a 10:00 to 17:00 visiting window, and the Food Alley at Visit Seoul: Gwangjang Market is listed 09:00 to 23:00 daily.
  2. Day 2: Changdeokgung, DDP, Namsan. Visit Seoul: Changdeokgung lists it as closed on Mondays; book the Secret Garden tour ahead via our Changdeokgung guide. Visit Seoul: DDP lists 10:00 to 20:00, with hours varying by space.
  3. Day 3: Keep it open. Choose one area, such as Ikseon-dong or Seongsu-dong, and pack for a morning flight.

Practical example: a traveller who lands on a Thursday does Gyeongbokgung on Friday and Changdeokgung on Saturday, which avoids both closing days, and flies on Monday.

How do I get from Seoul to Jeju on day 4?

Take the airport rail or subway to Gimpo Airport and fly to Jeju International Airport. VisitKorea: Airport Transportation Guide and our Gimpo Airport guide list AREX all-stop trains at about 23 minutes between Seoul Station and Gimpo. VisitKorea: Domestic Flights lists airlines at Gimpo including Korean Air, Asiana, Jeju Air, Jin Air, Air Busan and Air Seoul.

  1. Book the flight early. Compare airlines and confirm baggage rules and ID requirements with them.
  2. Reach Gimpo with a buffer. Allow extra time on a morning with a high-traffic commute; the official pages give no check-in deadline.
  3. Arrive and move on. VisitKorea: Fly to Jeju International Airport says buses leave from the first-floor stops for Jeju City, Seogwipo, major beaches and Jungmun, and that local buses accept transportation cards.

What should I do on Jeju on days 4 to 7?

Keep day 4 light near the airport, do the east coast on day 5, and use day 6 for Seogwipo or a Hallasan trail. Our Jeju 3-day itinerary has the full stop-by-stop version.

  1. Day 4 afternoon: Yongduam Rock and Dongmun Market. Visit Jeju: Yongduam Rock lists the rock as open at all times and free to view, from the designated trail only. Visit Jeju: Traditional Permanent Markets in Jeju City gives the market address as 16 Dongmun-ro and lists no hours, so confirm locally.
  2. Day 5: Manjanggul and Seongsan Ilchulbong. Visit Jeju: Manjanggul Lava Tube lists 09:00 to 18:00 with ticket sales ending at 17:00. Visit Jeju: Seongsan Ilchulbong says the summit climb takes about 30 to 40 minutes, and VisitKorea: Seongsan Ilchulbong lists 5,000 won for adults. From November to February it is listed 06:00 to 18:00.
  3. Day 6: Seogwipo. Visit Jeju: Oedolgae Rock lists the sea stack as open 24 hours; Visit Jeju: Cheonjiyeon Falls lists 09:00 to 22:00 with last admission at 21:20.
  4. Day 6 option: Hallasan. Visit Jeju: Hallasan National Park says only the Baengnokdam summit needs an advance reservation; the Eorimok, Yeongsil and Donnaeko trails do not. Visit Jeju: Hiking Hallasan Mountain describes Yeongsil as the shortest at 5.8 km, about 2.5 hours, on a page dated 2019.
  5. Day 7: Slow morning. Stay near the airport and keep a few hours of buffer before your flight. VisitKorea says domestic routes link Jeju with Seoul, Incheon and Busan.

Practical example: a couple staying in Jeju City on days 4 to 7 can use express buses for the east coast and a taxi back, as described in our no-car guide, which cites Visit Jeju: Public Transportation in Jeju.

Which closing days and fees should I check?

Check the Seoul palace weekdays and Jeju’s monthly closures before you fix the order of days. In December 2026 the first Monday is the 7th and the first Wednesday is the 2nd, so a trip then should move Seongsan and Manjanggul around those dates.

FAQ

How many days should I spend in Seoul and on Jeju in one week?

Three nights in Seoul and three nights on Jeju, plus a departure day, is a relaxed split. It gives Seoul one palace per day and Jeju one region per day, with the flight on the morning of day 4.

How do I get from Seoul to Jeju?

Fly from Gimpo International Airport to Jeju International Airport. VisitKorea calls the Gimpo to Jeju section the busiest route in the world and lists many airlines at Gimpo. The page gives no flight time, so check your airline.

How do I get from Jeju Airport to the sights?

Buses leave from the first-floor stops. VisitKorea's Jeju airport guide lists about 20 minutes by bus to Dongmun Market and about 120 minutes to Seongsan Ilchulbong, with faster times by taxi.

Do I need a rental car in Jeju?

Not strictly. Express buses, the 810 and 820 tourist shuttles and taxis cover the main sights, but local buses can be infrequent. VisitKorea suggests a car for the west coast and inland areas.

Which closing days should I plan around?

Gyeongbokgung is listed as closed on Tuesdays and Changdeokgung on Mondays. On Jeju, Seongsan Ilchulbong closes on the first Monday of each month and Manjanggul on the first Wednesday, which in December 2026 are the 7th and the 2nd.
