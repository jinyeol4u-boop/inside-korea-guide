Jagalchi Fish Market: How to Buy and Eat Seafood in Busan

By Dragon Kim · Updated October 30, 2026 · 7 min read

Quick answer

Jagalchi Market in Busan lists 5:00 to 22:00 hours and closes on the first and third Tuesdays, according to VisitKorea. Ride Busan Subway Line 1 to Jagalchi Station, choose live seafood from the first-floor tanks, then have it prepared at a second-floor restaurant. Confirm every price and any cooking or seating fee before you order.

What should I know before I arrive?

Details checked October 30, 2026

Jagalchi is a working fish market, so expect wet floors, loud sellers and prices that are negotiated rather than posted. VisitKorea describes it as one of Korea's largest seafood markets, selling live and dried fish, and says most vendors are women known as "Jagalchi Ajumeoni." The address is 52 Jagalchihaean-ro, Jung-gu, Busan, beside Nampo Port.

  • Worth knowing: Visitors can eat raw fish at the market itself. VisitKorea lists hoe (raw fish dishes), fresh fish and dried fish as the specialties, and notes a tourist information center, rest area and rooftop sky park.
  • A common mistake: Pointing at a fish and walking away without agreeing the price. Visitor guides advise asking the seller for the price first, and prices depend on species, size and the day's market rate.
  • Local tip: Search for it as 자갈치시장 in Naver Map or Kakao Map. Our Naver Map guide shows how to search by Korean name.

Wear closed shoes you do not mind getting wet, and bring a card plus some cash, since small stalls may prefer one or the other. See our cash or card guide for what to expect.

How do I get to Jagalchi Market by subway?

Take Busan Subway Line 1 to Jagalchi Station; the market is roughly a 5-minute walk from there. Busan's metro is run by the Busan Transportation Corporation, and our Busan Metro guide covers fares and payment.

  1. Ride Line 1 to Jagalchi Station. It is in the Nampo area of Jung-gu, close to the harbor.
  2. Use the exit signs. One visitor guide names Exit 10, then says to pass the Hanaro Mart agricultural cooperative store, turn left and cross the one-way street used by local buses. Exit numbers can change, so follow the signs and your map app.
  3. Head for the gull-shaped roof. The same guide describes the market building by its distinctive roof, which you reach on foot from the street.

Parking is listed as available by VisitKorea, but the area is busy, so the subway is the easier choice for most visitors. These walking details come from a visitor guide rather than an official timetable, so verify them on the day.

How do I buy fresh seafood at Jagalchi Market?

Pick from the live tanks on the first floor, ask the price, and agree it before the seller prepares anything. Visitor guides describe live fish and shellfish displayed in tanks, with prices that vary by species, size and daily rate, and some negotiation is normal.

  1. Walk the whole row first. Compare a few stalls before choosing, because the displays look alike and prices can differ.
  2. Point and ask the price. Show the fish you want and use a translation app if needed. Ask for the total, not just a per-piece figure.
  3. Agree the portion. A whole fish can be more than one or two people can eat, so say how many people you are feeding.
  4. Ask where it will be eaten. The seller can usually direct you to a second-floor restaurant that prepares what you bought.

No reliable price list is published: seafood prices change daily and the sources checked do not give one. Treat any number you see in a blog post as a rough guide only.

How do I eat it on the second floor?

Take your purchase upstairs to a sashimi restaurant to be cut and served, or skip the tanks and order from the restaurant menu. Visitor guides confirm both routes. A guide for first-time visitors suggests ordering from the menu, which avoids having to judge fish and prices alone.

  • Bring-your-own seafood: Restaurants prepare what you bought as sashimi or another dish. Cooking, seating or side-dish charges may apply, so ask the total first.
  • Menu ordering: You do not have to buy fish downstairs. Ask what is included, such as side dishes and a soup.
  • Sauces: Visitor guides say locals dip raw fish in a sweet-and-spicy sauce of chili paste and vinegar, and soy sauce is also available.
  • Seating: One guide says most sashimi restaurants now use table seating rather than floor rooms.

Practical example: two travelers who want to try raw fish without a large platter can skip the tanks, ask a second-floor restaurant for its smallest set, confirm the total in writing on a phone calculator, and then decide whether to add a seafood stew. Raw seafood is not for everyone, so cooked options are worth asking about; our first-trip dishes guide lists other options.

What are the hours and closing days?

VisitKorea lists 5:00 to 22:00 daily and closure on the first and third Tuesdays, but other guides differ, so confirm before a special trip. The table compares what the sources say.

What else is nearby?

Nampo and the harbor make an easy half day, and Busan's other main sights are a short subway ride away. The market sits at the edge of Nampo Port, and VisitKorea lists a rooftop sky park among its facilities.

  • Sky park: Check the rooftop for views of the harbor, according to VisitKorea's list of amenities.

FAQ

What are the opening hours of Jagalchi Market?

VisitKorea lists 5:00 AM to 10:00 PM. Other visitor guides give shorter hours for the first-floor market and the second-floor restaurants, so treat the exact times as approximate and check before you go.

Which days is Jagalchi Market closed?

VisitKorea lists the first and third Tuesdays of the month as closing days. Some visitor guides name different Tuesdays, so avoid Tuesdays if you can or confirm the schedule with the market (+82-51-245-2594).

Can I eat raw fish without buying it first?

Yes. A visitor guide notes that you can order from the menu at a second-floor restaurant without buying fish downstairs. Ask for the price, the portion size and any extra charge before you order.

Do restaurants charge extra to prepare fish I bought?

They may. Visitor guides say cooking, seating or side-dish charges can apply when you bring seafood upstairs, but they give no reliable amounts. Ask the restaurant to state the total before it starts.

How do I get to Jagalchi Market by subway?

Take Busan Subway Line 1 to Jagalchi Station. Visitor guides describe the market as roughly a 5-minute walk, and one lists Exit 10 as the exit to use. Follow the exit signs and confirm in a map app.
