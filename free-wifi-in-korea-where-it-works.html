Free Wi-Fi in Korea: Where It Works and Where It Doesn't

By Dragon Kim · Updated October 20, 2026 · 7 min read

Quick answer

Free Wi-Fi in Korea is common at airports, subway and train stations, cafes and tourist areas, and Seoul runs a citywide network named SEOUL, with a secured SEOUL_Secure version. It is not guaranteed on trains, in the countryside or while walking, so keep an eSIM or SIM as a backup.

What should I know before I arrive?

Sources checked October 20, 2026

Treat free Wi-Fi as a bonus, not your only connection, because coverage and rules differ by place. VisitKorea says the free public Wi-Fi service is available in most public locations, including airports, subway stations, train stations and certain bus stops.

  • Worth knowing: Seoul uses one shared network name, SEOUL, so your phone can stay on the same network as you move between districts.
  • A common mistake: Doing banking or entering passport and card details on an open network. Seoul officials advise the secured option for those tasks.
  • Local tip: Download offline maps and translation packs while you are on a reliable connection, so a patchy signal does not stop you.

Where does free Wi-Fi work in Korea?

It is easiest to find at airports, subway and train stations, cafes, malls, public buildings and major tourist areas. VisitKorea describes Korea as a "Wi-Fi Heaven" where subway stations, coffee shops, malls, public facilities and most tourist areas offer free Wi-Fi.

Seoul's city government says its Kkachi On network is installed at 1,443 spots in indoor public facilities, and lists parks, trails, traditional markets, tourist spots, public offices and city buses among the public spaces it serves. Seoul's own pages carry no date for these figures, so treat them as indicative.

How do I connect to Seoul's free Wi-Fi?

Turn on Wi-Fi, choose SEOUL for the open network or SEOUL_Secure for the secured one, and follow the prompt. Details below come from Seoul Metropolitan Government pages and Korea.net; settings can change, so check the city's page if a step fails.

  1. Open Wi-Fi settings. Look for a network named SEOUL or SEOUL_Secure.
  2. Pick the open network for browsing. Korea.net says the ordinary SEOUL connection needs you to log in each time you connect.
  3. Pick the secured network for sensitive tasks. According to the city, SEOUL_Secure uses the ID and password "seoul".
  4. Adjust the phone if asked. The city says Android needs the EAP method set to PEAP with the CA certificate set to "Do not validate", and iPhones need you to trust the certificate.
  5. Reconnect automatically. Once set up, the city says your phone connects automatically at any Kkachi On location.

The nationwide public Wi-Fi sign-up information is listed by VisitKorea at wififree.kr, in Korean and English. That site could not be opened while this guide was prepared, so check it directly before relying on it.

Where does free Wi-Fi fall short?

Expect gaps on trains, outside cities and in crowded spots, and be careful what you do on any public network. Public networks are shared, so keep banking, ticket purchases and passport details for the secured network or your mobile data.

  • Logins: Open networks often show a landing page and ask you to log in every time, which can interrupt navigation apps.
  • Security: Seoul officials advise using SEOUL_Secure for financial transactions and personal information.
  • Movement: Wi-Fi hops between hotspots as you walk, so maps and ride-hailing apps work better on mobile data.
  • Verification codes: Some Korean apps send an SMS code. See our guide to getting a Korean phone number as a tourist.

When should I use a SIM or eSIM instead?

Use a SIM, eSIM or pocket Wi-Fi if you will rely on maps, taxis or translation outside hotels and cafes. VisitKorea notes that airport roaming centres sell prepaid USIM cards and rent Wi-Fi eggs, convenience stores sell USIMs, and an eSIM can be bought online and set up by scanning a QR code.

FAQ

Is there free Wi-Fi at Incheon Airport?

VisitKorea lists airports, including Incheon, among places with free public Wi-Fi. Network names and rules can change, so check the signs and the airport's official site on arrival.

What is the password for Seoul's free Wi-Fi?

Seoul's secured network, SEOUL_Secure, uses the ID and password "seoul", according to a Seoul Metropolitan Government page. The open SEOUL network asks you to log in on a landing page instead.

Is free public Wi-Fi in Korea safe for banking?

Treat any public network with caution. Seoul officials advise using the secured SEOUL_Secure network for financial transactions and personal information, and mobile data is safer still.

Do I need a SIM card if there is free Wi-Fi?

Usually yes for longer trips. Free Wi-Fi is not available everywhere, such as many trains and rural areas, and some apps need a connection while you are moving.
