Seongsu-dong Guide: Seoul's Cafe and Pop-up District

By Dragon Kim · Updated October 18, 2026 · 7 min read

Quick answer

Seongsu-dong is reached by Seoul Subway Line 2 to Seongsu Station, Exit 3. Plan about half a day. VisitKorea lists a 2.1 km course covering Seoul Forest, Daelim Changgo, the Cafe Street and Handmade Shoes Street. Go on a weekday afternoon, because pop-up queues grow on weekends.

What should I know before I arrive?

Sources checked October 18, 2026

Seongsu-dong is a working neighbourhood turned trend district, so expect crowds on weekends and shops that change from month to month. It sits in Seongdong-gu in eastern Seoul, close to Seoul Forest and the Han River.

  • Worth knowing: Pop-up stores are temporary. Ivisitkorea names recent examples but gives no current dates, so check the brand's own page before you travel.
  • A common mistake: Treating a venue list as permanent. A 2016 Time Out Seoul article is itself a decade old, and some businesses it mentions may have closed or moved.
  • Local tip: Leave time for Seoul Forest. The neighbourhood is mostly cafe-hopping, and the park gives your feet a rest.

What is Seongsu-dong and why do people visit?

It is a former factory district in Seongdong-gu that now mixes specialty cafes, galleries, small brands and temporary pop-up shops. Time Out Seoul described the area as semi-industrial, with shoe factories, car repair garages and printing houses. It reported that new apartment towers began going up from 2011 and that social enterprises and young designers moved in from about 2014.

VisitKorea picks four stops for the area, and the table lists them with the page's own descriptions.

How do I get to Seongsu-dong?

Ride Seoul Subway Line 2 to Seongsu Station and leave by Exit 3. Ivisitkorea and Seoul Tourism Guide (an independent publisher, not a government body) both name Exit 3, and the latter also lists Exit 4 and says the cafe area is within about ten minutes on foot.

  1. Take Line 2 (the green line) to Seongsu Station.
  2. Leave by Exit 3 and follow the small streets toward the cafes.
  3. Finish at Seoul Forest by walking on or taking the Bundang Line to Seoul Forest Station. Seoul Metropolitan Government's media hub names Exits 3 and 4 there (article dated 2017).

Moving between districts is easy once you know the lines. See our Seoul subway guide for tourists for fares and tickets.

What is a good walking route?

Start with coffee near the station, browse the pop-ups and shoe shops, then end in Seoul Forest. This order keeps the long walk for the end, when you can sit down on the lawns.

  1. Cafe Street and Daelim Changgo. Spend the first hour on coffee and the warehouse gallery cafe, which VisitKorea describes as set in a former rice mill.
  2. Side-street shops and pop-ups. Walk the lanes without a fixed plan. Pop-up listings change, so use what is open on the day.
  3. Handmade Shoes Street. Look in the shop windows. Time Out noted the area's shoe-making roots, and some made-to-order brands show only in their own showrooms.
  4. Seoul Forest. The park opened in June 2005, covers about 481,000 square metres and has five themed areas, according to the city media hub. Check the park's official site for current hours and programs.

If you are staying nearby, our Myeongdong vs Hongdae vs Insadong guide shows how Seongsu compares with Seoul's other visitor areas.

How do pop-up stores and queues work?

Pop-ups are short-run brand shops, so there is no permanent list; search for current ones just before you go. Ivisitkorea suggests searching Instagram for the tag #성수팝업 and lists past pop-ups from beauty and fashion brands. It also names permanent flagship spaces such as Dior, Tamburins and Gentle Monster.

  • Expect lines at weekends. Seoul Tourism Guide says popular pop-ups can mean waits of 30 to 60 minutes on Saturdays and Sundays; treat that as a guide, not a guarantee.
  • Some events need booking. The same guide says certain events use free timed reservations on Naver, which is in Korean. Confirm on the brand's own page.
  • Ask before photographing. Rules differ by shop, so check signs or ask staff.

When should I go and what will it cost?

Go on a weekday afternoon, and budget mainly for coffee and food, since walking the streets and Seoul Forest costs nothing. Seoul Tourism Guide gives approximate coffee prices of about 6,000 to 9,000 won and says many small shops close on Mondays, but it does not cite a source for those figures, so verify before you rely on them.

FAQ

How do I get to Seongsu-dong by subway?

Take Seoul Subway Line 2 to Seongsu Station and leave by Exit 3, which Ivisitkorea and Seoul Tourism Guide both name for the cafe area. Ttukseom Station on Line 2 is the alternative for the riverside, and Seoul Forest Station on the Bundang Line is next to the park.

Is Seongsu-dong free to visit?

Yes. Walking the streets costs nothing, and Seoul Forest is a public park. Cafes, restaurants and shops charge their own prices, and some pop-up events ask you to book a timed slot.

When is the best time to visit Seongsu-dong?

Weekday afternoons are generally calmer, while Saturdays and Sundays bring the longest queues at popular pop-ups. Opening hours vary by shop, so check each venue's own page or social media before you go.

What are Seongsu-dong pop-up stores?

They are temporary brand shops and exhibitions that change often, so there is no fixed list. Ivisitkorea suggests searching Instagram for the Korean tag #성수팝업 before your trip to find current events and dates.

Why is Seongsu-dong called a cafe district?

The area was a semi-industrial zone of shoe factories, garages and print shops. Time Out Seoul described young designers and social enterprises moving in from about 2014, and warehouse-style cafes followed.
