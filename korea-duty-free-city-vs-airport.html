Duty-Free Shopping in Korea: City vs Airport

By Dragon Kim · Updated November 11, 2026 · 7 min read

Quick answer

Downtown duty-free stores let you buy up to 60 days before departure, per Shilla's FAQ, then collect the goods at the airport after immigration with your passport and flight details. Airport shops let you buy and carry on directly. Both skip VAT, but nothing is delivered to your hotel.

What should I know before I shop duty-free in Korea?

Details checked November 11, 2026

Duty-free shops are for people who are leaving Korea, so you buy before you fly and receive the goods only on the way out. According to Shilla Duty Free's FAQ, only departing travelers can buy, the buyer must be the person traveling, and arriving passengers cannot use the shop.

  • Worth knowing: Customs duties and VAT are removed at the point of sale in duty-free shops, according to a HANI Seoul guide. Road shops such as drugstores and department stores charge VAT and refund part of it later.
  • A common mistake: Expecting to carry goods out of a downtown store. Duty-free items bought in the city are handed over at the airport, and Shilla says they cannot be picked up on arrival or couriered.
  • Local tip: Domestic flights do not count. Shilla's FAQ says travelers going to Jeju cannot use the shop because they remain in the country. Plan the tax refund route in our tax refund guide instead.

Downtown duty-free gives you time to browse and an airport handover, the airport shop gives you instant carry-on, and road shops give you a partial refund. The table summarizes what the sources below describe.

How does downtown buying and airport pickup work, step by step?

Buy with your passport, keep your order details, then collect at the departures pickup counter with your passport and flight details. The operators describe this flow:

  1. Register your passport. Shilla accepts the passport itself or a copy at purchase. Lotte Duty Free introduced a mobile passport service on June 20, 2023, so you can register once in its app and shop at the Myeong-dong main store and World Tower without carrying the passport, according to the Moodie Davitt Report.
  2. Enter the right passport number. Shilla warns that the number on the order must match the passport you carry when you leave, or the goods will not be released.
  3. Buy early enough. Shilla allows purchases up to 60 days before departure, and the deadline depends on the departure airport.
  4. Go to the pickup counter. After immigration, find the operator's counter. Hyundai Duty Free lists several Incheon locations, including 4F across from Gate 43 and counters after Immigration Desk 2, and it asks for your passport and boarding pass. Shilla's page asks for your passport and airline ticket and adds that Terminal 1 pickup is on the 4th floor across from Gates 43 and 45 and Terminal 2 is on the 2nd floor near Gate 225, with a note dated 2020, so confirm on site.
  5. Allow time. Shilla advises collecting at least 1 to 2 hours before departure, checking item names and quantities, and signing to confirm.

If your travel date changes, tell the operator. Shilla says changes made within 2 to 3 days of departure may prevent pickup, and unclaimed items can be collected on a later departure within a month or refunded.

When is the airport shop the better choice?

Choose the airport shop when you want to see items first, are short on time or are flying out of Incheon. The Asia Economy reported that Lotte Duty Free reopened at Incheon on April 17, 2026, with a DF1 section for cosmetics, perfume, liquor and tobacco across Terminal 1, Terminal 2 and the concourse. Hyundai Duty Free lists Incheon shops in both terminals open 06:30 to 21:30 and a downtown Trade Center store open 09:30 to 20:00 and Dongdaemun 11:00 to 22:00, per its shopping guide.

  • Liquids: A third-party Lotte pickup review (updated October 7, 2026) says liquids, gels and creams come in a sealed security bag that must stay unopened until your final international destination. Ask the cashier how your items will be sealed.
  • Pickup is the same shop: The same review describes Lotte's Terminal 1 pickup as the 4th floor opposite Gate 45 with a passport scan at a kiosk. It is one reviewer's account, so confirm on the day.
  • Connections: Plan the route to the terminal with our Incheon Airport guide and compare Terminal 1 vs Terminal 2.

Is duty-free different from a tax refund?

Yes: duty-free removes the tax at the register, while a tax refund gives part of the VAT back after you shop at a regular store. The HANI Seoul guide gives the immediate refund range as 15,000 to 1,000,000 won per receipt at participating stores, with the physical or mobile passport shown. For airport refunds, Incheon Airport says to request refund documents for purchases over 15,000 won, keep the receipt until departure and carry refund-eligible items on board rather than checking them.

  • Kiosks: Terminal 1 has a 24-hour kiosk near Gate 28 on the 3rd floor and Terminal 2 has one near Gate 253, according to the airport page.
  • Keep items unused: Customs can ask to see unopened and unused goods, so do not open refund-eligible items before departure.
  • More detail: See our tax refund guide and the Olive Young guide for drugstore purchases.

What are the limits on liquor, perfume and tobacco?

Korea's allowance for goods carried in is USD 800 in total, with 2 bottles of liquor, 1 carton of cigarettes and a perfume limit that sources list differently. Incheon Airport lists USD 800, liquor of 2 bottles totaling 2 litres or less and valued under USD 400, 200 cigarettes and 100 ml of perfume. Shilla's FAQ lists perfume at 60 ml, so treat the perfume figure as unconfirmed and check with the Korea Customs Service.

  • Liquor rules may change: KED Global reported on December 23, 2024 that the government planned to drop the two-bottle cap while keeping the 2 litre and USD 400 limits. The article gives no effective date, so confirm the current rule.
  • Your home customs: These are Korea's entry rules. What you may bring into your own country is a separate rule set, so check it before you buy liquor or perfume.
  • Declare when unsure: The airport page says failing to declare can lead to confiscation, fines or imprisonment. Our customs rules guide covers the rest.

FAQ

When can I buy duty-free goods in Korea?

Shilla's FAQ says purchases can be made up to 60 days before departure, and that the deadline varies by departure airport, so check the operator's page. Only travelers leaving the country can buy, and the buyer must be the person departing.

Can I get duty-free purchases delivered to my hotel?

No. Shilla's FAQ says items are collected at the designated pickup counter in the departures area after immigration, and cannot be picked up on arrival or delivered by courier.

Is duty-free cheaper than a tax refund?

Not always. Duty-free shops remove customs duties and VAT at the point of sale, while road shops charge VAT and refund part of it later. Prices vary by brand and promotion, so compare the same item before you buy.

What do I need to pick up my purchases at Incheon Airport?

Your passport and your flight details. Shilla asks for the passport and airline ticket, and Hyundai Duty Free asks for the passport and boarding pass. Shilla advises collecting at least 1 to 2 hours before departure.

How much alcohol can I bring into Korea duty-free?

Incheon Airport lists 2 bottles totaling 2 litres or less and valued under USD 400, within a USD 800 total allowance. A December 2024 plan reported by KED Global would drop the two-bottle cap, so check the Korea Customs Service before you buy.
