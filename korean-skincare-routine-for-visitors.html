Korean Skincare Routine Explained for Visitors

By Dragon Kim · Updated November 21, 2026 · 7 min read

Quick answer

Three to five steps cover a real Korean skincare routine: cleanse, hydrate, moisturize and apply sunscreen every morning. The famous 10-step version is optional, and one dermatologist calls it good marketing. Visitors should buy sunscreen, a gentle cleanser and a moisturizer first, and patch test anything new.

What should I know before I arrive?

Details checked November 21, 2026

You do not need to arrive with a full routine in mind: Korean shops sell products by step, and a short routine is enough. Plan your shopping around a few daily products rather than a shelf of extras.

  • Worth knowing: Products are usually grouped by function (cleansers, toners, essences, creams, sun care), so you can build a routine by walking one aisle at a time.
  • A common mistake: Buying every step at once. Cleveland Clinic quotes a dermatologist who says people given a 10-step routine tend to do only two or three steps reliably.
  • Local tip: Tourists can often claim a tax refund on larger purchases; see our tax refund guide and Olive Young guide for how that works.

What are the steps of a Korean skincare routine?

A practical routine has four jobs: clean the skin, add water-based hydration, seal it with moisturizer, and protect it from the sun in the morning. Creatrip lists eight steps (cleanser, toner, ampoule/serum/essence, eye cream, lotion, moisturizer or cream, face oil and sunscreen) but notes you do not have to follow every one, and that some are crucial.

  • Double cleansing: Creatrip describes cleansing as a two-step process, oil or balm first and a face wash second, and says low-pH washes are suggested for mornings.
  • Evening versus morning: Cleveland Clinic's simpler plan runs cleanser, toner, serum or essence and a thin moisturizer in the morning, with sunscreen last. At night it uses the same steps and ends with a thicker moisturizer.

Do I really need ten steps?

No: the dermatologist quoted by Cleveland Clinic says the focus should be the philosophy of replenishing and respecting skin, not the number of steps. He describes the 10-step routine as brilliant from a marketing standpoint.

The 10-step list usually starts with an oil cleanser and a water cleanser, then adds an exfoliant once or twice a week, toner, essence, serum or ampoule, a sheet mask that is not for every day, eye cream, moisturizer and sunscreen. Practical example: a visitor staying one week can pack a cleanser, a moisturizer and a sunscreen, and add a sheet mask or an essence only as a souvenir treat.

Why is sunscreen the key step?

Sunscreen is the step both sources stress: Creatrip calls it crucial and says to wear it daily, even on cloudy days or in winter. Cleveland Clinic's advice is simply to always finish with sunscreen.

  • Read the label: MFDS says reports on sun products include SPF and PA data, so look for both ratings on the package.
  • Reapplying and removal: Sunscreen is why many people double cleanse in the evening; oil or balm cleansers are used to lift it.
  • Winter days: The advice to wear it year-round applies to a Korean November or January trip too.

How are cosmetics regulated in Korea?

Korea's Ministry of Food and Drug Safety (MFDS) requires makers and importers of functional cosmetics to be evaluated by it or to submit a report before selling. Its page lists functional categories including skin whitening, wrinkle improvement, protecting skin from ultraviolet rays, easing acne breakouts and easing dryness in atopic-prone skin.

The US International Trade Administration's country commercial guide adds that MFDS reviews only functional cosmetics before sale, while regular cosmetics go through an import permission process handled by the Korea Pharmaceutical Traders Association. This guide does not make medical claims: a functional label describes a regulatory category, not a treatment, so ask a doctor about skin conditions.

What should I buy first in Korea?

Start with the three daily products: sunscreen, a gentle cleanser and a moisturizer, then add one hydrating essence or a few sheet masks if there is room in your bag. This is a suggested approach based on the sources above, not a brand recommendation.

  1. Pick a store type: Browse a drugstore-style chain such as the one in our Olive Young guide or the shops in Myeongdong.
  2. Read the label: Check the SPF and PA ratings, the ingredient list and the expiry information before paying.
  3. Compare prices: Compare unit prices across shops; large department stores and duty-free stores may cost more or less, see our duty-free guide.
  4. Keep your passport: You need it for tax refunds, as explained in our tax refund guide.
  5. Test before you commit: Try one new product at a time, starting with a patch test.

How do I try new products safely?

Patch test on your inner arm before using a product on your face, and add one new product at a time. Cleveland Clinic warns that you can still have an allergic reaction or intolerance to a product.

  • Sensitive skin: Be careful with new products, especially those with fragrance or preservatives.
  • Acne, rosacea or eczema: Choose and combine products carefully, and ask a pharmacist or doctor if unsure.
  • Flare-ups: Cleveland Clinic notes hypersensitive skin can be hard to calm once it flares up, so avoid trying many new things at once on a short trip.

FAQ

Is the 10-step Korean skincare routine necessary?

No. A dermatologist quoted by Cleveland Clinic calls the 10-step routine brilliant marketing and says people tend to keep up only two or three steps reliably. A short routine of cleanser, moisturizer and sunscreen covers the core.

What should I buy first in Korea for skincare?

Sunscreen, a gentle cleanser and a moisturizer are sensible first buys because they are daily steps. Check that the sunscreen label shows SPF and PA, and confirm prices in store, since they vary by brand and shop.

What is double cleansing?

It means removing sunscreen and makeup with an oil or balm cleanser first, then washing with a water-based face wash. Creatrip describes cleansing as this two-step process; skip the oil step on days you wear neither.

Are Korean skincare products safe for sensitive skin?

Not automatically. Cleveland Clinic advises patch testing on the inner arm, adding products slowly, and being careful with fragrance and preservatives. If you have acne, rosacea or eczema, choose products carefully and ask a pharmacist or doctor.

What does MFDS approval mean on Korean cosmetics?

The Ministry of Food and Drug Safety says functional cosmetics, such as those claiming whitening, wrinkle improvement or UV protection, must be evaluated or reported to it before sale. Regular cosmetics are not reviewed in the same way.
