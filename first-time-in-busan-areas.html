First Time in Busan: Haeundae, Seomyeon or Nampo?

By Dragon Kim · Updated October 5, 2026 · 7 min read

Quick answer

Choose Haeundae for the beach, Seomyeon for a central base with easy subway links, and Nampo for Jagalchi Market and the old harbor district. Busan Metro fares start at about 1,600 won with a T-money card, and Lines 1 and 2 cross at Seomyeon, so any base lets you reach the other two.

What should I know before I choose an area?

Details checked October 5, 2026

Busan is spread along the coast, so pick the area that matches your main reason for visiting rather than trying to see everything from one spot. Haeundae sits on the east side, Nampo on the old harbor in the south, and Seomyeon in the middle of the city.

  • Worth knowing: Haeundae is on Metro Line 2, Nampo and Jagalchi are on Line 1, and Seomyeon is the station where Lines 1 and 2 meet, according to the Busan metro guide.
  • A common mistake: Expecting to tap a foreign credit card at the subway gate. The same guide reports that contactless credit cards and international bank cards do not work at Busan metro gates, so carry a T-money card or cash.
  • Local tip: If you arrive by KTX, Busan Station connects directly to the metro, so you can ride to any of the three areas without going outside. See our KTX booking guide for the train side.

Is Haeundae the right area for you?

Haeundae suits travellers whose picture of Busan is a big-city beach holiday, and it is the strongest choice from late spring to early autumn. The VisitKorea page on Haeundae Beach describes a beach roughly 1.5 kilometres long with white sand and a shallow bay, free to enter, with Dongbaekseom Island and Busan Aquarium close by.

  • What is nearby: The Haeundae Special Tourist Zone also lists Dalmaji Hill, sea water hot springs and Centum City for shopping, and it notes year-round events including the Busan International Film Festival and the Haeundae Sunrise Festival.
  • Trade-off: It is on the eastern edge of the city, so the old markets and downtown sights are a longer ride away, and summer weekends are crowded.
  • Hotels: The beach page says accommodation ranges from luxury hotels to private guesthouses.

Practical example: a traveller with three nights who wants a morning swim in July and a dinner by the sea can stay in Haeundae, then spend one day trip on Metro Line 1 to Nampo.

Is Seomyeon the best central base?

For most first-timers who want flexibility, Seomyeon is the safest base because two metro lines cross there. It is a downtown shopping and dining district; the Visit Busan page on Seomyeon mentions the Seomyeon Underground Shopping Center, Jeonpo Cafe Street, department stores and a large cluster of clinics that draws medical-tourism visitors.

  • Best for: Short stays, mixed itineraries, shopping and evening food, and anyone who does not want to commit to either the beach or the markets.
  • Trade-off: It is a busy urban area without a beach, so you will ride Line 2 to Haeundae or Gwangan for sea views.
  • Tip: Compare hotel locations near Seomyeon and Jeonpo stations, since the commercial streets can be a short walk from the platforms.

Is Nampo worth staying in?

Choose Nampo if seafood, markets and the old harbour are the point of the trip, not an optional detour. Jagalchi Market is described by VisitKorea as one of Korea's largest seafood markets, open 5:00 to 22:00 daily and closed on the 1st and 3rd Tuesdays, so check the date before you go.

  • Walkable sights: BIFF Square is a 428-metre film district in Nampo-dong, and VisitKorea's market and hillside village course links Jagalchi, Gukje Market, Bupyeong (Kkangtong) Market, Bosu-dong Book Street and Gamcheon Culture Village.
  • Trade-off: There is no beach in the immediate area, and the neighbourhood is older and busier than Haeundae's resort streets.
  • Getting here: Nampo and Jagalchi stations are on Line 1, which also serves Busan Station and Seomyeon.

How do the three areas compare?

Haeundae wins for the beach, Seomyeon for convenience and Nampo for markets. Use the table to decide, then confirm hotel prices and opening hours on the official pages below.

If you only have two nights, pick Seomyeon and visit the other two on day trips. If you have four or more, splitting nights between Haeundae and Nampo is realistic by metro.

How do I get around Busan between them?

Use the Busan Metro with a T-money card; base fares are reported at about 1,600 won by card and 1,700 won in cash, with extra zones adding about 100 won each. Confirm current fares on the operator's site, because the figure comes from a secondary guide.

  1. Get a T-money card. Convenience stores such as GS25, CU and 7-Eleven sell them, and you reload at station machines or shops.
  2. Pick the line. Take Line 2 for Haeundae and Seomyeon, and Line 1 for Seomyeon, Nampo and Jagalchi.
  3. Transfer at Seomyeon. This is the interchange between the two lines when you travel between Haeundae and Nampo.
  4. Allow for hours. Trains run from roughly 05:30 to midnight, with some late trains reported on weekends, so check the last train before dinner in Nampo.

FAQ

Which area of Busan is best for a first-time visitor?

Seomyeon is the safest all-round base because Subway Lines 1 and 2 cross there, but choose Haeundae if beach time is your priority and Nampo if markets are.

Can I visit Haeundae, Seomyeon and Nampo on one trip without moving hotels?

Yes. All three sit on the Busan Metro, so you can stay in one area and reach the others by subway; Haeundae and Nampo are on different lines, joined at Seomyeon.

Is Haeundae Beach worth visiting outside summer?

Yes, for walks, the aquarium and cafes, but swimming is a summer activity. Haeundae also hosts events through the year, including the Haeundae Sunrise Festival.

Do foreign credit cards work on the Busan subway?

Not at the gates. A secondary guide reports that contactless credit cards and international bank cards are not accepted, so use a T-money card or a cash token.
