Korean Souvenirs Worth Buying: What to Pack and Skip

By Dragon Kim · Updated November 10, 2026 · 6 min read

Quick answer

The best Korean souvenirs are light and easy to pack: dried seaweed (gim), yuja tea, snacks, hanji paper crafts, name seals and K-beauty. Insadong is the main area for traditional gifts, and Ssamzi-gil is open daily 10:30 to 20:30. Check your home country's food rules before buying edibles.

What should I know before I buy souvenirs in Korea?

Hours and tips checked November 10, 2026

Buy small, flat and non-perishable items first, and leave fragile or restricted goods for last. A visitor guide on iVisitKorea groups popular gifts into food and drink, fashion, traditional crafts, beauty and K-pop goods, and points travellers to Insadong for traditional items.

  • Worth knowing: Shops that display Tax-Free Shopping signs can issue refund vouchers, which you claim at the tax counter when you leave Korea, according to the same guide.
  • A common mistake: Buying food in bulk without checking what your own country allows through customs. The rules at your destination decide, not Korea's.
  • Local tip: Korean clothing sizes can run small, so try things on before buying clothes as gifts.

For cosmetics specifically, see our Olive Young guide, and for the refund steps read our tax refund guide.

Which food and drink souvenirs are worth buying?

Dried seaweed, yuja tea and snacks are the easiest food gifts because they are light, cheap and widely sold. The iVisitKorea guide describes gim as inexpensive and lightweight, yuja (honey-preserved citron) as sold in grocery stores and tea shops, and ramyeon and snacks as available at marts and convenience stores.

  • Gim (dried seaweed): Flat packs slip into a suitcase and suit large groups of friends or coworkers.
  • Yuja tea: Stir a spoonful into hot water; no tea bag is needed, per the same guide.
  • Regional snacks: The guide names Jeonju Choco Pie, Gangwon-do gamzabbang, Busan seagull bread and Jeju hallabong products as local specialties.
  • Alcohol: Makgeolli and ginseng liquor are listed, and some shops offer sample bottles. Liquids and alcohol have airline and customs limits, so check both before buying.

Ginseng is a classic gift, sold as root, tea or other food forms, but it is often restricted by other countries. If you want ginseng, look up your home rules first. Korea's own arrival limits (for example 300 g of ginseng per item type) are listed on Incheon Airport customs information and apply to people entering Korea, not leaving.

Which traditional crafts make good gifts?

Hanji (mulberry-bark paper) items and name seals are the most distinctive traditional gifts, and Insadong is where most visitors look for them. The iVisitKorea guide lists hanji dolls, lamps and stationery, personalized engraved name seals (dojang) in wood or jade, tal masks and hand-carved wooden items, and suggests a hanji doll as a gift for children or a hanji crafting class.

VisitKorea describes Insa-dong as a place where old but precious traditional goods are on display and where shops are popular because each one is unique. Galleries, including Hakgojae Gallery, and teahouses sit along the alleys.

Practical example: a traveller with one empty corner of a suitcase can pick a hanji notebook, a small tea tin and a few gim packs in a single Insadong afternoon, for gifts that weigh almost nothing. Our traditional crafts guide covers hands-on workshops if you want to make something yourself.

Where do I buy souvenirs in Seoul?

Start at Insadong and Ssamzi-gil, then add Myeongdong or a convenience store for easy extras. Visit Seoul lists Ssamzi-gil at 44 Insadong-gil, Jongno-gu, open daily 10:30 to 20:30 (closed on New Year's Day and Chuseok), 245 m from Anguk Station Exit 6 on Line 3, and describes it as featuring both traditional and modern crafts.

On weekends the main street of Insadong is closed to vehicles. VisitKorea gives the pedestrian hours as Saturday 14:00 to 22:00 and Sunday 10:00 to 22:00, with outdoor booths and Korean candy merchants.

  1. Take Line 3 to Anguk Station Exit 6 and walk to Ssamzi-gil on Insadong-gil.
  2. Browse the craft shops and galleries first while you have energy and room in your bag.
  3. Walk the main street for tea, candy and small gifts, especially on a weekend afternoon.
  4. Finish at a mart or convenience store for gim, snacks and instant noodles, which are the cheapest items.
  5. Keep every receipt if you plan a tax refund, and see our Myeongdong shopping guide for cosmetics and fashion.

What should I check before I buy?

Confirm customs rules at home, weigh fragile items against your bag space, and ask for the tax-free voucher at the register.

  • Customs at home: Meat, plants, seeds, fresh produce and some processed foods are restricted in many countries. Check your own agency before buying.
  • Liquids: Alcohol, sauces and pastes may need checked luggage, so check your airline's limits.
  • Tax refund: Ask for the voucher at the shop and follow the steps in our tax refund guide.
  • Fragile items: Ceramics and masks need padding; hanji and gim pack flat.
  • Sizes: Try on clothes and glasses frames; sizes can run small.

FAQ

What are the best cheap souvenirs from Korea?

Dried seaweed (gim), instant noodles, snacks, yuja tea and novelty socks are lightweight and inexpensive. One visitor guide lists socks at about 1,000 to 2,000 won a pair, so confirm the price on the day.

Where can I buy traditional Korean souvenirs in Seoul?

Insadong is the usual answer. Visit Seoul lists Ssamzi-gil at 44 Insadong-gil, open daily 10:30 to 20:30, a short walk from Anguk Station Exit 6, and VisitKorea describes Insadong as a street of galleries, teahouses and unique shops.

Can I bring Korean food souvenirs home?

It depends on your home country's rules, not Korea's. Meat, fresh produce, ginseng and kimchi are often restricted, so check your own customs or agriculture agency before buying food in bulk.

Can I get a tax refund on souvenirs?

Often yes, if the shop is a tax-free shop and you meet the minimum purchase. Look for Tax-Free Shopping signs and read our tax refund guide for the current limits and steps.
