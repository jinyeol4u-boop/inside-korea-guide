What You Can't Bring into Korea: Customs Rules

By Dragon Kim · Updated October 16, 2026 · 7 min read

Quick answer

Korea bans narcotics, counterfeit goods, weapons and endangered-species products, and restricts raw fruit, meat, eggs and dairy. Goods above USD 800, alcohol above 2 litres, and cash above USD 10,000 must be declared. When in doubt, declare: it is faster than being caught.

What should I know before I arrive?

Rules checked October 16, 2026

Most visitors pass through customs with nothing to declare, but food and medicine are where travellers most often get caught out. Incheon Airport lists banned goods, quarantine items and duty-free limits on one page, and says its information is for reference only.

  • Worth knowing: One declaration form can cover a whole family, according to Incheon Airport.
  • A common mistake: Packing homemade or shop-bought meat snacks, such as jerky or sausage, in a suitcase without declaring them.
  • Local tip: Keep medicine in its original packaging and carry the prescription, so an officer can see what it is.

What is banned from entering Korea?

Narcotics, counterfeit goods, weapons, endangered-species products and material that harms national security or public morals are prohibited. Incheon Airport says prohibited items are confiscated and the traveller may face inspection and investigation.

  • Drugs: narcotics, psychotropic drugs and related products.
  • Weapons: firearms, ammunition, swords and radioactive materials.
  • Fakes: counterfeit or forged currency, bonds and securities, and counterfeit branded goods.
  • Restricted media and devices: books, photos, videos, films and discs that harm national security, public safety or public morals; obscene material; North Korea praise items; and eavesdropping devices.
  • Protected species: endangered wild animals and plants, plus products such as bear bile, musk and crocodile leather.

Practical example: a souvenir made from an endangered species, bought abroad, can be seized on arrival even if it was sold legally in the shop.

Can I bring food, meat and fruit?

Fresh fruit, vegetables, soil and plants in soil are largely barred, and meat, egg and dairy products must be declared for quarantine inspection. A Korean consular summary says most fresh fruit, vegetables, walnuts and plants in soil are prohibited under plant quarantine, as listed by the Korean consulate in Canada.

A Korean embassy page lists the animal products that need declaring: beef, pork, lamb and poultry; processed meat such as ham, sausage and jerky; eggs; and milk, cheese and butter. Declared items may be allowed in with an inspection certificate from an import-allowed country, according to the Korean embassy in Norway. The same page says heated or processed plant foods such as kimchi and rice cake are not subject to quarantine.

Incheon Airport also sets limits on agricultural, forestry and livestock goods bought abroad: up to 40 kg per person with a total purchase value of KRW 100,000 or less, and they must pass quarantine. Individual caps include 10 kg of beef, 5 kg of sesame oil or honey and 300 g of ginseng.

Can I bring medicine?

Personal-use medicine is generally accepted in limited quantities, but anything containing narcotics is prohibited, and rules for stronger drugs need checking before you fly. Incheon Airport lists narcotics and psychotropic drugs as prohibited and does not give detailed rules for ordinary prescription or over-the-counter medicine.

An older US Embassy information sheet on controlled substances says medicine containing narcotics, including CBD oil, is barred even with a prescription, and that other medicine is allowed for personal use in amounts such as a three-month supply, with some needing a permit. That sheet warns procedures can change, so confirm current rules with Korea Customs Service before travelling. Medicinal herbs fall under the agricultural limits above.

Practical example: a traveller on a regular ADHD or strong pain medication should contact customs and carry the original prescription and a doctor's letter, rather than assume it will be accepted.

What are the duty-free limits?

You can bring goods worth up to USD 800 duty-free, including up to 2 litres of alcohol, 200 cigarettes and 100 ml of perfume. Alcohol and tobacco are not duty-free for anyone under 19, and if an item goes over its limit, tax applies to the entire amount, according to Incheon Airport.

Older consular pages show lower figures, so use the airport's current numbers and check the Korea Customs Service if you carry anything near a limit. For what happens before you reach the customs lane, see our arrival guide.

How do I declare items?

Tick the matching box on the declaration form, hand it in at the declaration gate and use the declaration lane. Incheon Airport says you must declare goods over the limits, items subject to quarantine, and currency over USD 10,000.

  1. Check your bags. Look for food, medicine, cash and anything bought abroad above the limits.
  2. Complete the form. Mark the box for each category, such as animal products or plants.
  3. Visit quarantine if needed. Animal and plant items are inspected by quarantine officers, per the Korean embassy page.
  4. Pay any tax. Customs calculates duty on declared goods above the allowance.

The penalty for skipping this is steep: Incheon Airport cites extra tax of 40% or 50% of the tax payable, confiscation, and possible imprisonment of up to five years or fines. Buying souvenirs here? See our tax refund guide for the departure side.

FAQ

Can I bring kimchi or snacks into Korea?

Heated or processed plant foods such as rice cakes are described by a Korean government page as not subject to quarantine, but meat, egg and dairy snacks must be declared. When unsure, declare.

Can I bring prescription medicine into Korea?

Medicine for personal use is generally allowed in limited amounts, but anything containing narcotics is barred even with a prescription, according to a US Embassy sheet. Carry the original prescription and a doctor's letter, and check with Korea Customs Service first.

Do I have to declare cash at Korean customs?

Yes, if you carry currency, checks and securities worth more than USD 10,000 in total, according to Incheon Airport.

What happens if I do not declare something?

Incheon Airport says false or missing declarations can bring extra tax of 40% or 50% of the tax payable, goods can be confiscated, and serious cases can lead to fines or imprisonment of up to five years.
