Essential Apps for Traveling in Korea

By Dragon Kim · Updated October 1, 2026 · 7 min read

Quick answer

Install five apps: Naver Maps, Papago, k.ride or Kakao T, KorailTalk and Mobile Tmoney. In an official 2024 survey, 91.7% of foreign visitors used Korean apps during their stay, and 56.2% used Naver Map. Set them up on home Wi-Fi before you fly.

What should I know before I arrive?

App details checked October 1, 2026

Plan to use Korean apps rather than only the ones you use at home, because most visitors do. According to Korea.net, a March 2024 study by the Ministry of Culture, Sports and Tourism and the Korea Tourism Organization found that 91.7% of foreign tourists used Korean apps during their visit, with Naver Map used by 56.2% and the Papago translator by 48.3%.

  • Worth knowing: Several travel apps now allow sign-up without a Korean phone number, but rules differ by app and change over time, so check each app's sign-up screen.
  • A common mistake: Relying on a single international map app. The Korea Herald reported in August 2023 that Google Maps was missing walking directions and turn-by-turn navigation in Korea, so confirm its current state and keep a Korean map app as a backup.
  • Local tip: Install and test your apps on Wi-Fi at home. Account set-up and card registration are easier before you land.

Which map and translation apps do I need?

Install Naver Maps for routes and Papago for translation; these are the two most-used Korean apps among visitors. VisitKorea's essential apps list lists NAVER Maps for route navigation in Korean, English, simplified Chinese and Japanese.

  • Naver Maps: VisitKorea's Helpful Apps & Resources page calls it Korea's most popular mapping service, with route suggestions for walking, biking, driving and public transit, linked to Naver search for reviews, hours and business information.
  • Papago: The same page describes a translator supporting 14 languages that reads text from images, handwriting, webpages and speech, and retrieves food information from VISITKOREA when you scan Korean food names.
  • VISITKOREA app: The official tourism platform offers attraction and festival information, GPS services and suggested itineraries, available on Android and iOS.

Practical example: standing outside a restaurant with a Korean-only menu, you can scan the menu with Papago's camera, then open Naver Maps to check opening hours and reviews before you go in.

Which apps help with taxis, trains and buses?

Use k.ride or Kakao T for taxis, and KorailTalk or Let's Korail for trains. VisitKorea highlights k.ride as a taxi service that works "without complicated verification" and lists KorailTalk, Klook and GoHanpass for trains and express buses.

Which apps handle food, payment and emergencies?

Catchtable for restaurant bookings, Mobile Tmoney or WOWPASS for payments, and the Emergency Ready App for safety. All three appear on VisitKorea's Helpful Apps & Resources page.

  • Catchtable: A restaurant booking platform with real-time queue status and customer reviews, searchable by food type, price range and location.
  • WOWPASS: A prepaid card app combining a transport card and debit functions; funds are loaded at kiosks or via the app.
  • Emergency Ready App: A multilingual government guide with disaster alerts, shelter locations, medical centers, police and fire stations, embassy information and first-aid guidance.
  • 1330 Korea Travel Helpline: Multilingual phone support in Korean, English, Japanese, Chinese, Russian, Vietnamese, Thai, Malay and Indonesian.

How do I set up my apps before the flight?

Download a short core list at home, create accounts on Wi-Fi, and save the helpline number.

  1. Install the core five. Naver Maps, Papago, k.ride (or Kakao T), KorailTalk and Mobile Tmoney cover navigation, language, taxis, trains and transit payment.
  2. Create your accounts. Register your email, passport or card details while you still have home Wi-Fi, following each app's own sign-up screen.
  3. Set the language. Switch Naver Maps and Papago to English and download any offline language options the app offers.
  4. Save emergency details. Note the 1330 helpline and install the Emergency Ready App.
  5. Test on arrival. Check mobile data works, then run one route search and one translation to confirm everything loads.

FAQ

Do I need to download Korean apps before visiting Korea?

It helps. An official survey reported by Korea.net found that 91.7% of foreign tourists used Korean apps during their stay, and Naver Map was the most used at 56.2%. Install the main ones before your flight.

Which taxi app should a tourist use in Korea?

k.ride is built for visitors: VisitKorea says it accepts international credit cards and does not require a Kakao Talk signup. Kakao T is the most widely used taxi app and offers English.

Which app translates Korean menus and signs?

Papago. VisitKorea describes it as supporting 14 languages and recognizing text from images, handwriting, webpages and speech, and it can pull food information from VISITKOREA when you scan Korean food names.

Who can I call if I need help while travelling in Korea?

The 1330 Korea Travel Helpline offers multilingual phone support, and VisitKorea also lists the Emergency Ready App for alerts, shelters, hospitals and embassy information.
