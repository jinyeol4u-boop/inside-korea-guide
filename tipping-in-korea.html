Tipping in Korea: Do You Need To?

By Dragon Kim · Updated October 18, 2026 · 6 min read

Quick answer

No, you do not need to tip in Korea. Menu prices should already include tax, staff earn wages, and tipping is not customary at restaurants, cafes, taxis or hotels. Some upscale hotels and restaurants add a 10% service charge instead, so check the bill. If you see a tip jar, leaving nothing is fine.

What should I know before I arrive?

Information checked October 18, 2026

Plan to pay exactly what the menu, meter or bill says, because Korea runs on a no-tip system. The Seoul Tourism Organization’s visitor guide states that tipping is not a Korean custom and will not be expected by service personnel. That covers most situations a visitor meets in a normal trip.

  • Worth knowing: Large hotels and upscale restaurants may add a service charge to the bill. It is a built-in fee, not a tip you must top up.
  • A common mistake: Leaving cash on the table out of habit. It is unnecessary and may cause confusion.
  • Local tip: Check the total at the end of the meal. If a line already says service charge, you owe nothing more.

Do I need to tip in Korea?

No. Tipping is not required or expected in Korea, and not tipping is never rude. A Korea Times explainer describes tipping as not customary in Korea and notes that employees are paid at least the national minimum wage directly by their employers. Good service is treated as part of the job rather than something you pay extra for.

A voluntary tip handed directly to a person is not illegal, and the Korea Herald notes that a tip is purely out of personal gratitude. It is simply unusual. It has also been seen as a show of status, so a sincere “gamsahamnida” (thank you) is the usual way to show gratitude.

Practical example: after a 28,000 won meal at a local restaurant, you pay 28,000 won at the counter on your way out. There is nothing to calculate, and no percentage to add.

Where might a tip or service charge come up?

Almost nowhere, except a 10% service charge at some hotels and upscale restaurants and an optional tip at valet parking. Use the table as a quick reference and confirm the final bill each time.

The 10% service charge appears on the Seoul Tourism Organization guide, which says many large hotels and upscale restaurants add it in addition to 10% VAT. Because it is not shown at every business, ask or check the menu footnote if you are unsure.

In taxis, a tip function is not part of everyday rides. Kakao T added an optional tip function in July 2023, according to the Korea Herald, and polls it cited showed most respondents opposed it. The Korea Times, however, says the tipping feature was piloted and never formally launched, so reports differ. Treat it as optional at most.

What should I do if someone asks for a tip?

Decline politely and pay the listed price; you are not obliged to give anything extra. Requests are uncommon, but recent cases show they do happen.

  1. Look at the bill. Confirm whether a service charge is already included before you decide anything.
  2. Check any kiosk or screen. If an optional contribution is preselected, remove it before paying.
  3. Decline kindly. A smile and “no, thank you” is enough. Do not argue or feel pressured.
  4. Pay the listed total. If a charge looks wrong, ask the staff to explain it, and check official tourism information on VisitKorea.

The Korea Times reports several examples from 2025: a Seoul restaurant placed a tip container at checkout, a noodle restaurant added an optional 300 won contribution at a kiosk, and a pizzeria reportedly asked for 2,000 won before ordering. These cases drew public criticism, which suggests how far this is from normal practice.

Why is there no tipping culture in Korea?

Because staff are paid wages by their employers, service is expected as standard, and the law pushes businesses to show one final price. Together these make tips unnecessary.

  • Wages: Employees are paid at least the national minimum wage by employers, according to the Korea Times explainer.
  • Law: The 2013 Food Sanitation Act revision requires menu prices to include taxes and service charges, so mandatory fees on top are not allowed.
  • Public opinion: In the Korea Herald’s coverage of the 2023 taxi-app tip function, one survey found 71.7% opposed it and 17.2% supported it.

Practical example: if you want to thank a guesthouse host or a driver, a sincere thank-you or a positive online review is a safe alternative to cash. For more social rules, read our guides on Korean etiquette and ordering at a Korean restaurant.

FAQ

Do you tip in Korea?

No. Tipping is not a Korean custom and staff do not expect it. Pay the price on the menu or meter and say thank you.

Should I tip a taxi driver in Korea?

No tip is needed. Drivers do not expect one, though some riders let the driver keep small change as a courtesy. Pay the metered fare.

Is a service charge the same as a tip?

Not exactly. A service charge is a set percentage that some large hotels and upscale restaurants add to the bill, often 10% on top of 10% VAT. It is not a tip, so you do not need to add anything further.

What if a restaurant asks me for a tip?

You can politely decline. Korean law requires menu prices to include tax and service charges, and a request for extra fees has drawn public criticism. Voluntary tips are not illegal, but you are never required to give one.

Is it rude not to tip in Korea?

No. Not tipping is the normal behavior. Good service is seen as part of the price, so leaving nothing is not an insult.
