Busan in 3 Days: Beaches, Markets and Temples

By Dragon Kim · Updated October 17, 2026 · 7 min read

Quick answer

Three days covers Busan's core: Nampo markets on day 1, Gamcheon and Seomyeon on day 2, then Haedong Yonggungsa and Haeundae on day 3. Metro rides start near 1,600 won, and the temple and beach are free. Jagalchi Market closes on the 1st and 3rd Tuesdays.

What should I know before I arrive?

Details checked October 17, 2026

Group Busan's sights by metro line, because the city stretches along the coast and the best stops are far apart. Nampo and Jagalchi sit on Line 1, Seomyeon is where Lines 1 and 2 meet, and Haeundae is on Line 2, according to a Busan metro guide. This itinerary is a plan built from official listings, not a record of a trip.

  • Closing day to avoid: VisitKorea lists Jagalchi Market as closed on the 1st and 3rd Tuesdays of the month. In October 2026 those are October 6 and 20.
  • A common mistake: Expecting to tap a foreign credit card at the metro gate. The same guide reports that contactless credit cards and international bank cards are not accepted, so buy a T-money card (reported deposit about 2,500 won) or use cash.
  • Getting here: The KTX from Seoul takes roughly 2 hours 15 minutes for about 55,000 to 60,000 won each way. See our KTX booking guide, and note that Busan Station connects directly to Metro Line 1.
  • Where to sleep: Our Busan area guide compares Haeundae, Seomyeon and Nampo as bases.

What should I do on day 1?

Spend day one in Nampo and the old harbor markets, where Jagalchi, BIFF Square and Gukje Market are within walking range. VisitKorea's Marketplace and Hillside Village course links Jagalchi, Gukje Market, Bupyeong (Kkangtong) Market, Bosu-dong Book Street and Gamcheon Culture Village over about 3.45 km, and this plan uses the first half of it.

  1. Morning: Jagalchi Market. VisitKorea describes it as one of Korea's largest seafood markets, listed 05:00 to 22:00 daily except the 1st and 3rd Tuesdays (VisitKorea: Jagalchi Market). Take Line 1 to Jagalchi Station.
  2. Midday: Gukje Market, Kkangtong Market and Bosu-dong Book Street. The course lists all three in Jung-gu, so you can walk between them.
  3. Evening: BIFF Square. A 428-metre street in Nampo-dong with cinemas, shops and street food, described by VisitKorea as the heart of Busan's film district (VisitKorea: BIFF Square).

Practical example: a traveller arriving by KTX on a Monday morning can ride Line 1 from Busan Station, leave bags at the hotel and still reach Jagalchi before lunch, since the market opens at 05:00.

What should I do on day 2?

Use day two for Gamcheon Culture Village in the morning and Seomyeon's shopping streets in the afternoon. Gamcheon is the hillside village at the far end of the market course, and Seomyeon is the central interchange for Lines 1 and 2.

  1. Morning: Gamcheon Culture Village. A hillside village of pastel houses and wall paintings (VisitKorea: Gamcheon Culture Village). The Busan metro guide says to take Line 1 to Toseong Station, then a 10 to 15 minute bus up the hill, or a taxi. Check the official page for current visiting information, as hours and any fees were not confirmed for this guide.
  2. Afternoon: Seomyeon. Visit Busan mentions the Seomyeon Underground Shopping Center, Jeonpo Cafe Street and department stores, which makes this a good wet-weather block.
  3. Evening: free choice. Eat near your hotel, or go back to Nampo for street food if you skipped it on day one.

What should I do on day 3?

Finish on the east coast with Haedong Yonggungsa Temple in the morning and Haeundae Beach in the afternoon. The temple is far from the metro lines, so do it first while energy is high.

  1. Morning: Haedong Yonggungsa Temple. A seaside temple in Gijang, listed open 04:30 to 19:20 with free admission and last entry at 18:50 (VisitKorea: Haedong Yonggungsa). VisitKorea gives no public transit route, so check a map app or take a taxi.
  2. Afternoon: Haeundae Beach. About 1.5 km of white sand in a shallow bay, free to enter, with Dongbaekseom Island and Busan Aquarium nearby (VisitKorea: Haeundae Beach). On Line 2 it is reported as a five to eight minute walk from Haeundae Station.
  3. Late afternoon: Haeundae Special Tourist Zone. VisitKorea also lists Dalmaji Hill, sea water hot springs and Centum City for shopping (VisitKorea: Haeundae Special Tourist Zone).

How much will three days in Busan cost for transport and sights?

Budget about 1,600 won per basic metro ride, and expect the temple and beach to cost nothing. Figures below are approximate and several come from a secondary guide, so confirm them before you travel.

What if it rains or a stop is closed?

Swap days or move indoors rather than cutting a stop. The three days are independent, so you can reorder them.

  • Jagalchi closed: If day one lands on a 1st or 3rd Tuesday, do Gamcheon first and move Nampo to day two.
  • Rain: Seomyeon's underground shopping center and Haeundae's Busan Aquarium and Centum City are covered options listed by Visit Busan and VisitKorea.
  • Short on time: Drop Seomyeon, since it is the only stop that is a shopping block rather than a sight.
  • Different base: If you stay in Haeundae, do day three first and return by Line 2 to avoid a long evening commute.

FAQ

Is three days enough for Busan?

Yes for the main sights. Three days fits the Nampo markets, Gamcheon Culture Village, Seomyeon, Haedong Yonggungsa Temple and Haeundae Beach if you group stops by metro line and keep one coastal day for the temple and beach.

Which area of Busan should I stay in for three days?

Seomyeon is the safest all-round base because Metro Lines 1 and 2 cross there. Choose Haeundae for beach time or Nampo for markets; our Busan area guide compares all three.

Is Jagalchi Market open every day?

VisitKorea lists it as open 05:00 to 22:00 and closed on the 1st and 3rd Tuesdays of each month. In October 2026 that means October 6 and 20; in November 2026 it means November 3 and 17.

Can I use a foreign credit card on the Busan metro?

Not reliably. A secondary Busan metro guide reports that contactless credit and international bank cards do not work at the gates, so carry a T-money card or cash.

How do I get to Haedong Yonggungsa Temple?

VisitKorea lists the temple's hours and admission but gives no public transit route, so check a map app on the day or take a taxi. The listing shows it open 04:30 to 19:20 with free entry.
