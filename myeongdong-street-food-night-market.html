Myeongdong Street Food Night Market Guide

By Dragon Kim · Updated November 1, 2026 · 6 min read

Quick answer

Most snacks at Myeongdong's evening stalls cost about 2,000 to 6,000 won, and a full tasting dinner roughly 20,000 to 30,000 won. Stalls start filling the pedestrian streets around 3 to 4 p.m., peak after 5 p.m. and mostly close by 10 p.m. Exit 6 of Myeongdong Station (Line 4) is the easiest entry.

What should I know before I arrive?

Prices, hours and rules checked November 1, 2026

Myeongdong's street food is a tourist-focused evening scene, so expect crowds, higher prices than traditional markets and stalls that change from night to night. Visit Seoul lists the area as a mix of street stalls and retail shops, reached from Myeongdong Station on Line 4 (Exits 5 to 10), and notes that hours vary by store. The Korea Tourism Organization describes the wider Myeong-dong tourist zone as full of street food, restaurants and cafes.

  • Worth knowing: According to a private travel guide (Treksplorer), about 300 vendors are licensed but only roughly 100 to 150 appear on a given night, so a stall you read about may not be there.
  • A common mistake: Ordering without checking the price. Premium items such as lobster or large skewers cost far more than a 2,000 won egg bread, so confirm the amount first.
  • Local tip: Public bins are scarce, so carry a small bag for sticks and wrappers.

When and where do the stalls set up?

Stalls begin appearing around 3 to 4 p.m., reach full strength after about 5 p.m. and mostly pack up between 9 and 10 p.m. These times come from a private travel guide (Treksplorer) updated October 31, 2026, not from an official schedule, so treat them as a guide.

  • Where: The same guide places the stalls on two pedestrian streets: Myeongdong 8-gil, running north from the station, and Myeongdong-gil, which ends at Lotte Department Store.
  • How to get there: Take Subway Line 4 to Myeongdong Station; Exit 6 puts you at the southern end of Myeongdong 8-gil.
  • When to go: Fridays and weekends bring the most vendors and the biggest crowds; Tuesday or Wednesday evenings are calmer.

Practical example: a visitor finishing dinner at 8 p.m. can still sample several stalls before closing, but arriving after 10:30 p.m. may mean many carts have already left.

What should I eat and how much does it cost?

Plan on about 2,000 to 6,000 won per snack, with grilled lobster at the top end near 15,000 to 18,000 won. Prices below are approximate, taken from one private guide, and they change, so check each stall.

The same guide suggests sweet-and-spicy fried chicken (dakgangjeong), candied fruit (tanghulu) and japchae-filled hotteok, and says a full food tour costs about 20,000 to 30,000 won. For cheaper, more traditional dishes it points to Namdaemun and Gwangjang markets; see our Gwangjang Market food guide and the wider Korean street food guide.

Can I pay by card?

Often yes, but bring cash as a backup. The Korea Times reported in February 2024 that card payments for Myeong-dong street food would begin April 1, 2024, with vendors to display price tags. Korea.net reported that the Jung District Office planned for registered street vendors to install card readers by March 2024 and to check the prices of ten popular foods monthly.

Practices can differ by stall: the private guide says most vendors still prefer cash and some give a small discount for it, and suggests carrying 30,000 to 40,000 won. See our cash or card guide for general tips.

How do I plan a one-hour food walk?

Arrive around 5 p.m., walk one street end to end, then buy from the shortest line with a visible price.

  1. Get there. Ride Line 4 to Myeongdong Station and leave by Exit 6.
  2. Scout first. Walk the length of Myeongdong 8-gil without buying so you can compare prices and see which stalls are open.
  3. Buy small. Share two or three items rather than ordering a full portion at the first cart.
  4. Ask about ingredients. Stalls do not label allergens, so ask the vendor or use a translation app.
  5. Finish with a hot drink or dessert. Then clean up, since bins are scarce.

FAQ

When does the Myeongdong night market open?

Food stalls start to appear around 3 to 4 p.m. and are busiest after about 5 p.m., according to a private travel guide checked on November 1, 2026. Most close around 9 to 10 p.m., so arrive early.

Do Myeongdong street food stalls accept cards?

Many do. The Jung District Office planned card readers for registered street vendors from spring 2024, but reports say some stalls still prefer cash, so carry both.

How much does a Myeongdong street food dinner cost?

Single snacks are roughly 2,000 to 6,000 won and a grilled lobster tail with cheese about 15,000 to 18,000 won. A multi-stall meal is roughly 20,000 to 30,000 won per person, based on a private guide.

Is Myeongdong street food worth it compared with Gwangjang Market?

Go for the atmosphere and novelty snacks. For cheaper, more traditional dishes such as tteokbokki and mandu, markets like Gwangjang or Namdaemun are usually better value.
