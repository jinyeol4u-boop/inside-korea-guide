Jeju Olle Trail for Beginners: The Easiest Routes

By Dragon Kim · Updated November 5, 2026 · 7 min read

What should I know before I arrive?

Details checked November 5, 2026

The Olle Trail is a network of marked walking routes around Jeju, so you can walk a single route in a day without committing to the whole island. The Jeju Olle Foundation was established in 2007 by Suh Myung-sook, and the trail now has 21 numbered main routes plus six sub-routes totaling 437 km, according to Wikipedia.

  • Worth knowing: Each route has its own difficulty rating. Wikipedia lists Routes 3-B, 6, 10-1, 14-1, 15-B and 21 as easy, and Visit Jeju rates Routes 1, 6 and 21 as low.
  • A common mistake: Treating the figures as exact. Visit Jeju gives Route 6 as 13.3 km in its summary but 11 km in its description, so read distances as approximate.
  • Local tip: For route questions, the course pages list the Olle contact line 064-762-2190 and the Jeju tourist line 064-740-6000.

Which Olle routes are easiest for beginners?

Routes 6, 21 and 1 are the best starting points, and Route 10-1 on Gapado island is the shortest and flattest option. All four are rated low or easy by Visit Jeju or the Wikipedia route list.

  • Route 6 (Soesokkak to the Jeju Olle Tourist Center): Visit Jeju: Course 6 describes it as a relatively flat path of about 4 to 5 hours that passes Jejigi Oreum, Socheonji and Sojeongbang Falls, then reaches downtown Seogwipo and Lee Jung-seop Street. A separate Visit Jeju page says the first 3.3 km is wheelchair-accessible.
  • Route 21 (Hado to Jongdal): Visit Jeju: Course 21 lists 11.1 km and 3 to 4 hours, flat apart from Jimibong Peak, which you can skip by walking around it.
  • Route 1 (Siheung to Gwangchigi Beach): Visit Jeju: Course 1 lists 11.5 km, 4 to 5 hours and low difficulty. It passes Malmi Oreum and Al Oreum with views of Seongsan Ilchulbong and Udo. Wikipedia lists Route 1 as medium, so the rating differs by source.
  • Route 10-1 (Gapado): Visit Jeju: accessible trails describes it as nearly flat, with a ferry of about 10 minutes from Woonjin Port. That page dates from December 2020, so check the ferry schedule.

Practical example: a traveller staying near Seongsan Ilchulbong can walk Route 1 in the morning and still have the afternoon free, then use our guide to the Seongsan Ilchulbong sunrise hike to plan the climb.

How do the beginner routes compare with the harder ones?

The beginner routes run about 11 to 15 km, while medium routes such as 2, 8 and 10 add 3 to 8 km and more climbing. Compare the figures below before you choose.

How do I follow the trail and get back?

Follow the painted arrows, and decide your transport back before you leave the start. A fan-run guide reports that blue arrows mean clockwise and orange arrows mean counter-clockwise, with both directions signposted, and that stops lie near villages and bus stops.

  1. Pick a route and direction. Choose one route from the table and confirm the start point on its Visit Jeju page.
  2. Get to the start. The course pages give addresses but no transit details, so use a map app and see our guide to getting around Jeju without a car.
  3. Follow the arrows. Keep to the blue arrows for clockwise or the orange arrows for counter-clockwise.
  4. Plan the return. Check the last bus or taxi options at the end point before you set off.

The same guide says you need stamps from every route in a paper passport (20,000 won) or the app only if you want a completion certificate. Walking without one is fine, but confirm current prices with the Foundation.

When should I walk, and what should I carry?

Mid-September to mid-November is the most comfortable period, and late June to August is hot. A fan-run guide says October is especially good and recommends late March to mid-May as a second window.

  • Water and snacks: The guide says routes pass villages and convenience stores, but carry water for coastal sections.
  • Footwear: Wear walking shoes, since some paths use rocky coast and field edges.
  • Sun and wind: Bring a hat and a windproof layer, since coastal stretches are exposed.
  • Ticks and dogs: The guide notes ticks in tall grass and loose dogs near livestock fields, so stay on the marked path.

FAQ

Which Jeju Olle route is best for a first-timer?

Route 6 (Seogwipo) or Route 21 (Hado to Jongdal). Visit Jeju rates both as low difficulty, at about 11 to 13 km and 3 to 5 hours. Route 1 near Seongsan Ilchulbong is also rated low and is the original route.

Do I need a passport or stamps to walk the Olle Trail?

No. Stamps only matter if you want a completion certificate. A fan-run guide says the paper passport costs 20,000 won, so you can walk any route without one. Confirm current details with the Jeju Olle Foundation.

How do I follow the Olle Trail markings?

Look for the painted arrows. A fan-run guide reports that blue arrows point clockwise and orange arrows point counter-clockwise around the island, with both directions signposted. Ribbons and signs also mark the way.

When is the best time to walk the Olle Trail?

Mid-September to mid-November, and late March to mid-May, are described as the most comfortable periods by a fan-run guide, while late June to August is hot. Check the weather and daylight before you start.

Can I do an Olle route without a car?

Yes, but plan the return. Routes start and end in villages or ports, so check local bus or taxi options. Visit Jeju's tourist line at 064-740-6000 can help with route questions.
