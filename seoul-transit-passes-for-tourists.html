Seoul Unlimited Transit Passes for Tourists Compared

By Dragon Kim · Updated October 21, 2026 · 7 min read

Quick answer

Seoul's Climate Card tourist pass costs 5,000 won for one day and 20,000 won for seven days, plus a 3,000 won card for first-time buyers. It pays off at about four subway or bus rides a day. On lighter days, a T-money card at roughly 1,550 won a subway ride is cheaper and covers more.

What should I know before I buy a pass?

Prices and rules checked October 21, 2026

Seoul has one unlimited pass aimed at short-stay visitors, the Climate Card short-term pass, and it only beats a plain T-money card if you ride a lot. According to the VisitKorea transportation cards page, it comes in 1, 2, 3, 5 and 7 day versions and runs on the Seoul subway and city buses.

  • Worth knowing: The 30-day Climate Card is a different product. The Seoul city traffic site says prepaid 30-day charging ended on August 31, and the VisitKorea page says the 30-day card was discontinued, so short-stay visitors should look at the 1 to 7 day passes.
  • A common mistake: Loading a pass the evening you arrive. VisitKorea says the pass activates as soon as it is loaded and cannot be changed or reloaded until it expires, so load it on the morning you start riding.
  • Local tip: A T-money card also works on subways and buses and is reusable, so many visitors simply top it up. Our Seoul subway guide covers fares and how to ride.

How do Seoul's transit passes and cards compare?

The Climate Card pass is cheapest per day for heavy riders, T-money is cheapest for light riders, and Mpass and WOWPASS suit visitors who want a bundled or cash-free option. The 2-day and 5-day Climate Card passes cost 8,000 and 15,000 won on the same page. Mpass is a foreigner-only T-money pass that VisitKorea says covers up to 20 rides a day on the Seoul subway, city buses and AREX, and it is bought with Korean cash at Tmoney Town near Seoul Station.

Is an unlimited pass worth it?

A Climate Card pass wins when you take about four or more rides a day, because a subway ride costs roughly 1,550 won with a transport card. That fare is covered in our Seoul subway guide and can rise slightly on longer trips.

  • 1-day pass: 5,000 won divided by 1,550 won is about 3.2, so four rides (6,200 won) beat T-money and three rides (4,650 won) do not.
  • 7-day pass: 20,000 won breaks even at roughly 13 base-fare rides across the week, or about two a day.
  • The card fee: The 3,000 won card is a one-time cost, though a T-money card also costs 3,000 to 5,000 won, so the fee matters only if you would otherwise skip a card.

Practical example: a visitor who spends a day on Gyeongbokgung, Insadong, Myeongdong and Hongdae often makes five or six hops, which costs about 7,750 to 9,300 won by card but 5,000 won with a one-day pass. A visitor with a slow museum day and two rides pays about 3,100 won by T-money instead.

What does the Climate Card cover?

It covers Seoul subway lines and Seoul city buses, but not every rail line or bus type, so check your route before you rely on it. VisitKorea describes it as an unlimited pass for the Seoul subway and buses, and the Korea Times reported the same at launch in 2024.

  • Usually excluded (third-party reports): Travel guides that reviewed the official rules list the Shinbundang Line, GTX, airport limousine buses, intercity and express buses, taxis, KTX and bike rentals as outside the pass. These lists are not from an official page I could open, so verify on the Seoul Metropolitan Government Climate Card page.
  • Airport trips: Reports differ on AREX, and the Incheon Airport section is generally described as excluded. Plan the airport leg using our Incheon Airport to Seoul guide.
  • Buses: Our Seoul city bus guide explains bus colours, which help you spot city versus wider-area routes.

How do I buy and load a Climate Card?

Buy the 3,000 won card, then load your chosen pass at a subway ticket machine using cash or a Korean-issued card. That is the process on the VisitKorea page, which also notes that overseas-card options may differ by machine.

  1. Pick your pass. Count your riding days and choose 1, 2, 3, 5 or 7 days.
  2. Get the card. VisitKorea lists the Seoul Metro information centers and nearby convenience stores for the 3,000 won card.
  3. Load on day one. Use a subway ticket machine, choose English, select the Climate Card and your pass, and pay with cash or a Korean card.
  4. Tap in and out. Touch the card at the gates and on buses as with any transport card.
  5. Plan the refund. VisitKorea says travelers without a Korean bank account can request a refund at the T-money headquarters, so spend the balance before you leave if you can.

Some third-party guides report that international Visa and Mastercard cards have worked at supported machines since March 2026 with a fee. That is not on the official pages I checked, so keep some Korean cash as a backup. Our cash or card guide has more on payment options.

FAQ

Is the Seoul Climate Card worth it for tourists?

It pays off if you ride about four or more subway or bus trips a day on a one-day pass, at roughly 1,550 won a subway ride. For lighter days a T-money card is usually cheaper. Confirm current prices on the official pages.

Where can tourists buy the Seoul Climate Card?

The physical card costs 3,000 won at Seoul Metro information centers or nearby convenience stores, and passes are loaded at subway ticket machines, according to VisitKorea. Confirm sales points before you go.

Does the Climate Card work on the airport train?

Reports differ, and the Incheon Airport section of AREX is generally listed as excluded, so do not rely on the pass for the airport trip. Check the official pages and see the Incheon Airport guide.

Can I get a refund on a Climate Card pass?

VisitKorea says travelers without a Korean bank account can request a refund at the T-money headquarters and recommends spending the balance before you leave. Rules and fees can change, so confirm first.

Is the 30-day Climate Card still available?

The Seoul city traffic site says prepaid 30-day charging ended on August 31, and the VisitKorea page says the 30-day card was discontinued. Tourist short-term passes are listed separately. Confirm current status.
