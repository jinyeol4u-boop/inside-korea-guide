Hanok Stays in Jeonju and Gyeongju: Where to Sleep

By Dragon Kim · Updated November 3, 2026 · 7 min read

Quick answer

Jeonju Hanok Village has about 700 hanok and free entry, so you can sleep inside the village itself. Gyeongju's hanok guesthouses cluster around Hwangnidan-gil, near tomb parks and Cheomseongdae. Choose Jeonju for the village atmosphere and Gyeongju for Silla sights, and confirm bedding, bathroom and breakfast before booking.

What should I know before I arrive?

Details checked November 3, 2026

Hanok stays in Jeonju and Gyeongju are small, individually run properties, so rooms, bedding and breakfast differ from one house to the next. Official tourism pages give addresses and phone numbers but, in the pages checked, no room rates.

  • Worth knowing: The Jeonju city tourism page lists check-in from 4pm and check-out between 10am and 11am for its three featured Jeonju stays. A Gyeongju guesthouse listing shows 15:00 and 11:00, so plan your arrival time around the host.
  • A common mistake: Assuming you will have a bed. The Jeonju page says only some rooms at one stay have beds, so ask. Our hanok stay basics guide explains ondol floors and shoe rules.
  • Local tip: Jeonju's Hanok Village Tourist Information Center is listed as open 24 hours a day on the Jeonju Hanok Village site, with phone +82-63-282-1330.

Should I stay in Jeonju or Gyeongju?

Pick Jeonju to sleep inside a hanok village, and Gyeongju to sleep close to Silla-era tombs and Cheomseongdae. The table compares what the listings say; none of the pages gave nightly prices.

Where can I stay in Jeonju Hanok Village?

The Jeonju city tourism page names three hanok stays: Dongnagwon, Sam Do Hun and Agbae, all in Wansan-gu. That page is undated, so treat programs and prices on it as a guide and confirm with the property.

  • Dongnagwon: 33-6 Eunhaeng-ro, phone (063) 285-3490, website jkhanok.co.kr, as listed on the Jeonju city tourism page. It lists hanbok rental at 15,000 won for two people, a tea ceremony at 11,000 won per person and a hanji craft workshop at 7,700 won per person.
  • Sam Do Hun: 12-8 Choimyunghee-gil, phone (063) 282-3337. It is described as quiet and behind Hotel Lewin, away from busy Taejo-ro, and offers a breakfast voucher for a restaurant known for kongnamul gukbap, with an early walk to Omokdae Pavilion.
  • Agbae: 59 Eojin-gil, phone (063) 285-2583. It is a guesthouse above an underground cafe-gallery, with rooms for two to more than ten people and beds in some rooms.

The Korea Tourism listing for the village names Dong Nak Won, about 178 m away, and Lahan Hotel Jeonju, about 139 m away, among nearby options and says you can book a hanok stay or visit the Hanok Life Experience Hall (listing). The village address is 99 Girin-daero, Wansan-gu.

Where can I stay in a Gyeongju hanok?

Look near Hwangnidan-gil, where Hanokinn sits in the Hanok Conservation Area, with an old alley leading toward Cheomseongdae. The Hanokinn listing gives 19 Poseok-ro 1050beon-gil, seven rooms and a capacity of 20 people.

  • Rooms: A traditional ondol room with private bathroom and shower, a two-storey Sarangchae with a double and a family room for up to four, and a single-storey Byeolchae with two double rooms.
  • Facilities: Korean-style breakfast at the cafe, parking and programs such as tea ceremony and yunnori. Airport or station pickup is listed as not available.
  • Booking: Phone +82-54-749-8090, hanokinn.com or booking.naver.com as listed. The page carries a Korea Tourism Quality Certification mark, but its update date is not stated, so check current details.

Gyochon Traditional Village, at 39-2 Gyochon-gil, is free to enter, has parking and toilets, and contains the Choi family house, designated folklore cultural heritage No. 27, plus a workshop for Gyeongju Gyodong Beopju liquor (listing). The listing does not mention lodging, so visit it on foot rather than expecting a stay there.

What can I see on foot from each stay?

From Jeonju Hanok Village you can reach Gyeonggijeon, the cathedral and the Confucian school on foot, and from Hwangnidan-gil you are close to Daereungwon and Cheomseongdae.

  • Gyeonggijeon (Jeonju): 44 Taejo-ro. Adult admission is 3,000 won on the Korea Tourism listing. The Jeonju Hanok Village site lists 10am to 6pm and free entry on Culture Day, the last Wednesday of each month, while the other listing gives winter hours of 9:00 to 18:00 with last entry an hour before closing. Plan to arrive early.
  • Jeondong Catholic Cathedral: Listed 9am to 5pm, excluding mass times, with free admission.
  • Daereungwon (Gyeongju): Cheonmachong Tomb is paid at 3,000 won for adults, and the complex is listed open 09:00 to 22:00 (listing). A VisitKorea itinerary gives main gate last admission as 21:30.
  • Cheomseongdae: Free, with winter hours of 09:00 to 21:00 per the same VisitKorea itinerary, about 626 m from Gyochon on the Gyochon listing.
  • Hwangnidan-gil: Shop hours and holidays vary by store, and Cheonmachong is within 139 m (listing).

If you are also planning a bike day around the Silla sites, see Gyeongju in One Day.

How do I book a hanok stay in either city?

Choose the city, then contact the property directly or compare its own page, and confirm bedding, bathroom and breakfast in writing.

  1. Choose the stay type. Decide whether you want a village setting (Jeonju) or tombs and cafes on foot (Gyeongju).
  2. Check the room. Ask whether the room has a bed or floor bedding, who it sleeps, and whether the bathroom is private.
  3. Confirm times. Ask about check-in, since listings show 4pm in Jeonju and 15:00 at one Gyeongju stay, and late arrival.
  4. Ask about food. Some stays include breakfast or a voucher, and others do not.
  5. Save the helpline. The 1330 Korea Travel Helpline, +82-2-1330, offers information in several languages including English.

FAQ

Which is better for a hanok stay, Jeonju or Gyeongju?

Jeonju suits travellers who want to sleep inside a large hanok village, listed as having about 700 hanok. Gyeongju suits those who want a hanok guesthouse close to tomb parks and Cheomseongdae. Both are worth a night if you have time.

Can I book a hanok stay in Jeonju Hanok Village online?

The Jeonju city tourism page says to book by phone or through each property's own website and lists no central booking system. Many travellers also use general booking sites, so compare the property's own page and confirm the details.

What are typical check-in and check-out times?

Jeonju's city page lists check-in from 4pm and check-out between 10am and 11am for its three featured stays. A Gyeongju guesthouse listing gives 15:00 check-in and 11:00 check-out. Always confirm with your own host.

Is Gyeongju's Gyochon Traditional Village a place to sleep?

Not necessarily. The listing for Gyochon describes a village you can visit free of charge, with the Choi family house and a traditional liquor workshop, but it does not mention lodging. Hanok guesthouses are listed separately, such as Hanokinn near Hwangnidan-gil.

Do hanok stays include breakfast?

Some do. The Jeonju city page says one stay gives a voucher for a nearby restaurant instead of breakfast, and a Gyeongju guesthouse listing mentions Korean-style breakfast at its cafe. Ask before booking.
