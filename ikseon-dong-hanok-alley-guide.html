Ikseon-dong Guide: Hanok Alleys and Old Jongno Walk

By Dragon Kim · Updated October 25, 2026 · 6 min read

Quick answer

Ikseon-dong is free to enter and reached from Jongno 3-ga Station, Exit 4. VisitKorea says its hanok alleys date to the 1920s and are still home to residents. Plan one to two hours, then extend into Jongno with Unhyeongung (free), Jongmyo (1,000 won) and Pojangmacha Street at night.

What should I know before I arrive?

Sources checked October 25, 2026

Ikseon-dong is a small, free hanok neighbourhood where the lanes are narrow and the shops set their own hours. It sits in Jongno-gu between Changdeokgung Palace and Jongmyo Shrine, according to Seoul Metropolitan Government's media hub.

  • Worth knowing: VisitKorea says the alleys have no maps or signs, so use distinctive shops or murals as landmarks. Download an offline map before you go.
  • A common mistake: Expecting a museum. Residents still live here and the hanok are now mostly cafes, restaurants and small shops.
  • Local tip: Check hours shop by shop. VisitKorea lists the street as open all year but says hours vary by business.

What is Ikseon-dong and why do people visit?

It is a hanok lane district in Jongno that visitors come to for atmosphere, cafes and a look at traditional housing close to downtown towers. The city media hub says the village was developed in 1920 by a real estate developer, and VisitKorea says the hanok alley dates to the 1920s. A 2017 Munhwa Ilbo article identifies that developer's company, Gunyangsa, as founded in 1920 by Jeong Se-gwon, and says his hanok reached as far as Ikseon-dong.

The modern revival came later. The media hub reports that the area was designated for redevelopment in 2004, the plan stalled and the union committee was dissolved in 2014. Owners then let tenants renovate the old hanok for businesses, and cafes and small restaurants followed. Walls, beams and tiled roofs were kept.

How do I get to Ikseon-dong?

Ride the subway to Jongno 3-ga Station and leave by Exit 4. Visit Seoul lists Exit 4 as the nearest exit and about 123 m away, while VisitKorea (a page last updated in 2018) says to walk about 300 m from Exit 4. Treat the distance as short but confirm on a map.

  1. Take Line 1, 3 or 5 to Jongno 3(sam)-ga Station.
  2. Use Exit 4. Ivisitkorea recommends Line 5 Exit 4 and says to turn left, cross the street and enter the alley beside the CU convenience store.
  3. Alternative: Ivisitkorea also lists Line 1 or 3 Exit 6 as the nearest exit, and Anguk Station (Line 3) Exit 6 as a roughly 10-minute walk.

What is a good walk through Ikseon-dong and old Jongno?

Walk the Ikseon-dong lanes first, then add one royal site and finish with street food. Several of the best stops are within a short walk of each other, so you can pick two or three.

  1. Ikseon-dong alleys (1 to 2 hours). Wander without a plan and stop for one cafe or meal. Ivisitkorea lists examples, but check each shop's current hours.
  2. Nakwon Arcade (short stop). Visit Seoul says the village connects to Nakwon Sanga, the music-instrument market. The media hub reports the building was constructed in 1968.
  3. Unhyeongung or Jongmyo. VisitKorea says Unhyeongung was the birthplace of Gojong and the home of his father Heungseon Daewongun. Jongmyo is the Joseon royal shrine and is closed on Tuesdays, so plan around that.
  4. Dinner at Pojangmacha Street. VisitKorea says the stalls sell jeon, udon, ramyeon, eomuk and tteokbokki. Reach it from Jongno 3-ga Exit 6.

How should I behave in a residential hanok alley?

Keep noise down, leave doorways clear and ask before photographing people, because residents still live in Ikseon-dong. VisitKorea states that residents remain in the area. These are common-sense manners rather than posted rules, so also follow any signs you find.

  • Stay in the lanes. Do not step into private courtyards or onto steps unless a shop invites you in.
  • Go in a small group. The lanes are narrow, and a few people pass more easily than a large group.
  • Take rubbish with you or use the bins inside the shops you visit.

When should I go and what will it cost?

Go on a rainy weekday or at night for fewer crowds and better atmosphere; entry is free, so budget only for food, drinks and any ticketed sites. Ivisitkorea says rainy days mean fewer crowds and easier cafe seating, and it advises visiting popular restaurants early because waits grow after about 6 pm. Visit Seoul reviewers recommend walking at night when the hanok shops are lit.

FAQ

How do I get to Ikseon-dong by subway?

Take Seoul Subway Line 1, 3 or 5 to Jongno 3(sam)-ga Station. Visit Seoul names Exit 4 as the nearest exit, and Ivisitkorea recommends Line 5 Exit 4 and also lists Line 1 or 3 Exit 6. The alleys are a few minutes on foot, so check a map app when you surface.

Is Ikseon-dong free to visit?

Yes. VisitKorea lists the hanok street as free with shop-by-shop opening hours. You pay only for what you buy in cafes, restaurants and shops.

How long do I need in Ikseon-dong?

Plan about one to two hours for the alleys and one cafe stop. Add Unhyeongung, Jongmyo or Pojangmacha Street if you want a half-day Jongno walk.

Do people still live in Ikseon-dong?

Yes. VisitKorea notes that residents still live in the area, so keep your voice low, avoid blocking doorways and ask before photographing people.

What is the best time to visit Ikseon-dong?

Visit Seoul reviewers recommend walking at night when the hanok shops are lit, while Ivisitkorea says rainy days mean fewer crowds and easier seating. Opening hours vary by shop, so check each venue before you go.
